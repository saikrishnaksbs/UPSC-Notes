### 1. Delhi Sultanate: Central Administration & Offices

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Naib / Naib-i-Mamlakat**| Imperial Regent exercising de facto sovereign authority on behalf of a weak or minor Sultan. | Sultanate Central Admin |
| **Wazir** | Prime Minister heading the imperial revenue and financial administration (*Diwan-i-Wizarat*). | Sultanate Central Admin |
| **Ariz-i-Mamalik** | Imperial Minister of War and head of military recruitment, reviews, and inspections (*Diwan-i-Arz*). | Sultanate Military |
| **Dabir-i-Khas** | Chief Imperial Secretary heading the department of royal correspondence (*Diwan-i-Insha*). | Sultanate Central Admin |
| **Sadr-us-Sudur** | Minister of ecclesiastical affairs, religious endowments, and state charitable stipends. | Sultanate Administration |
| **Qazi-ul-Quzat** | Chief Justice of the Sultanate heading civil and criminal sharia judiciary. | Sultanate Judiciary |
| **Barid-i-Mamalik** | Head of the state intelligence network, secret news-writers, and imperial postal couriers. | Sultanate Administration |
| **Amir-i-Hajib** | Lord Chamberlain supervising court etiquette, ceremonial protocols, and royal access. | Sultanate Court |
| **Sar-i-Jandar** | Commander of the Sultan's elite corps of personal royal bodyguards (*Jandars*). | Sultanate Military |
| **Muhtasib** | Public municipal officer censoring morals, checking market weights, and enforcing sharia conduct. | Sultanate & Mughal |
| **Kotwal** | Head of city police responsible for urban law, order, nighttime watch, and criminal investigations. | Sultanate & Mughal |
| **Amir-i-Akhur** | Master of the Royal Horse; commander supervising imperial cavalry stables (post held by Malik Yaqut). | Sultanate Administration |

### 2. Sultanate Specialized Departments (Diwans)

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Founded By / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Diwan-i-Wizarat** | Imperial Department of Finance, state budgets, and revenue accounting. | Core Sultanate Ministry |
| **Diwan-i-Arz** | Ministry of Military Affairs created by Balban to defend against Mongol invasions. | Balban |
| **Diwan-i-Insha** | Imperial Secretariat drafting state dispatches, royal farmans, and treaties. | Core Sultanate Ministry |
| **Diwan-i-Rasalat** | Department of Foreign Affairs and religious appeals/endowments. | Core Sultanate Ministry |
| **Diwan-i-Mustakhraj**| Special department established by Alauddin Khalji to recover revenue arrears from corrupt collectors. | Alauddin Khalji |
| **Diwan-i-Riyasat** | Ministry of Commerce instituted by Alauddin Khalji to enforce market price control regulations. | Alauddin Khalji |
| **Shahna-i-Mandi** | Market superintendent appointed by Alauddin Khalji to inspect food grain markets (*Mandi*). | Alauddin Khalji |
| **Diwan-i-Kohi** | Department of Agriculture established by Muhammad bin Tughlaq to bring fallow lands under cultivation. | Muhammad bin Tughlaq |
| **Diwan-i-Khairat** | Department of Public Charity created by Firoz Shah Tughlaq to aid destitute widows and orphans. | Firoz Shah Tughlaq |
| **Diwan-i-Bandagan** | Department of Royal Slaves established by Firoz Shah Tughlaq supervising over 180,000 state slaves. | Firoz Shah Tughlaq |
| **Diwan-i-Istihqaq** | Department of State Pensions established by Firoz Shah Tughlaq for impoverished scholars and nobles. | Firoz Shah Tughlaq |
| **Dar-ul-Shifa** | Free public charitable hospital staffed by Unani physicians founded by Firoz Shah Tughlaq in Delhi. | Firoz Shah Tughlaq |

### 3. Agrarian, Land Revenue & Fiscal Terminology

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Iqta** | Revenue-yielding territorial assignment given to officers (*Muqtis/Iqtadars*) to maintain troops. | Sultanate Land System |
| **Fawazil** | Excess surplus revenue remitted by an Iqtadar to the imperial central treasury after meeting military expenses. | Sultanate Fiscal Policy |
| **Khalisa** | Crown lands directly administered by the imperial exchequer whose revenues supported royal household. | Sultanate & Mughal |
| **Paibaqi** | Lands held by the central treasury temporarily earmarked for subsequent jagir assignments. | Mughal Land System |
| **Madad-i-Maash** | Hereditary tax-free land grants given for religious, educational, or charitable purposes (*Sayurghal*). | Mughal Land Grants |
| **Inam** | Revenue-free land gifts bestowed upon pious men, saints, or religious shrines. | Land Grants |
| **Polaj** | Premium agricultural land cultivated continuously every year, never allowed to lie fallow. | Mughal Land System |
| **Parauti** | Land left uncultivated temporarily for 1 to 2 years to allow the soil to regain natural fertility. | Mughal Land System |
| **Chachar** | Land left fallow for 3 to 4 years before being brought back under the plow. | Mughal Land System |
| **Banjar** | Low-grade uncultivated wasteland left fallow for 5 or more years. | Mughal Land System |
| **Zabt / Zabti** | System of land revenue assessment based on actual physical measurement of cultivated fields. | Mughal Revenue |
| **Dahsala (Ain-i-Dahsala)**| Raja Todar Mal's 10-year settlement (1580 CE) assessing average yields and prices of past decade in cash. | Akbar's Revenue Reform |
| **Batai / Ghalla-Bakhshi**| System of crop sharing between the state and peasant (divided into *Khet-batai*, *Lang-batai*, *Rasi-batai*). | Agrarian Practice |
| **Kankut** | System of revenue estimation where standing crop yield is assessed visually or through sample cutting. | Agrarian Practice |
| **Nasaq** | Rough estimation or bureaucratic compounding of past revenue records without fresh annual measurement. | Mughal Revenue |
| **Jama** | Assessed or estimated gross revenue potential of an agricultural estate or province. | Mughal Fiscal Terms |
| **Hasil** | Actual net revenue collected and realized from an estate or province. | Mughal Fiscal Terms |
| **Taccavi / Sondhar**| State agricultural advancement loans given to distressed peasants to buy seeds, bullocks, or dig wells. | Agrarian Welfare |
| **Khot / Muqaddam** | Dominant rural village headmen and landed intermediaries in medieval Gangetic plains. | Rural Administration |
| **Patwari** | Village accountant maintaining detailed field registers, boundary maps, and tax records. | Rural Administration |
| **Qanungo** | Hereditary district/pargana revenue officer preserving customary tax rates and land tenures. | Rural Administration |

### 4. Mughal Mansabdari System & Military Terminology

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Mansab** | Official rank, grade, or status held by an imperial officer within the centralized Mughal bureaucracy. | Mughal Mansabdari |
| **Mansabdar** | Holder of a Mansab rank obligated to maintain a designated contingent of cavalry troops. | Mughal Mansabdari |
| **Zat** | Personal rank indicator fixing the officer's personal status, court precedence, and salary scale. | Mughal Mansabdari |
| **Sawar** | Military rank indicator denoting the exact number of cavalrymen and horses the officer must maintain. | Mughal Mansabdari |
| **Do-Aspa Sih-Aspa** | Innovation by Jahangir requiring a mansabdar to maintain double the sawar quota without increasing zat rank. | Jahangir's Military Reform |
| **Dagh** | Royal branding of military cavalry war-horses to prevent fraudulent horse substitution at inspections. | Military Administration |
| **Chehra / Tashiha** | Detailed descriptive muster-roll of each individual cavalry soldier kept on imperial military registers. | Military Administration |
| **Jagir** | Territorial revenue assignment granted to a mansabdar in lieu of cash salary (*Tankha Jagir*). | Mughal Land System |
| **Watan Jagir** | Hereditary, non-transferable ancestral homeland jagir granted to Rajput and regional princes by Akbar. | Mughal Diplomacy |
| **Mashrut** | Conditional increase in sawar rank granted temporarily for serving in difficult frontier campaigns. | Mughal Military |
| **Ahadis** | Elite gentlemen troopers directly recruited by the Mughal Emperor, serving as royal bodyguards. | Mughal Army |
| **Dakhilis** | Supplementary soldiers paid directly by the state treasury but assigned to serve under various mansabdars. | Mughal Army |

### 5. Mughal Provincial, Sarkar & Pargana Administration

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Subah** | Imperial province; Akbar divided his empire into 12 Subahs (expanded to 15, and later 22 under Aurangzeb). | Mughal Provincial Admin |
| **Subahdar / Sipahsalar**| Provincial Governor appointed by the Emperor exercising military command and general executive powers. | Mughal Provincial Admin |
| **Provincial Diwan** | Provincial head of finance appointed directly by the central Wazir, serving as check on the Subahdar. | Mughal Provincial Admin |
| **Bakshi** | Paymaster-general of provincial military forces responsible for conducting regular muster-rolls. | Mughal Provincial Admin |
| **Sarkar** | Intermediate administrative division between the Subah and Pargana (equivalent to modern district). | Mughal Local Admin |
| **Faujdar** | Executive and military commander of a Sarkar responsible for suppressing rebellions and policing highways. | Sarkar Administration |
| **Amalguzar / Amil** | Chief revenue collector of a Sarkar responsible for assessing crop yields and collecting imperial taxes. | Sarkar Administration |
| **Pargana** | Sub-district administrative unit composed of a cluster of villages. | Pargana Administration |
| **Shiqdar** | Executive officer and magistrate of a Pargana responsible for local policing and law enforcement. | Pargana Administration |
| **Amin / Munsif** | Civil judge and land survey officer in a Pargana settling agrarian land disputes. | Pargana Administration |

### 6. Coinage, Commercial Terms & Financial Instruments

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Tanka** | Standard silver coin (approx. 175 grains) introduced by Iltutmish; backbone of Sultanate currency. | Sultanate Currency |
| **Jital** | Standard copper/billon currency of the Delhi Sultanate (48 jitals = 1 silver tanka). | Sultanate Currency |
| **Rupiya** | Standard high-purity silver coin (178 grains) introduced by Sher Shah Sur; precursor of modern Rupee. | Sur & Mughal Currency |
| **Dam** | Massive copper coin introduced by Sher Shah and standardized by Akbar (40 dams = 1 silver rupiya). | Mughal Currency |
| **Mohur** | Standard imperial gold coin minted by Mughal emperors, primarily used for hoarding and ceremonial gifts. | Mughal Currency |
| **Hundi** | Traditional credit instrument and negotiable bill of exchange used by merchants for long-distance trade. | Medieval Banking |
| **Sarraf** | Indigenous money changer, bullion assayer, and traditional private merchant banker. | Medieval Banking |
| **Dastak** | Trade permit exempting goods from paying internal provincial customs tolls and transit duties. | Trade & Commerce |
| **Karkhanas** | Royal manufacturing workshops producing luxury textiles, weapons, carpets, and court regalia. | State Industry |
| **Rahdari** | Transit duty or road toll collected along highways and river trade routes. | Medieval Commerce |

### 7. Vijayanagara Empire & Southern Institutions

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Nayankara System** | Administrative system where military captains (*Amara-nayakas*) held land (*Amaram*) in return for military quotas. | Vijayanagara Polity |
| **Amara-nayaka** | Military feudatory chieftain obligated to maintain troops, war-elephants, and remit annual tribute to Raya. | Vijayanagara Polity |
| **Ayagar System** | Self-sufficient village administrative body composed of 12 hereditary functionaries (*Ayagars*). | Vijayanagara Rural Admin |
| **Varaha / Pagoda** | Standard imperial gold coin of the Vijayanagara Empire bearing depictions of Hindu deities and Boar. | Vijayanagara Currency |
| **Fanam** | Small fractional gold/silver coin widely circulated across Vijayanagara and South Indian markets. | South Indian Currency |
| **Athavana** | Central revenue secretariat managing imperial land assessments and customs taxes in Vijayanagara. | Vijayanagara Revenue |
| **Mahanavami Dibba**| Massive ceremonial stone platform at Hampi used by Vijayanagara Rayas for royal Dussehra spectacles. | Vijayanagara Architecture |
| **Kattumangu** | Village artisans and craftsmen granted rent-free agricultural lands for providing public village services. | South Indian Society |

### 8. The Maratha Empire: Administrative & Fiscal Terms

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Swarajya** | Sovereign self-governed homeland territories of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj free from foreign rule. | Maratha Polity |
| **Mughlai** | Surrounding non-Maratha territories from which the Marathas collected tribute (*Chauth*). | Maratha Polity |
| **Ashtapradhan** | Council of eight sovereign ministers advising Chhatrapati Shivaji on state governance. | Maratha Central Admin |
| **Peshwa** | Prime Minister in Shivaji's council; later became hereditary executive rulers of Maratha Confederacy. | Maratha Central Admin |
| **Amatya / Majumdar** | Finance and accounts minister in the Ashtapradhan council. | Maratha Central Admin |
| **Sari Naubat / Senapati**| Commander-in-Chief of the Maratha armed forces in Shivaji's Ashtapradhan. | Maratha Military |
| **Waqia-Navis / Mantri**| Home minister responsible for royal court daily proceedings, intelligence, and internal security. | Maratha Central Admin |
| **Sachiv / Shurnis** | Royal secretary in charge of official correspondence and auditing pargana revenue accounts. | Maratha Central Admin |
| **Sumant / Dabir** | Foreign minister managing diplomatic relations and foreign embassies in the Maratha court. | Maratha Central Admin |
| **Nyayadhish** | Chief Justice of the Maratha kingdom administering civil and criminal justice. | Maratha Judiciary |
| **Panditrao** | Minister of religious affairs, charities, moral conduct, and patronage to Vedic scholars. | Maratha Administration |
| **Chauth** | Military levy of 25% (1/4th) of gross revenue extracted from neighboring states to prevent plundering raids. | Maratha Taxation |
| **Sardeshmukhi** | Additional 10% tax levied by Shivaji claiming sovereign rights as hereditary supreme head (*Sardeshmukh*). | Maratha Taxation |
| **Bargir** | State-equipped and state-paid regular cavalry soldier riding horses provided by the state treasury. | Maratha Military |
| **Shiledar** | Mercenary cavalry soldier who brought and maintained his own horse, armor, and weapons. | Maratha Military |
| **Saranjam / Mokasa** | Military land fief granted to Maratha sardars in lieu of salary for maintaining defensive garrisons. | Maratha Land System |
| **Ganimi Kawa** | Rapid guerrilla warfare technique perfected by Shivaji utilizing Western Ghats hill-fort terrain. | Maratha Military Tactics |

### 9. Bhakti & Sufi Socio-Religious Terms

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Silsila** | Continuous spiritual chain or genealogical lineage tracing authority back to Prophet Muhammad. | Sufism |
| **Khanqah** | Sufi spiritual hospice or monastery where master and disciples gathered for prayers and communal dining. | Sufism |
| **Pir / Murshid** | Spiritual master or guru in Sufism guiding the novice seeker (*Murid*) towards mystical union. | Sufism |
| **Murid** | Disciple or spiritual novice initiated into a Sufi silsila following the guidance of a Pir. | Sufism |
| **Sama** | Musical audition and ecstatic devotional singing practiced by the Chishti Sufis to induce divine trance. | Sufism |
| **Wahdat-ul-Wujud**| 'Unity of Being'; pantheistic philosophical doctrine propounded by Ibn Arabi popular among Indian Sufis. | Sufi Philosophy |
| **Fana** | Spiritual annihilation of the personal ego in the presence of the Divine Reality. | Sufi Mysticism |
| **Ziyarat** | Practice of making devotional pilgrimages to the sacred tombs (*Dargahs*) of revered Sufi saints. | Sufi Practices |
| **Urs** | Annual death-anniversary festival celebrating a Sufi saint's final mystical union with God. | Sufi Traditions |
| **Nirguna Bhakti** | Devotional path worshiping the Formless, Attribute-less, and Omnipresent Supreme Reality (Kabir, Nanak). | Bhakti Traditions |
| **Saguna Bhakti** | Devotional path worshiping God manifested in specific anthropomorphic divine avatars (Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai). | Bhakti Traditions |
| **Abhanga** | Devotional Marathi poetic stanzas sung in praise of Lord Vitthala at Pandharpur (Tukaram, Namdev). | Varkari Tradition |
| **Vachana** | Epigrammatic rhythmic Kannada prose compositions composed by Lingayat/Veerashaiva saints (Basavanna). | Veerashaivism |
