# Ancient Indian History: Master Chronology (Prehistory to 750 CE)

> **UPSC Revision Tool**: Chronological sequencing of all prehistoric, proto-historic, and ancient historical milestones.
> **Cross-Reference**: Aligned with , R.S. Sharma, and Upinder Singh.

---

## 🏛️ Comprehensive Ancient Chronological Master Table

| Era / Date | Period / Dynasty | Landmark Historical Event | Key Personalities / Sources | UPSC Key Takeaway & Significance |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **c. 2,000,000 – 100,000 BCE** | Lower Paleolithic | Pebble & core tools, handaxes, cleavers | Bori (earliest site), Bhimbetka, Attirampakkam | Hunting-gathering stage; Acheulean tool industry. |
| **c. 100,000 – 40,000 BCE** | Middle Paleolithic | Flake tools, scrapers, borers | Nevasa, Belan Valley, Bhimbetka | Levallois tool technique; utilization of chert, jasper. |
| **c. 40,000 – 10,000 BCE** | Upper Paleolithic | Blade & burin industry; rock art origins | Bhimbetka Rock Shelters, Kurnool Caves | Emergence of Homo sapiens; bone tool technology; ostrich eggshell beads (Patne). |
| **c. 10,000 – 6,000 BCE** | Mesolithic Period | Microlithic technology; animal domestication | Bagor (Rajasthan), Adamgarh (MP), Sarai Nahar Rai | Geometric microliths; transition to Holocene; earliest domestic dogs and cattle. |
| **c. 7,000 – 2,500 BCE** | Neolithic Revolution | Sedentary farming, polished celts, pottery | Mehrgarh (Baluchistan), Burzahom, Chirand, Daojali Hading | Earliest wheat/barley agriculture at Mehrgarh; pit dwellings and dog burials at Burzahom. |
| **c. 3,500 – 1,000 BCE** | Chalcolithic Cultures | Copper-stone tool usage; regional cultures | Ahar-Banas, Malwa (Navdatoli), Jorwe (Inamgaon) | Black-and-Red ware; earliest metallurgy; terracotta mother goddess figurines. |
| **c. 3,300 – 2,600 BCE** | Early Harappan Phase | Regional urbanization & fortified settlements | Kot Diji, Amri, Kalibangan I, Sothi | Precursor stage; mud-brick fortifications; fish-scale pottery designs. |
| **c. 2,600 – 1,900 BCE** | Mature Harappan (IVC) | Peak Bronze Age civilization; planned cities | Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, Lothal, Kalibangan | Grid layout, drainage system, Great Bath, dockyard (Lothal), water reservoirs (Dholavira). |
| **c. 1,900 – 1,300 BCE** | Late / Post-Harappan | De-urbanization, regional migrations | Cemetery H, Jhukar, Rangpur | Inundation/drying of Ghaggar-Hakra; ruralization of settlements. |
| **c. 1,500 – 1,000 BCE** | Early Vedic Period | Pastoral economy; Rig Veda composition | Aryans, Dasas; Sapta Sindhu region | Rig Veda; Sabha and Samiti popular assemblies; Battle of Ten Kings (*Dasarajna*). |
| **c. 1,000 – 600 BCE** | Later Vedic Period | Iron Age (*Syamayas*); Kuru-Panchala states | Sama, Yajur, Atharva Vedas; Painted Grey Ware (PGW) | Transition to sedentary agriculture; territorial Janapadas; Ashvamedha and Rajasuya sacrifices. |
| **c. 600 – 500 BCE** | Second Urbanization | 16 Mahajanapadas emerge; iron plowshare agriculture | Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW); Punch-Marked Coins | Magadha supremacy under Bimbisara and Ajatashatru; rise of heterodox sramana sects. |
| **c. 563 – 483 BCE** | Gautama Buddha | Life and ministry of Siddhartha Gautama | Shakya clan, Kapilavastu, Bodh Gaya, Sarnath | Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path; Nirvana; First Buddhist Council at Rajgriha (483 BCE). |
| **c. 540 – 468 BCE** | Vardhamana Mahavira | Life and teachings of 24th Tirthankara | Jnatrika clan, Kundagrama, Pavapuri | Triratna (Right Faith, Knowledge, Conduct); Syadvada; Jain monastic canonical councils. |
| **c. 544 – 492 BCE** | Haryanka Dynasty | Bimbisara consolidates Magadhan hegemony | Bimbisara, physician Jivaka, Ajatashatru | Matrimonial alliances (Kosala, Vaishali); annexation of Anga. |
| **c. 492 – 460 BCE** | Ajatashatru's Reign | Annexation of Kashi and Vajji confederacy | Mahashilakantaka & Rathamusala war machines | Convened First Buddhist Council; built Pataliputra watch-post. |
| **c. 460 – 444 BCE** | Udayin | Foundation of imperial Pataliputra | Ganga-Son river confluence | Capital permanently shifted from Rajgriha to Pataliputra. |
| **c. 413 – 345 BCE** | Shishunaga Dynasty | Overthrew Haryankas; crushed Avanti | King Shishunaga, Kalashoka | Second Buddhist Council at Vaishali (383 BCE); split into Sthaviravadins & Mahasanghikas. |
| **c. 345 – 322 BCE** | Nanda Dynasty | First pan-Indian empire; massive army | Mahapadma Nanda (*Ekrat*), Dhana Nanda | First Non-Kshatriya empire; enormous wealth and standing army deterring Alexander. |
| **326 BCE** | Alexander's Invasion | Battle of the Hydaspes (Jhelum River) | Alexander the Great vs. King Porus | Mutiny of Greek troops at Beas River; direct Greco-Indian cultural and trade contacts. |
| **c. 322 – 298 BCE** | Chandragupta Maurya | Foundation of the Mauryan Empire | Kautilya / Chanakya, Megasthenes | Overthrew Nandas; defeated Seleucus Nicator (305 BCE); Jain abdication at Shravanabelagola. |
| **c. 298 – 273 BCE** | Bindusara (*Amitraghata*) | Mauryan southern peninsula expansion | Deimachus (Syrian envoy), Dionysius | Patron of Ajivika sect; crushed revolt in Taxila with Prince Ashoka. |
| **c. 268 – 232 BCE** | Ashoka the Great | High point of Mauryan imperial and moral power | Major Rock Edicts, Pillar Edicts, Third Buddhist Council | Kalinga War (261 BCE); conversion to Dhamma; third council at Pataliputra; missionary missions. |
| **c. 185 BCE** | Mauryan Collapse | Assassination of last Mauryan king Brihadratha | Pushyamitra Shunga | Pushyamitra Shunga founded Shunga dynasty; revival of Brahmanical Vedic sacrifices. |
| **c. 2nd Cent. BCE – 3rd Cent. CE** | Post-Mauryan Era | Influx of Central Asian powers & trade boom | Indo-Greeks, Shakas, Parthians, Kushanas | Indo-Greek gold coinage; Gandhara and Mathura art schools; Milindapanha (Menander & Nagasena). |
| **c. 78 – 101 CE** | Kanishka I (Kushana) | Peak Kushana Empire; Silk Route control | Kanishka, Ashvaghosha, Charaka, Nagarjuna | Initiated Shaka Era (78 CE); Fourth Buddhist Council in Kashmir (split into Mahayana & Hinayana). |
| **c. 1st Cent. BCE – 3rd Cent. CE** | Satavahana Dynasty | Deccan maritime power & land grants | Simuka, Gautamiputra Satakarni, Vashishtiputra | Earliest epigraphic royal land grants (*Brahmadeya*); Nasik inscription; Amaravati art. |
| **c. 3rd Cent. BCE – 3rd Cent. CE** | Sangam Age (South India) | Cheras, Cholas, and Pandyas | Tolkappiyam, Silappadikaram, Manimekalai | Three Sangams at Madurai; extensive Roman maritime trade (Arikamedu, Muziris). |
| **c. 319 – 335 CE** | Chandragupta I (Gupta) | Founding of the Imperial Gupta Empire | Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi | Initiated Gupta Era (319–320 CE); assumed title *Maharajadhiraja*. |
| **c. 335 – 380 CE** | Samudragupta | Aggressive military conquest across India | Harishena (Prayag Prashasti / Allahabad Pillar) | *Napoleon of India*; performed Ashvamedha; minted gold coins playing Veena (*Kaviraja*). |
| **c. 380 – 415 CE** | Chandragupta II (*Vikramaditya*) | Golden Age; conquest of Western Shakas | Navaratnas (Kalidasa, Varahamihira), Faxian | Iron Pillar of Mehrauli; peak of classical Sanskrit literature; Chinese pilgrim Faxian visit. |
| **c. 415 – 455 CE** | Kumaragupta I | Foundation of Nalanda Mahavihara | Nalanda University, epigraphic copper-plates | World-renowned center of Buddhist learning; repelled early Huna invasions. |
| **c. 455 – 467 CE** | Skandagupta | Repelled White Huna invasions | Bhitari Pillar Inscription, Junagadh rock | Repaired Sudarshana Lake dam; preserved empire from nomadic collapse before eventual decline. |
| **c. 606 – 647 CE** | Harsha Vardhana | Pushyabhuti Empire of Kanauj | Banabhatta (*Harshacharita*), Xuanzang | Assembled Kanauj and Prayag assemblies; playwright (*Ratnavali*, *Priyadarsika*, *Nagananda*). |
| **c. 610 – 642 CE** | Pulakeshin II (Chalukya) | Badami Chalukyan supremacy in Deccan | Ravikirti (Aihole Inscription) | Defeated Harsha at Narmada River (618 CE); defeated by Pallava Narasimhavarman I. |
| **c. 630 – 668 CE** | Narasimhavarman I (Pallava) | Pallava naval and structural architectural peak | Mahabalipuram Rock-cut Rathas | Sacked Badami (assumed title *Vatapikonda*); sent successful naval expedition to Sri Lanka. |
