## 1. Overview of the Palaeolithic Period

- **Timeline**: Approximately **300,000 BC – 10,000 BC** (spanning the Pleistocene geological epoch / Ice Age).
- **Subsistence**: Humans lived as pure **hunters and food gatherers**. They had no knowledge of cultivation, animal husbandry, or pottery making.
- **Tools Progression**:
  - **Lower Palaeolithic**: Heavy hand-axes, pebble choppers, cleavers (made primarily from **Quartzite**).
  - **Middle Palaeolithic**: Lighter tools fabricated from stone flakes (**Flake tools**: scrapers, borers, points).
  - **Upper Palaeolithic**: Emergence of **Flake & blade** technology, burins, bone needles, parallel-sided blades, accompanied by the earliest artistic expressions.

## 2. Prehistoric Rock Art and Cave Paintings

Artistic expressions of early humans are preserved on the walls and ceilings of naturally formed rock shelters and caves across India.

### Subjects and Themes
- Confined to:
  - **Simple human figures** (stick-like human silhouettes)
  - **Human activities** (hunting expeditions, collective dancing, ritual gatherings)
  - **Geometric designs** (zigzags, concentric shapes, grids)
  - **Symbols**

### Classification of Paintings
Paintings discovered can be grouped into **three primary categories**:
1. **Man**: Stick-figure representations of hunters, dancers, men armed with sticks or spears.
2. **Animal**: Dynamic sketches of wild fauna including bison, tigers, boars, deer, elephants, and rhinoceroses.
3. **Geometric Symbols**: Linear and abstract motifs.

---

## 3. Geographical Spread of Rock Art in India

Remnants of cave and rock paintings have been discovered in several districts across:
- **Madhya Pradesh** (e.g., Bhimbetka, Jogimara)
- **Uttar Pradesh** (e.g., Mirzapur, Belan Valley shelters)
- **Andhra Pradesh**
- **Telangana** (e.g., Kupgallu)
- **Karnataka** (e.g., Piklihal, Tekkalkotta)
- **Kerala** (e.g., Marayur, Edakkal)
- **Bihar**
- **Uttarakhand** (e.g., Lakhudiyar)
- **Tamil Nadu** (e.g., Karikiyoor)

## 4. Prominent Palaeolithic & Rock Art Sites

| Site | State | Key Features & Historical Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bhimbetka Caves** | **Madhya Pradesh** | • Located in the Raisen district in the Vindhyan foothills.<br>• Represent **the most magnificent prehistoric paintings** in India.<br>• Comprise **nearly 400 painted rock shelters distributed across five clusters**.<br>• UNESCO World Heritage Site displaying continuous habitation from Upper Palaeolithic through Mesolithic to Medieval times. |
| **Lakhudiyar** | **Uttarakhand** | • Situated in Almora district along the River Suyal.<br>• The name *Lakhudiyar* literally means **"one lakh caves"**.<br>• Features paintings depicting man, animals, and geometric patterns in white, black, and red mineral pigments. |
| **Kupgallu** | **Telangana** | • Prominent rock art and prehistoric habitation site. |
| **Piklihal** | **Karnataka** | • Prehistoric rock shelter with engravings and paintings; also an important Neolithic site. |
| **Tekkalkotta** | **Karnataka** | • Granite rock hills with prehistoric rock paintings and stone tool industries. |
| **Jogimara Caves** | **Madhya Pradesh** | • Ancient rock shelters with early rock art and subsequent historical inscriptions. |
| **Karikiyoor** | **Tamil Nadu** | • Located in the Nilgiris; ancient rock art depicting wildlife and hunting scenes. |
