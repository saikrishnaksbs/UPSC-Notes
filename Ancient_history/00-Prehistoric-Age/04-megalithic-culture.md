## 1. Concept and Etymology

- **Definition**: Derived from the Greek words *Megas* ("large") and *Lithos* ("stone"). Megaliths are massive stone monuments or structures erected as sepulchral (burial) monuments or commemorative memorials.
- **Chronological Span**:
  - Construction of megaliths commenced from the **late Neolithic age onwards**.
  - Floruit / Peak era: **6th Century BC to 1st Century AD** (contemporary with early historic period and Sangam Age in South India).
  - Radiocarbon dating at **Hallur** places early Megalithic levels around **1000 BC**, while **Paiyampalli** dates around the **4th Century BC**.

---

## 2. Geographical Distribution

- **Primary Concentration**: While traces occur across various parts of India, archaeological evidence is heavily concentrated in **Peninsular / Deccan India**, especially **south of the Godavari River**.
- **Evidence from Other Parts of India**:
  - **Bihar**: Seraikala
  - **Uttarakhand**: Deodhoora in Almora district
  - **Uttar Pradesh**: Khera near Fatehpur Sikri
  - **Madhya Pradesh / Maharashtra (Vidarbha)**: Nagpur, Chanda, Bhandara districts
  - **Rajasthan**: Dausa near Jaipur
  - **Kashmir**: Burzahom

## 3. Key Excavated Megalithic Sites

### A. Adichanallur (Thoothukudi / South Tamil Nadu)
- Located in the lower Thamirabarani valley.
- Vast prehistoric urn-burial necropolis.
- **Archaeological Discoveries**:
  - Baked earthenware utensils.
  - Distinctive **gold and bronze artefacts** (including gold diadems/fillets, bronze animal figurines).
  - Rare **Mother Goddess sculpture** in bronze.
  - Abundant **iron weapons and implements**: primarily knives, short sword blades, hatchets, daggers, and arrowheads.
  - Large quantities of human bones, skulls, and burial urns.

### B. Brahmagiri (Chitradurga District, Karnataka)
- Excavated by Sir Mortimer Wheeler.
- Demonstrates a rare, uninterrupted cultural stratigraphy spanning:
  $$\text{Microlithic} \longrightarrow \text{Neolithic} \longrightarrow \text{Iron Age (Megalithic)} \longrightarrow \text{Mauryan} \longrightarrow \text{Chalukya-Hoysala}$$
- Identified as the ancient town of *Isila*; contains an **Ashokan Minor Rock Edict** marking the southernmost reach of the Mauryan Empire.

## 4. Distinctive Features of Megalithic Culture

| Feature | Archaeological Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Burial Markers** | Burial sites are predominantly marked on the surface by **large stone circles**, cairn heaps, or dolmens. |
| **Burial Sizing & Urns** | Burials are generally sized to accommodate a human or are even smaller (fractional secondary burials). Several sites feature **Urn Burials (*Ezhuthani*)**, where post-excarnation skeletal remains and ashes were placed inside large terracotta urns. |
| **Iron Technology** | In the Deccan and South India, the Megalithic age was characterized by the widespread use of **iron** (stone tools were completely replaced by iron weapons and implements). |
| **Pottery** | Characteristic **Black and Red Ware (BRW)**, produced through inverted firing; frequently found as grave goods. |
| **Agriculture & Irrigation** | Practice of large-scale agriculture supported by **tank irrigation**. **Rice was the staple food crop**. Presence of iron arrowheads, spears, and javelins indicates hunting remained a strong complementary activity. |
| **Animal Husbandry** | Domestication of a wide variety of animals including cattle, sheep, goats, and domestic fowl/poultry. |
| **Rock Murals & Art** | Megalithic rock paintings and murals depicting hunting scenes have been found at **Marayur** and **Attala** (in Kerala). |

## 5. Classification of Megalithic Monuments

Megaliths are classified into **8 primary architectural types**:

```
                          Megalithic Structures
                                    │
    ┌───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┐
    │                               │                               │
Subterranean / Pit Burials     Surface Stone Settings          Monoliths & Rock-Cut
    │                               │                               │
    ├── Pit Burials                 ├── Stone Circles               ├── Menhirs (Alignments & Avenues)
    ├── Barrows                     ├── Cairn Circles               ├── Rock-Cut Caves
    └── Dolmenoid Cists             └── Hood & Hat Stones / Capstones
```

1. **Rock-Cut Caves**: Subterranean chambers carved directly into laterite scarps (frequent in Malabar/Kerala).
2. **Hood Stones and Hat Stones / Cap Stones (*Kudaikkallu* and *Topikkallu*)**: Convex or dome-shaped dressed stones placed over burial pits.
3. **Menhirs**: Monolithic stone pillars planted vertically into the ground as commemorative memorials (single standing stones, alignments, or parallel avenues).
4. **Dolmenoid Cists**: Burial chambers built of upright stone slabs (*orthostats*) covered by a massive capstone.
5. **Cairn Circles**: Stone circles filled with heaps of rubble, pebbles, and earth (*cairns*) covering grave pits.
6. **Stone Circles**: Single or multiple rings of large boulders encircling an urn or cist burial.
7. **Pit Burials**: Burials placed in simple excavated earthen pits accompanied by grave goods.
8. **Barrows**: Low earthen or rubble mounds raised over grave pits.
