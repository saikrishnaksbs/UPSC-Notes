## 1. Climatic Shift and the Advent of the Neolithic Age

- **Timeline**: Began approximately **10,000 years ago (~8,000 BC)**.
- **Environmental Transition**: Coincided with a major global climate transition:
  - Transition from the cold, arid Pleistocene (Ice Age) to the **warm, humid Holocene**.
  - Widespread **growth and expansion of grasslands**.
  - Surge in herbivorous animal populations dependent on grass (deer, antelope, goat, sheep, and cattle).

---

## 2. Foundations of the Neolithic Revolution

Gordon Childe termed this transformation the **"Neolithic Revolution"** due to radical socio-economic changes:

### A. Domestication of Animals
- Hunters shifted progressively toward **herding and animal husbandry**.
- **Earliest domesticated animals**: **Sheep and goat**, followed by cattle and pigs.
- **Fishing** emerged as a critical subsistence activity alongside herding.

### B. Emergence of Agriculture (Farming)
- Natural grain-bearing wild grasses were gathered, identified, selected, and cultivated.
- **Earliest domesticated crops**: **Wheat and barley**.
- Broadening spectrum of domesticated grains: **Wheat, barley, rice, and lentils** became dietary staples.
- Morphological evolution: Domesticated strains diverged clearly from wild counterparts (e.g., tough rachis preventing wild dispersal of grain).

### C. Settled Sedentary Life & Architecture
- Cultivation required populations to remain in the same location for extended periods (soil tilling, sowing, weeding, watering, and harvesting).
- **Storage Infrastructure**: Storing grain for both daily consumption and next season's seeds compelled innovation:
  - Making large coarse **clay pots** (handmade, later wheel-turned).
  - Weaving plant-fibre baskets.
  - Digging **storage pits** into the ground.
- **Burzahom (Jammu & Kashmir) Pit-Houses**:
  - Dwellings constructed as **subterranean pit-houses** dug into the natural soil, with clay-cut steps leading down.
  - Provided essential thermal insulation against harsh sub-Himalayan winters.
  - **Cooking Hearths**: Charcoal-lined fire hearths discovered both **inside and outside** the pit-houses, indicating seasonal indoor/outdoor culinary activities.

### D. Material Culture & Technology
- **Polished Stone Tools**: Stone implements (celts, chisels, axes) were ground and polished to achieve exceptionally smooth surfaces and fine, sharp cutting edges.
- Stones utilized: **Dyke, Basalt, Dolomite**.
- **Grinding Equipment**: Mortars, pestles, and saddle querns engineered specifically for processing and grinding grain and wild plant produce.
- **Ceramics**: Wide variety of earthen pots for grain storage and boiling food.

## 3. Site Deep-Dive: Mehrgarh (Baluchistan, Pakistan)

*The earliest documented agro-pastoral village settlement in South Asia.*

- **Chronological Span**: Dated between **7000 BC and 2000 BC**.
- **Geographical Setting**:
  - Located on the fertile alluvial Kacchi plains of Baluchistan.
  - Positioned adjacent to the **Bolan Pass**, historically one of the premier arterial mountain passes connecting the Indus Valley to the Iranian Plateau and Central Asia.
- **Key Discoveries & Significance**:
  - **Earliest Agriculture**: Earliest empirical evidence of wheat and barley cultivation and the rearing of sheep and goats in the subcontinent.
  - **Faunal Stratigraphy**: Deeper strata contain bones of wild game (deer, wild pig), while succeeding layers show a clear preponderance of domesticated sheep, goat, and cattle (*Bos indicus*).
  - **Architecture**:
    - Neatly planned square or rectangular mud-brick structures.
    - Each house featured **four or more separate compartments**, some dedicated to grain storage (early granaries).
  - **Burial Practices & Belief in Afterlife**:
    - Several formal cemetery burial sites excavated.
    - Skeletons accompanied by grave offerings: bead ornaments (marine shell, lapis lazuli, turquoise).
    - In one notable grave, a deceased individual was **buried alongside goats**, intended to provide sustenance on the journey into the next world (unmistakable evidence of belief in an afterlife).

## 4. Important Neolithic Sites in the Indian Subcontinent

| Site | Region | Defining Archaeological Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mehrgarh** | Baluchistan, Pakistan | Earliest evidence of farming (wheat/barley), mud-brick rectangular houses with 4+ rooms, granaries, goat burials. |
| **Burzahom** | Jammu & Kashmir | Subterranean **pit-dwellings**, bone tools (harpoons, needles), coarse grey pottery, unique burials of dogs with masters. |
| **Chirand** | Bihar (Saran district) | Exceptional abundance of **bone tools and implements made of deer antlers**, alongside polished stone celts. |
| **Koldihwa** | Uttar Pradesh (Belan Valley) | **Earliest evidence of rice cultivation** in the subcontinent (rice husks embedded in clay pottery), cord-impressed pottery. |
| **Mahagara** | Uttar Pradesh (Belan Valley) | Evidence of rice farming, circular huts, and a fenced **cattle pen** bearing animal hoof marks. |
| **Hallur** | Karnataka (Tungabhadra Valley) | Southern Neolithic-Chalcolithic site; evidence of animal domestication, polished stone axes, early iron transition. |
| **Paiyampalli** | Tamil Nadu (Vellore district) | Southern Neolithic settlement; polished stone celts, black-and-red pottery, cultivation of horse gram and green gram. |
