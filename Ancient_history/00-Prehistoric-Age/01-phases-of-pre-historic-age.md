## 1. Division of History

History can be divided into three distinct phases based on the nature and availability of literary evidence:

| Category | Timeline | Description & Sources |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-History** | **300,000 BC – 2,500 BC** | Period prior to the availability of written or literary sources of study. Reconstructed entirely using archaeological remains (stone tools, pottery, artefacts, cave art). |
| **Proto-History** | **2,500 BC – 600 BC** | Period characterized by events with literary sources that cannot be used or understood (undeciphered scripts, e.g., Indus Valley Civilisation script) or cultures transitioning toward literacy. |
| **History** | **600 BC till Date** | Period with written literary sources that can be read and understood (e.g., Vedic texts, Buddhist/Jain literature, Ashokan inscriptions, Greek accounts). |

---

## 2. Archaeological Sources for Pre-History

Human settlements in India trace back directly to Pre-historic times. While **no written records or literary sources are available**, extensive archaeological sources enable historians to reconstruct this period:
- **Stone tools and implements** (core tools, flakes, blades, microliths, celts)
- **Pottery and earthenware** (coarse grey ware, black and red ware, burnished ware)
- **Artefacts and ornaments** (shell beads, steatite, lapis lazuli)
- **Cave paintings and petroglyphs** (rock shelters like Bhimbetka)
- **Faunal and floral remains** (charred seeds, animal bones, pollen analyses)

## 3. Sub-Division of Pre-History Based on Tool Typology

Pre-History is categorized into successive stages based on the **typology of tools** and the **raw stone materials** used:

```
Pre-History
│
├── Palaeolithic Age (Old Stone Age): 300,000 BC – 10,000 BC
│   ├── Lower Palaeolithic: Quartzite hand-axes, choppers
│   ├── Middle Palaeolithic: Flake tools, scrapers, borers
│   └── Upper Palaeolithic: Flake & blade tools, burins
│
├── Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age): 10,000 BC – 4,000 BC
│   └── Microliths (Agate, Chert); Hunters & food gatherers + animal domestication
│
├── Neolithic Age (New Stone Age): 4,000 BC – 1,800 BC
│   └── Polished stone tools (Dyke, Basalt, Dolomite); Food producers & animal husbandry
│
├── Chalcolithic Age (Copper-Stone Age): 3,500 BC – 1,000 BC
│   └── Copper & Bronze metallurgy alongside stone tools; Agro-pastoral societies
│
└── Iron Age: 1,000 BC – 500 BC
    └── Iron technology; Triggered transition to historical urban civilisation
```

### Archaeological Stages Comparison Table

| Stage | Archaeological Age | Timeline | Diagnostic Tools & Raw Materials | Lifestyle & Subsistence |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Palaeo – lithic age** *(Old – Stone)* | **300,000 BC – 10,000 BC** | Primitive rough stone tools (hand axe, choppers, cleavers).<br>• **Lower**: Quartzite<br>• **Middle**: Flake tools<br>• **Upper**: Flake & blade | Hunters and food gatherers; nomadic lifestyle; no settled dwellings. |
| **2** | **Meso – lithic age** *(Middle – Stone)* | **10,000 BC – 4,000 BC** | Sharp, pointed, very small tools (**microliths**). Raw material mostly used: **Agate**, chert, chalcedony. | Hunters & food gatherers + **earliest animal domestication**. |
| **3** | **Neo – lithic age** *(New – Stone)* | **4,000 BC – 1,800 BC** *(early sites c. 7000 BC)* | **Polished Stone Tools**. Stones mostly used: **Dyke, Basalt, Dolomite**. Grinding stones & mortars. | **Food producers** (farming of wheat, barley, rice) and **animal husbandry**; settled villages. |
| **4** | **Chalco – lithic** *(Copper – stone)* | **3,500 BC – 1,000 BC** | **Copper** (metal) started getting used, followed by **Bronze** (copper + tin alloy). | Mixed farming-pastoral economies; painted pottery; regional cultures. |
| **5** | **Iron Age** | **1,000 BC – 500 BC** | **Iron** technology (weapons, implements, axes, ploughshares). | Agricultural surplus, clearance of forests, state formation. |

---

## 4. Metallurgical Progression in Ancient India

- **Sequence of Metal Use**: In India, **Iron followed Copper and Bronze**.
- **Historical Impact of Iron**: Throughout the Indian subcontinent, the introduction of iron led slowly but perceptibly to the transition from **pre- and proto-historical culture to historical urban culture** (*Second Urbanisation* in the 6th century BC).

---

## 5. Chronology and Scientific Dating Methods

These periods/ages are **not uniform** across the Indian subcontinent (e.g., Neolithic cultures persisted in South India while Bronze Age Harappan urbanism flourished in the Northwest).

To resolve dating across diverse ecological zones, two scientific methods are widely utilized:
1. **Radiocarbon Dating ($C^{14}$)**:
   - Measures the rate of loss/decay of radioactive carbon ($^{14}\text{C}$) in organic materials (bones, shells, charcoal, wood) over time.
   - Half-life of $^{14}\text{C}$ is approximately $5730 \pm 40$ years.
2. **Dendrochronology**:
   - Scientific study of annual growth increments or **tree rings** preserved in archaeological wood specimens.
   - Enables exact calendar-year dating and calibration of radiocarbon curves.
