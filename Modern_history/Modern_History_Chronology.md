# Modern Indian History: Master Chronology (1757 to 1947)

> **UPSC Revision Tool**: Exhaustive chronological sequencing of all modern colonial, constitutional, and nationalist milestones.
> **Cross-Reference**: Aligned with , Bipan Chandra, and Shekhar Bandyopadhyay.

---

## 🏛️ Comprehensive Modern Chronological Master Table

| Year / Date | Epoch / Category | Landmark Historical Event | Key Personalities / Acts | UPSC Key Takeaway & Significance |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **1757 (23 June)** | British Expansion | Battle of Plassey | Robert Clive, Siraj-ud-Daulah, Mir Jafar | Company won via conspiracy and treason; gained control over 24 Parganas; beginning of British Raj. |
| **1760 (22 Jan)** | European Rivalry | Battle of Wandiwash | Sir Eyre Coote defeats Count de Lally | Crushed French military ambitions in India; eliminated French East India Company as imperial rival. |
| **1764 (22 Oct)** | British Conquest | Battle of Buxar | Major Hector Munro vs. Triple Alliance (Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daulah, Shah Alam II) | Decisive military victory proving British field superiority; de facto mastery of northern India. |
| **1765 (August)** | Colonial Governance | Treaty of Allahabad & Dual Government | Robert Clive, Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II | Company secured Diwani rights (Bengal, Bihar, Orissa); Clive instituted Dual Government (1765–1772). |
| **1767 – 1769** | Regional Resistance | First Anglo-Mysore War | Hyder Ali defeats British; Treaty of Madras | Hyder Ali outmaneuvered British-Nizam coalition; dictated terms of peace at the gates of Madras. |
| **1770** | Economic Disaster | The Great Bengal Famine | Catastrophic failure of harvest & monsoon | Over 10 million people (one-third of Bengal's populace) died while Company extracted full revenue. |
| **1772** | Administrative Reform | Abolition of Dual Government | Warren Hastings, Board of Revenue | Terminated Dual System; Company assumed direct administrative and fiscal responsibility over Bengal. |
| **1773** | Constitutional Act | Regulating Act of 1773 | Lord North, Warren Hastings | First British parliamentary control over Company; Governor of Bengal made Governor-General; Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774). |
| **1775 – 1782** | Regional Resistance | First Anglo-Maratha War | Mahadji Scindia, Nana Phadnavis, Warren Hastings | Marathas won Battle of Wadgaon (1779); ended with Treaty of Salbai (1782), securing 20 years of peace. |
| **1780 – 1784** | Regional Resistance | Second Anglo-Mysore War | Hyder Ali (died 1782), Tipu Sultan, Sir Eyre Coote | Battle of Pollilur; Porto Novo; ended in mutual restitution under Treaty of Mangalore (1784). |
| **1784** | Constitutional Act | Pitt's India Act of 1784 | William Pitt the Younger | Established Dual Control (Board of Control for politics, Court of Directors for commerce); coined 'British Possessions in India'. |
| **1790 – 1792** | Regional Resistance | Third Anglo-Mysore War | Lord Cornwallis vs. Tipu Sultan | Cornwallis besieged Seringapatam; Treaty of Seringapatam (1792) stripped Tipu of half his kingdom. |
| **1793** | Colonial Land Policy | Permanent Settlement of Bengal | Lord Cornwallis, Sir John Shore | Recognized Zamindars as permanent owners; revenue fixed perpetually at 10/11th to Company; Sunset Law. |
| **1798** | British Expansion | Introduction of Subsidiary Alliance | Lord Wellesley, Nizam of Hyderabad | Indian rulers surrendered external affairs, hosted British garrison, expelled French; Nizam signed first. |
| **1799 (4 May)** | Regional Resistance | Fourth Anglo-Mysore War | Tipu Sultan, Lord Wellesley, General Harris | Fall of Seringapatam; Tipu died heroically fighting; Wodeyar infant installed under Subsidiary Alliance. |
| **1802** | British Expansion | Treaty of Bassein | Peshwa Baji Rao II, Lord Wellesley | Cowardly Peshwa surrendered Maratha sovereignty for British protection, triggering Second Maratha War. |
| **1803 – 1805** | Regional Resistance | Second Anglo-Maratha War | Arthur Wellesley, Lord Lake vs. Scindia, Bhonsle, Holkar | Battles of Assaye and Laswari; British captured Delhi and Agra; forced Treaties of Deogaon & Surji-Anjangaon. |
| **1809** | Frontier Diplomacy | Treaty of Amritsar | Maharaja Ranjit Singh & Charles Metcalfe | Established Sutlej River as perpetual boundary between Sikh Empire and British East India Company. |
| **1813** | Constitutional Act | Charter Act of 1813 | British Parliament | Ended Company's commercial monopoly in India (except Tea & China trade); allotted Rs. 1 lakh for education; allowed missionaries. |
| **1817 – 1818** | Regional Resistance | Third Anglo-Maratha War | Lord Hastings vs. Peshwa Baji Rao II | Battles of Khadki & Koregaon; Peshwaship abolished; Baji Rao II exiled to Bithur; Pax Britannica established. |
| **1820** | Colonial Land Policy | Ryotwari System Introduced | Sir Thomas Munro & Alexander Read | Direct settlement with individual ryots in Madras and Bombay Presidencies; revenue assessed at 50–60%. |
| **1822** | Colonial Land Policy | Mahalwari System Introduced | Holt Mackenzie & Robert Merttins Bird | Revenue settled with village bodies (*Mahals*) through headmen (*Lambardars*) in NWFP, Punjab, and Central India. |
| **1828 (20 August)** | Socio-Religious Reform | Brahmo Sabha / Samaj Founded | Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Debendranath Tagore | Preached monotheism; denounced polytheism, idol worship, priestcraft, caste; heralded Modern Renaissance. |
| **1829 (4 Dec)** | Social Legislation | Abolition of Sati Act (Regulation XVII) | Lord William Bentinck & Raja Ram Mohan Roy | Declared practice of Sati illegal and punishable as culpable homicide in Bengal Presidency. |
| **1833** | Constitutional Act | Charter Act of 1833 | Lord William Bentinck, Lord Macaulay | Abolished all Company commercial activity; Governor-General of Bengal made Governor-General of India; Macaulay appointed Law Member. |
| **1835** | Education Policy | Macaulay's Minute on Education | Lord Macaulay, Lord William Bentinck | Promoted Western education in English language; introduced Filtration Theory (educating upper classes to filter to masses). |
| **1845 – 1846** | Regional Resistance | First Anglo-Sikh War | Lal Singh, Tej Singh vs. Sir Hugh Gough | Battles of Mudki, Ferozeshah, Sobraon; Sikh army betrayed by generals; Treaty of Lahore & Bhairowal; Kashmir sold to Gulab Singh. |
| **1848** | British Expansion | Lord Dalhousie & Doctrine of Lapse | Lord Dalhousie | Annexed states without natural male heirs: Satara (1848), Jaitpur/Sambalpur (1849), Baghat, Udaipur, Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854). |
| **1848 – 1849** | Regional Resistance | Second Anglo-Sikh War | Diwan Mulraj, Sher Singh Attariwalla vs. Gough | Battles of Chillianwala & Gujarat ('Battle of Guns'); annexation of Punjab on 29 March 1849; Koh-i-Noor to Crown. |
| **1853** | Modern Infrastructure | First Indian Railway Line & Telegraph | Lord Dalhousie | First passenger train ran 34 km from Bombay to Thane (16 April 1853); telegraph link opened from Calcutta to Agra. |
| **1854** | Education Policy | Charles Wood's Education Despatch | Sir Charles Wood, Lord Dalhousie | 'Magna Carta of English Education in India'; recommended vernacular primary schools, Anglo-vernacular high schools, universities at Presidencies. |
| **1855 – 1856** | Tribal Uprising | The Santhal Hool / Rebellion | Sidhu, Kanhu, Chand, and Bhairav Murmu | Over 60,000 Santhals revolted against Dikus, moneylenders, and railway contractors; created Santhal Parganas (1855). |
| **1856** | British Aggression | Annexation of Awadh & Social Acts | Lord Dalhousie, Lord Canning | Annexed Awadh on pretext of 'misgovernance'; passed Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar) & General Service Enlistment Act. |
| **1857 (March–May)** | Anti-Colonial Revolt | The Great Revolt of 1857 | Mangal Pandey (Barrackpore), Meerut sepoys | Sepoys revolted over greased Enfield cartridges; captured Delhi; crowned Bahadur Shah Zafar *Shahenshah-e-Hindustan*. |
| **1857 – 1858** | Anti-Colonial Revolt | Centers of 1857 Resistance | Nana Saheb, Tatya Tope, Lakshmibai, Begum Hazrat Mahal, Kunwar Singh | Heroic resistance across Northern and Central India; recaptured by Campbell, Outram, and Hugh Rose after savage terror. |
| **1858 (1 Nov)** | Constitutional Shift | Queen Victoria's Proclamation & GOI Act 1858 | Lord Canning read at Allahabad Durbar | Liquidated East India Company; direct Crown governance; Governor-General designated Viceroy; promised religious non-interference. |
| **1859 – 1860** | Peasant Resistance | Indigo Revolt in Bengal | Digambar Biswas, Bishnu Biswas, Dinabandhu Mitra | Peasants refused to plant indigo; dramatized in play *Neel Darpan*; led to Indigo Commission (1860) confirming peasant rights. |
| **1861** | Institutional Acts | Indian Councils Act & High Courts Act 1861 | Lord Canning | Restored legislative powers to Bombay & Madras; introduced Portfolio system; empowered Viceroy to issue Ordinances. |
| **1875** | Socio-Religious Reform | Arya Samaj & Aligarh College Founded | Swami Dayananda Saraswati; Sir Syed Ahmad Khan | Dayananda gave slogan 'Go Back to the Vedas'; Syed Ahmad founded MAO College at Aligarh for modern scientific education. |
| **1878** | Imperial Reaction | Vernacular Press Act & Arms Act | Lord Lytton | 'Gagging Act' targeting vernacular anti-colonial newspapers (*Amrita Bazar Patrika* switched to English overnight); banned firearms for Indians. |
| **1881 – 1882** | Local Self-Governance | Factory Act & Ripon's Local Self-Government | Lord Ripon ('The Good Viceroy') | First regular all-India census (1881); First Factory Act; 1882 Local Self-Government Resolution (Father of Local Self-Government). |
| **1883** | Racial Controversy | The Ilbert Bill Controversy | Sir C.P. Ilbert, Lord Ripon | European fury over bill allowing Indian judges to try Europeans; exposed white colonial racism and catalyzed organized national politics. |
| **1885 (28–31 Dec)** | Nationalist Genesis | Foundation of Indian National Congress | W.C. Bonnerjee (President), A.O. Hume (Founder) | First session at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay; attended by 72 delegates; inaugurated Moderate constitutional era. |
| **1892** | Constitutional Act | Indian Councils Act 1892 | British Parliament | Marginally enlarged legislative councils; introduced indirect election principle; allowed discussion of imperial budget. |
| **1897** | Radical Resistance | Chapekar Brothers assassinate Rand | Damodar & Balkrishna Chapekar | Assassinated tyrannical Plague Commissioner W.C. Rand in Pune; first political assassination of British official in modern India. |
| **1899 – 1900** | Tribal Uprising | The Munda Ulgulan (The Great Tumult) | Birsa Munda (*Dharti Aba*) | Armed revolt against *Bet-begari* (forced labor) and destruction of *Khuntkatti* land tenure; led to Chhotanagpur Tenancy Act 1908. |
| **1905 (16 Oct)** | Anti-Partition Struggle| Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi Movement | Lord Curzon, Rabindranath Tagore, Surendranath Banerjea | Partition took effect; observed as day of mourning (*Arandhan*, Raksha Bandhan); historic Boycott Resolution passed at Calcutta Town Hall (7 Aug). |
| **1906 (30 Dec)** | Muslim Politics | Foundation of All-India Muslim League | Nawab Salimullah of Dacca, Aga Khan, Mohsin-ul-Mulk | Formed at Dacca to safeguard Muslim aristocratic political rights and cultivate loyalty to British Crown. |
| **1907 (Dec)** | Congress Fracture | The Surat Split | Rashbehari Ghosh, Tilak, Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta | Ideological clash over extending Swadeshi nationwide; violent pandemonium at Surat session; Moderates expelled Extremists. |
| **1908** | Revolutionary Action | Alipore Bomb Case & Tilak's Imprisonment | Khudiram Bose, Prafulla Chaki, Aurobindo Ghosh, Bal Gangadhar Tilak | Kingsford carriage bombing; Khudiram hanged; Aurobindo acquitted by C.R. Das; Tilak sentenced to 6 years in Mandalay prison. |
| **1909** | Constitutional Act | Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909) | John Morley, Lord Minto II | Introduced **Separate Electorates for Muslims**, legalizing communalism; Satyendra Prasad Sinha became first Indian in Viceroy's Executive Council. |
| **1911 (Dec)** | Imperial Reversal | Annulment of Bengal Partition & Delhi Durbar | King George V, Lord Hardinge II | Annulled Partition of Bengal; created Bihar and Orissa as separate provinces; shifted imperial capital from Calcutta to Delhi. |
| **1913 (Nov)** | Revolutionary Diaspora | Foundation of the Ghadar Party | Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, Kartar Singh Sarabha | Formed in San Francisco; published weekly *The Ghadar*; aimed at armed rebellion in India during World War I. |
| **1914** | Wartime Incident | Komagata Maru Incident & World War I | Baba Gurdit Singh, Lord Hardinge | Japanese steamship carrying 376 Indian immigrants turned back by Canada; fired upon at Budge Budge (Calcutta); WWI broke out in Europe. |
| **1915 (9 Jan)** | Gandhian Arrival | Mahatma Gandhi Returns to India | Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi | Returned from South Africa after 21 years of successful Satyagraha; celebrated annually as *Pravasi Bharatiya Divas*. |
| **1916 (Dec)** | Nationalist Unity | Lucknow Session: Reunion & The Lucknow Pact | A.C. Mazumdar, Tilak, Annie Besant, Mohammad Ali Jinnah | Extremists readmitted into Congress; historic INC-Muslim League joint agreement accepting separate electorates for common self-government demand. |
| **1916 – 1918** | Mass Awakening | The Home Rule League Agitations | Bal Gangadhar Tilak & Annie Besant | Tilak launched League in Belgaum (April); Besant in Madras (Sept); popularized demand for Swaraj through newspapers (*Kesari*, *New India*). |
| **1917 (April)** | Early Gandhian Move | Champaran Satyagraha (First Civil Disobedience)| Mahatma Gandhi, Rajkumar Shukla, Rajendra Prasad | Opposed exploitative European planter *Tinkathia* system (forced 3/20th indigo planting); Gandhi's first great victory in India. |
| **1917 (20 August)** | Constitutional Policy | The Montagu Declaration (August Declaration) | Edwin Montagu (Secretary of State) | Pledged British policy of 'gradual development of self-governing institutions' to achieve responsible government within British Empire. |
| **1918 (Feb–March)** | Early Gandhian Moves | Ahmedabad Mill Strike & Kheda Satyagraha | Gandhi, Anusuya Sarabhai, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel | Ahmedabad: First Hunger Strike (secured 35% wage hike); Kheda: First Non-Cooperation (peasant tax remission after crop failure). |
| **1919 (March)** | Coercive Act | The Rowlatt Act (Anarchical Crimes Act) | Sir Sidney Rowlatt, Lord Chelmsford | Empowered government to arrest and detain political suspects for two years without warrant or trial ('No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal'). |
| **1919 (13 April)** | Colonial Barbarism | The Jallianwala Bagh Massacre | Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer, Michael O'Dwyer | Unarmed Baisakhi gathering gunned down inside closed garden in Amritsar; over 1,000 murdered; Tagore surrendered British Knighthood. |
| **1919 (Dec)** | Constitutional Act | Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (GOI Act 1919) | Edwin Montagu & Lord Chelmsford | Introduced **Dyarchy in Provinces** (Transferred vs. Reserved subjects); introduced bicameral legislature at Centre; extended communal electorates. |
| **1920 (1 August)** | Mass Mobilization | Non-Cooperation & Khilafat Movement Launched | Mahatma Gandhi, Ali Brothers (Shaukat & Mohammad Ali) | Tilak died same day; movement endorsed at Calcutta & Nagpur sessions; nationwide boycott of foreign cloth, courts, and colonial schools. |
| **1922 (4 Feb)** | Movement Withdrawal | Chauri Chaura Incident & Bardoli Resolution | Mahatma Gandhi | Violent crowd burnt police station killing 22 policemen; Gandhi unilaterally suspended Non-Cooperation Movement on 12 February. |
| **1923 (1 Jan)** | Legislative Strategy | Formation of the Swaraj Party | C.R. Das (President), Motilal Nehru (Secretary) | Advocated entering councils to 'wreck dyarchy from within'; won 42 seats in 1923 assembly elections. |
| **1925** | Parliamentary & Armed | Vithalbhai Patel Speaker & Kakori Train Action | Vithalbhai Patel; Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqullah Khan | Patel elected first Indian Central Assembly Speaker; HRA looted government cash at Kakori; Bismil & Ashfaqullah hanged (1927). |
| **1927 (Nov)** | Colonial Review | Appointment of Simon Commission | Stanley Baldwin, Sir John Simon | Seven-member all-white statutory commission appointed to review 1919 Act; unanimous Indian boycott. |
| **1928 (Feb–Nov)** | National Defiance | Simon Boycott, Lala's Death & Nehru Report | Lala Lajpat Rai, Motilal Nehru, Jinnah | 'Simon Go Back' black flag protests; Lala battered by police lathis, died 17 Nov; Nehru Report drafted; Jinnah countered with 14 Points. |
| **1928 (June)** | Peasant Triumph | The Bardoli Satyagraha | Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel | Women of Bardoli bestowed title 'Sardar' upon Patel after successful peasant protest rolled back 22% land revenue hike to 6.03%. |
| **1929 (8 April)** | Revolutionary Ideology | Central Assembly Bombing | Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt | Threw harmless smoke bombs and leaflets ('To make the deaf hear') into Delhi Assembly; protested Public Safety & Trade Disputes Bills. |
| **1929 (13 Sept)** | Prison Martyrdom | Martyrdom of Jatin Das | Jatindra Nath Das | Died on 63rd day of historic hunger strike in Lahore Central Jail demanding political prisoner rights for revolutionaries. |
| **1929 (Dec)** | Sovereign Declaration| Lahore Congress Session: Purna Swaraj | Jawaharlal Nehru (President) | Passed historic Complete Independence (*Purna Swaraj*) resolution on banks of Ravi; fixed 26 Jan 1930 as First Independence Day. |
| **1930 (26 Jan)** | National Pledge | First Independence Day Celebrated | Pan-Indian populace | Millions gathered across towns and villages taking the solemn pledge of complete independence from British imperial slavery. |
| **1930 (12 Mar – 6 Apr)**| Epic Satyagraha | The Dandi March & Breaking of Salt Law | Mahatma Gandhi & 78 chosen satyagrahis | 240-mile march from Sabarmati to Dandi; Gandhi picked up salt on 6 April, launching nationwide Civil Disobedience Movement. |
| **1930 (18 April)** | Armed Insurrection | The Chittagong Armoury Raid | Masterda Surya Sen, Pritilata Waddedar, Kalpana Datta | Captured British armouries in East Bengal; proclaimed Provisional Revolutionary Government; fought pitched battle on Jalalabad Hill. |
| **1930 (May)** | Non-Violent Heroism | The Dharasana Salt Works Raid | Sarojini Naidu, Imam Saheb, Manilal Gandhi | 2,500 satyagrahis marched in peaceful waves, battered down by steel lathis; reported worldwide by Webb Miller; shattered British moral claim. |
| **1930 (Nov) – 1931** | Constitutional Parley | First Round Table Conference (London) | Ramsay MacDonald, B.R. Ambedkar, Tej Bahadur Sapru | Congress boycotted; discussed federation and provincial autonomy; proved unviable without Congress participation. |
| **1931 (5 March)** | Sovereign Parity | The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (Delhi Pact) | Mahatma Gandhi & Viceroy Lord Irwin | British placed Congress on equal footing; government released satyagrahis and allowed domestic salt making; Congress suspended agitation. |
| **1931 (23 March)** | Supreme Sacrifice | Execution of Bhagat Singh, Rajguru & Sukhdev | Bhagat Singh, Shivaram Rajguru, Sukhdev Thapar | Hanged in secret at Lahore Central Jail; national mourning; immortalized as *Shaheed Bhagat Singh*. |
| **1931 (March)** | Economic Blueprint | Karachi Congress Session | Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (President) | Endorsed Gandhi-Irwin Pact; passed historic resolutions on **Fundamental Rights** and the **National Economic Programme** (drafted by Nehru). |
| **1931 (Sept–Dec)** | Diplomatic Deadlock | Second Round Table Conference (London) | Mahatma Gandhi (sole INC delegate) | Deadlocked on communal question; Gandhi rejected minority quotas prioritized over freedom; returned empty-handed. |
| **1932 (16 August)** | Communal Wedge | Ramsay MacDonald's Communal Award | British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald | Granted **Separate Electorates for Depressed Classes (Dalits)**, treating them as a separate minority community from Hindus. |
| **1932 (24 Sept)** | Caste Integration | The Poona Pact | Mahatma Gandhi (fasting in Yerwada) & Dr. B.R. Ambedkar | Abandoned separate electorates for Depressed Classes; increased reserved legislative seats from 71 to **148** under joint electorates. |
| **1932 (Nov–Dec)** | Constitutional Parley | Third Round Table Conference | British Government | Poorly attended; issued White Paper (1933) which formed the basis of the Government of India Act 1935. |
| **1934 (April)** | Movement Transition | Formal Withdrawal of Civil Disobedience | Mahatma Gandhi, Congress Socialist Party (CSP) | Agitation formally ended; Congress returned to electoral politics; Acharya Narendra Deva & JP founded CSP within Congress. |
| **1935 (August)** | Constitutional Act | Government of India Act 1935 | British Parliament | Proposed All-India Federation; introduced **Provincial Autonomy**; introduced dyarchy at Centre; established Federal Court & RBI. |
| **1937 (Feb)** | Democratic Triumph | 1937 Provincial Assembly Elections | Indian National Congress vs. Muslim League | Congress swept elections, forming absolute ministries in 8 out of 11 provinces; League failed to win majority in any Muslim province. |
| **1937 – 1939** | Popular Governance | 28 Months of Congress Rule | C. Rajagopalachari, Govind Ballabh Pant, B.G. Kher | Restored civil liberties, passed tenant debt relief acts, introduced prohibition; National Planning Committee set up under Nehru (1938). |
| **1939 (3 Sept)** | Imperial Arrogance | Outbreak of WWII & Resignation of Ministries | Lord Linlithgow, Congress Working Committee | Linlithgow declared India at war without consulting assemblies; all 8 Congress ministries resigned in protest (Oct–Nov 1939). |
| **1939 (22 Dec)** | Communal Celebration | The 'Day of Deliverance' | Mohammad Ali Jinnah & Muslim League | Muslim League celebrated resignation of Congress ministries with British blessing. |
| **1940 (23 March)** | Partition Genesis | The Lahore Resolution (Pakistan Resolution) | Mohammad Ali Jinnah, Fazlul Huq | Muslim League demanded independent sovereign states in Muslim-majority northwestern and eastern zones of India. |
| **1940 (8 August)** | War Concession | Lord Linlithgow's August Offer | Viceroy Lord Linlithgow | Offered Dominion Status after war, expansion of Viceroy's Council, and minority veto; rejected by Congress and League. |
| **1940 (Oct) – 1941** | Moral Protest | Individual Civil Disobedience (Satyagraha) | Mahatma Gandhi, Vinoba Bhave (1st), Nehru (2nd) | Asserted democratic freedom of speech against imperial war; over 25,000 individual satyagrahis courted arrest without mass disruption. |
| **1942 (Mar–Apr)** | Wartime Mission | The Cripps Mission | Sir Stafford Cripps | Proposed post-war Dominion status and provincial secession clause; rejected by Gandhi as *'post-dated cheque on a crashing bank'*. |
| **1942 (8 August)** | Final Mass Rebellion | The Quit India Movement ('Do or Die') | Mahatma Gandhi, AICC Gowalia Tank (Bombay) | Gandhi gave call *'Do or Die'*; Operation Zero Hour arrested all leaders on 9 Aug; massive spontaneous rural and urban uprising. |
| **1942 – 1944** | Underground Networks | Parallel Governments & Secret Radio | Aruna Asaf Ali, Jayaprakash Narayan, Usha Mehta, Nana Patil | Prati Sarkars at Balia (Chittu Pandey), Tamluk Jatiya Sarkar (Bengal), and Satara (Nana Patil); Congress Secret Radio broadcasted. |
| **1943 (Oct)** | Armed Liberation | Provisional Government of Azad Hind (INA) | Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose | Proclaimed Free India government in Singapore; slogans *'Delhi Chalo'*, *'Jai Hind'*; Rani of Jhansi Regiment; fought at Kohima/Imphal (1944). |
| **1943** | Colonial Catastrophe | The Great Bengal Famine | Winston Churchill's war cabinet policies | Over 3 million died of starvation due to military rice diversions, boat denials, and Churchill's refusal to ship relief supplies. |
| **1945 (June–July)** | Political Deadlock | The Wavell Plan & Simla Conference | Lord Wavell, Jawaharlal Nehru, Jinnah | Failed because Jinnah insisted that only the Muslim League had the exclusive right to nominate Muslim members to the Executive Council. |
| **1945 (Nov) – 1946** | National Resurgence | The Red Fort INA Trials | Prem Sahgal, Gurbaksh Dhillon, Shah Nawaz Khan | Joint court-martial of Hindu, Sikh, and Muslim officers; defended by Bhulabhai Desai & Nehru; pan-Indian mass fury forced their release. |
| **1946 (18–23 Feb)** | Armed Forces Revolt | The Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny | B.C. Dutt, M.S. Khan (HMIS Talwar) | 20,000 naval ratings mutinied across 78 ships in Bombay and Karachi against racial abuse; shattered the loyalty of British armed forces. |
| **1946 (Mar–June)** | Transfer Framework | The Cabinet Mission Plan | Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander | Rejected Pakistan; proposed Three-Tier Federation with Grouping (Sections A, B, C); Constituent Assembly to be elected. |
| **1946 (16 August)** | Communal Holocaust | Direct Action Day & Great Calcutta Killings | Mohammad Ali Jinnah, H.S. Suhrawardy | League called for Direct Action; unleashed horrific mass slaughter in Calcutta (5,000 dead); spread pogroms to Noakhali, Bihar, and Punjab. |
| **1946 (2 Sept)** | Provisional Rule | Formation of the Interim Government | Jawaharlal Nehru (Vice-President) | Nehru took office with 11 colleagues; Muslim League joined in October only to sabotage governance through Finance Minister Liaquat Ali Khan. |
| **1946 (9 Dec)** | Constitution Making | First Meeting of the Constituent Assembly | Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha (Temp. Chairman) | Met in New Delhi; Dr. Rajendra Prasad elected permanent President (11 Dec); Nehru moved historic Objectives Resolution (13 Dec). |
| **1947 (20 Feb)** | Imperial Deadline | Clement Attlee's Historic Declaration | British Prime Minister Clement Attlee | Announced British determination to transfer power to responsible Indian hands no later than **30 June 1948**; replaced Wavell with Mountbatten. |
| **1947 (3 June)** | Partition Blueprint | The Mountbatten Plan (June 3rd Plan) | Lord Louis Mountbatten | Accepted Partition of British India into India and Pakistan; partition of Punjab and Bengal; advanced independence date to **15 August 1947**. |
| **1947 (18 July)** | Imperial Statute | Indian Independence Act 1947 | British Parliament | Terminated British suzerainty over India and princely states; created two independent sovereign Dominions of India and Pakistan on 15 August 1947. |
| **1947 (14–15 August)**| Birth of Free Nation | Independence and Partition of India | Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Patel, Mahatma Gandhi | Pakistan born 14 August; India attained freedom midnight 15 August; Nehru delivered *'Tryst with Destiny'* address; Patel integrated 565 states. |
