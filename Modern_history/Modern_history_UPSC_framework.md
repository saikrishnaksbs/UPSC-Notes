# 1. Carnatic Wars & European Commercial Rivalry (1740–1763)

```text
CARNATIC WARS (1740–1763)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Decline of Mughal central authority in Deccan
│   ├── Commercial rivalry between British and French East India Companies
│   ├── War of Austrian Succession in Europe
│   └── Disputed successions in Hyderabad and Carnatic
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Political: Imperial rivalries transplanted from Europe to India
│   ├── Economic: Monopolistic trade control over Coromandel textile exports
│   └── Strategic: Puppet nawab installations by Dupleix and Clive
│
├── MAIN EVENTS
│   ├── First Carnatic War (1746–1748): Battle of St. Thome
│   ├── Second Carnatic War (1749–1754): Battles of Ambur and Arcot
│   └── Third Carnatic War (1758–1763): Decisive Battle of Wandiwash
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Joseph Francois Dupleix, Robert Clive, Stringer Lawrence
│   └── Chanda Sahib, Anwar-ud-din, Nasir Jung, Muzaffar Jung
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Recall of Dupleix by French government (1754)
│   ├── British naval supremacy blockading French supplies
│   └── Complete destruction of French fortifications at Pondicherry
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Elimination of French political ambitions in India
│   ├── British East India Company established unchallenged coastal mastery
│   └── Carnatic and Hyderabad turned into British client dependencies
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Proved small European-trained infantry could route large Indian armies
    └── Laid structural foundation for British territorial conquest of India
```

### 1. Background
- **Decline of Central Authority**: Death of Aurangzeb and Nizam-ul-Mulk's preoccupation weakened southern subahs.
- **European Commercial Enclaves**: French headquartered at Pondicherry; British East India Company anchored at Fort St. George (Madras).
- **Austrian Succession Spillover**: European hostilities between Great Britain and France triggered immediate naval clashes in Indian waters.
- **Coromandel Coast Trade**: High profits from cotton calicoes, silk, and spices incentivized exclusive imperial trade monopolies.

### 2. Causes
- **Political Causes**:
  - Absence of strong regional paramountcy enabled foreign companies to interfere in local dynastic disputes.
  - Dupleix's visionary ambition to convert a commercial corporation into an expansive territorial empire.
- **Economic Causes**:
  - Competing mercantilist desires to establish total monopoly over Coromandel weaving centers and ports.
  - Need to eliminate rival European tariff exemptions and customs privileges.
- **Immediate Causes**:
  - First War: British Royal Navy under Commodore Curtis Barnett seized French merchant ships (1745).
  - Second War: Simultaneous disputed successions to thrones of Hyderabad and Carnatic (1748).
  - Third War: Outbreak of the Seven Years' War in Europe (1756).

### 3. Main Events
- **First Carnatic War (1746–1748)**:
  - French captured Madras (1746) under Admiral La Bourdonnais and Governor Dupleix.
  - **Battle of St. Thome (1746)**: Tiny French force decisively defeated Nawab Anwar-ud-din's 10,000-strong cavalry army.
  - Concluded by **Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748)**; Madras restored to British in exchange for Louisbourg (North America).
- **Second Carnatic War (1749–1754)**:
  - Dupleix backed Muzaffar Jung (Hyderabad) and Chanda Sahib (Carnatic).
  - British supported Nasir Jung (Hyderabad) and Muhammad Ali (Carnatic).
  - **Battle of Ambur (1749)**: Anwar-ud-din defeated and killed; Chanda Sahib became Nawab of Carnatic.
  - **Siege of Arcot (1751)**: Robert Clive captured and heroically defended Arcot for 53 days.
- **Third Carnatic War (1758–1763)**:
  - French dispatched Count de Lally; captured Fort St. David (1758).
  - Lally committed strategic blunder by recalling Bussy from Hyderabad court.
  - **Battle of Wandiwash (January 1760)**: British General Sir Eyre Coote crushed French forces under Lally.
  - British captured Pondicherry (1761), demolishing its fortifications.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Joseph Francois Dupleix**: French Governor-General; pioneered subsidiary alliance tactics and European military intervention in Indian courts.
- **Robert Clive**: British clerk-turned-military officer; seized Arcot (1751), turning the strategic tide in favor of British.
- **Count de Lally**: French commander-in-chief; impetuous leadership and poor diplomacy alienated Indian allies and French officers.
- **Sir Eyre Coote**: British military commander; delivered decisive victory at Wandiwash (1760).
- **Charles Joseph Patissier, Marquis de Bussy**: Brilliant French officer who maintained French diplomatic control over the Nizam of Hyderabad.

### 5. British Response
- **Naval Superiority**: Deployed powerful Royal Navy squadrons to isolate French settlements and intercept reinforcements.
- **Financial Mobilization**: Leveraged vast commercial revenues from Bengal trade after 1757 to fund Carnatic military campaigns.
- **Diplomatic Neutralization**: Concluded **Treaty of Paris (1763)**; French factories returned but strictly forbidden from maintaining troops or fortifications.

### 6. Consequences
- **Elimination of French Power**: French East India Company reduced to mere commercial trading posts at Pondicherry, Chandernagore, and Mahe.
- **Puppet Carnatic Rulers**: Nawab Muhammad Ali made completely dependent on British financial loans and military protection.
- **Northern Circars Ceded**: British secured fertile coastal Northern Circars strip from the Nizam of Hyderabad.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Military Revolution**: Exposed fatal vulnerability of traditional Indian feudal cavalry against disciplined European musket-firing infantry.
- **Imperial Blueprint**: Provided British East India Company the master operational strategy used subsequently to conquer Bengal, Mysore, and Marathas.

# 2. British Conquest of Bengal: Plassey (1757) to Buxar (1764) & Dual Government

```text
CONQUEST OF BENGAL (1757–1772)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Bengal as richest Mughal Subah (contributed 50% of Asian trade imports)
│   ├── Farrukhsiyar's Farman of 1717 granting Dastak privileges
│   └── Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah's accession and defiance of Company encroachments
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Political: Unauthorised fortification of Fort William by British
│   ├── Economic: Rampant misuse of private trade Dastaks by Company officials
│   └── Immediate: Black Hole Tragedy allegations and refuge to fugitive Krishna Das
│
├── MAIN EVENTS
│   ├── Treaty of Alinagar (February 1757)
│   ├── Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Betrayal by Mir Jafar
│   ├── Deposition of Mir Jafar and accession of Mir Qasim (1760)
│   ├── Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764): Triple Alliance routed
│   └── Treaty of Allahabad (1765): Diwani rights granted to Company
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Siraj-ud-Daulah, Mir Jafar, Mir Qasim
│   ├── Robert Clive, Major Hector Munro
│   └── Jagat Seth (Fateh Chand), Omichund, Rai Durlabh
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Installation of compliant puppet Nawabs (Mir Jafar, Mir Qasim, Najm-ud-Daulah)
│   ├── Institution of Robert Clive's Dual Government (1765–1772)
│   └── Direct annexation and abolition of Dual System by Warren Hastings (1772)
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Complete economic plunder and Bengal Famine of 1770
│   ├── Company transformed from merchant trader to sovereign territorial master
│   └── Bengal revenues financed subsequent British military expansions across India
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Buxar made British de facto masters of Northern India up to Awadh
    └── Laid economic bedrock of British colonial empire in Asia
```

### 1. Background
- **Economic Pre-eminence**: Bengal accounted for nearly 60% of British East India Company imports from Asia (saltpeter, silk, calico).
- **Imperial Farman of 1717**: Emperor Farrukhsiyar granted duty-free trade rights against annual payment of Rs. 3,000.
- **Sovereign Friction**: Successive Nawabs (Murshid Quli Khan, Alivardi Khan) resisted Company attempts to subvert provincial customs revenues.
- **Young Nawab's Accession (1756)**: Siraj-ud-Daulah succeeded Alivardi Khan; faced treacherous court intrigues from Ghaseti Begum and Shaukat Jung.

### 2. Causes
- **Political Causes**:
  - British gave asylum to political fugitive Krishna Das, son of corrupt governor Raj Ballabh.
  - British insulted the sovereign Nawab by refusing to dismantle illegal fortifications at Fort William (Calcutta).
- **Economic Causes**:
  - British private merchants rampantly misused duty-free imperial trade permits (**Dastaks**), defrauding provincial treasury.
  - Indian merchants suffered unfair competition, leading to widespread loss of internal trade revenue.
- **Immediate Causes**:
  - Siraj-ud-Daulah attacked and seized Calcutta (June 1756); renamed it **Alinagar**.
  - Exaggerated British propaganda of **Black Hole Tragedy** (Holwell claimed 123 soldiers died of suffocation).

### 3. Main Events
- **Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757)**:
  - Clive forged secret conspiracy with Commander-in-Chief Mir Jafar, financier Jagat Seth, and Omichund.
  - Battle fought on banks of Bhagirathi River; Mir Jafar and Rai Durlabh kept their armies inactive.
  - Siraj-ud-Daulah fled; captured and assassinated by Mir Jafar's son Miran.
- **Reign of Mir Qasim (1760–1763)**:
  - Mir Qasim replaced Mir Jafar (1760); ceded Burdwan, Midnapore, and Chittagong districts to Company.
  - Shifted capital from Murshidabad to **Munger** to escape British surveillance.
  - Modernized army with European guns and abolished all internal transit customs to level playing field for Indian traders.
- **Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764)**:
  - Mir Qasim formed **Triple Alliance** with Nawab Shuja-ud-Daulah of Awadh and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II.
  - Major **Hector Munro** commanded disciplined British force; decisively routed the allied Indian army at Buxar (Bihar).
- **Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765)**:
  - Clive secured **Diwani Rights** (revenue collection of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa) from Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II.
  - Emperor granted annual tribute of Rs. 26 lakhs and districts of Kara and Manikpur.
  - Nawab of Awadh forced to pay Rs. 50 lakh war indemnity; Awadh converted into a buffer state.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Siraj-ud-Daulah**: Last independent Nawab of Bengal; defeated at Plassey due to military betrayal by his generals.
- **Mir Jafar**: Commander who betrayed Siraj-ud-Daulah; dubbed "Clive's Jackal" for paying enormous personal bribes to Company officials.
- **Mir Qasim**: Competent administrator who attempted to modernize revenue and military systems before rebelling against Company exploitation.
- **Robert Clive**: Architect of British political supremacy in Bengal; instituted Dual Government (1765).
- **Jagat Seth (Mahtab Rai)**: Ultra-wealthy imperial banker of Bengal; funded British conspiracy to depose Siraj-ud-Daulah.

### 5. British Response
- **Establishment of Dual Government (1765–1772)**:
  - **Diwani** (fiscal and civil justice) held directly by the Company.
  - **Nizamat** (police, criminal justice, and civil administration) left to Nawab through Deputy Nazim.
  - Company wielded complete power without any administrative responsibility; Nawab held nominal responsibility without financial resources.
- **Abolition by Warren Hastings (1772)**:
  - Terminated Dual Government; dismissed Deputy Diwans Mohammad Reza Khan and Raja Shitab Rai; Company took direct governance.

### 6. Consequences
- **Economic Destruction & Drain of Wealth**:
  - Bengal revenues used to buy Indian silk and textiles ("investment") without importing bullion from Britain.
  - Artisans coerced into signing monopolistic supply contracts under Company agents (**Gumashtas**).
- **Catastrophic Bengal Famine (1770)**:
  - Over 10 million people (one-third of Bengal's population) perished while Company ruthlessly enforced full revenue collections.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Plassey vs. Buxar Distinction**:
  - Plassey was a skirmish won by treason and bribery; Buxar was a pitched military contest proving British operational superiority.
  - Transformed East India Company from a commercial mercantile body into the sovereign ruler of India's richest province.

# 3. Anglo-Mysore Wars: Resistance of Hyder Ali & Tipu Sultan (1767–1799)

```text
ANGLO-MYSORE WARS (1767–1799)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Rise of Mysore under Hyder Ali (1761) over Wodeyar dynasty
│   ├── Strategic location controlling Malabar spice trade and Deccan routes
│   └── Modernized artillery and French military alliance
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Territorial clashes over Northern Circars and Carnatic frontiers
│   ├── British dread of French-Mysorean military collaboration
│   └── Mysore's economic embargo on British pepper and sandalwood exports
│
├── MAIN EVENTS
│   ├── First War (1767–1769): Hyder Ali dictated Treaty of Madras
│   ├── Second War (1780–1784): Battle of Pollilur; Treaty of Mangalore
│   ├── Third War (1790–1792): Cornwallis's siege; Treaty of Seringapatam
│   └── Fourth War (1799): Fall of Seringapatam; heroic death of Tipu Sultan
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Hyder Ali, Tipu Sultan ("Tiger of Mysore")
│   └── Warren Hastings, Lord Cornwallis, Lord Wellesley, General Harris
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Forged triple alliances uniting British, Marathas, and the Nizam
│   ├── Systematic economic strangulation and territorial annexation
│   └── Imposed Subsidiary Alliance on restored infant Wodeyar Raja (1799)
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Partition of Mysore kingdom among British, Nizam, and Marathas
│   ├── Complete elimination of French influence from southern India
│   └── Total British hegemony established over South Indian peninsula
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Fiercest state resistance encountered by the British in 18th century
    └── Pioneered rocket artillery and modern state mercantilism in India
```

### 1. Background
- **Rise of Hyder Ali (1761)**: Rose from soldier to de facto ruler, eclipsing Prime Minister Nanjaraja and titular Wodeyar kings.
- **Strategic Power**: Established modern artillery arsenal at Dindigul with French technicians; built disciplined infantry and cavalry.
- **Maritime Hegemony**: Mysore controlled Malabar ports (Calicut, Cannanore, Mangalore), dominating rich spice, timber, and horse trades.
- **Deccan Geopolitics**: Mysore was locked in continuous tripartite frontier warfare with the Marathas and the Nizam of Hyderabad.

### 2. Causes
- **Political Causes**:
  - British feared Mysore's rapid expansion towards Carnatic frontiers and Madras borders.
  - Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan cultivated close military and diplomatic ties with Revolutionary France.
- **Economic Causes**:
  - Tipu banned export of sandalwood, pepper, and cardamom through Mysore ports, choking British trade monopolies.
  - Tipu established state commercial corporations, modern shipyards, and attempted direct trade with Oman and Ottoman Turkey.
- **Immediate Causes**:
  - First War: British joined offensive alliance with Nizam and Marathas against Hyder Ali (1766).
  - Second War: British captured French port of **Mahe** located within Hyder Ali's sovereign jurisdiction (1779).
  - Third War: Tipu attacked Raja of Travancore, a key British ally, over fortification disputes (1789).
  - Fourth War: Tipu planted "Tree of Liberty" at Seringapatam and joined the French Jacobin Club; Wellesley demanded unconditional submission.

### 3. Main Events
- **First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–1769)**:
  - Hyder Ali outmaneuvered British-Nizam coalition; detached Nizam through astute diplomacy.
  - Marched within 5 miles of Madras; forced British to sign humiliating defensive **Treaty of Madras (1769)**.
- **Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–1784)**:
  - Hyder captured Arcot and crushed Colonel Baillie's force at **Battle of Pollilur (1780)**.
  - Sir Eyre Coote checked Hyder at **Porto Novo (1781)**.
  - Hyder Ali died of cancer (1782); his son Tipu Sultan continued the war.
  - Concluded on terms of mutual restitution under **Treaty of Mangalore (1784)**.
- **Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–1792)**:
  - Lord Cornwallis allied with Marathas and Nizam; besieged Tipu inside capital Seringapatam.
  - **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)**: Tipu surrendered half his territory; paid Rs. 3.3 crore indemnity and surrendered two sons as hostages.
- **Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799)**:
  - Lord Wellesley launched coordinated double offensive under General Harris and General Stuart.
  - Seringapatam was breached on **4 May 1799**; Tipu Sultan died fighting heroically at the city water gate.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Hyder Ali**: Brilliant military strategist and diplomat; only Indian ruler to dictate a peace treaty to the British at Madras.
- **Tipu Sultan**: "Tiger of Mysore"; introduced solar calendar, new coinage, state trading corporation, and pioneer military Mysorean rockets.
- **Lord Cornwallis**: Governor-General who personally commanded campaigns in the Third Mysore War, crippling Tipu's strategic capacity.
- **Lord Wellesley**: Imperialist Governor-General who utilized Subsidiary Alliance system to wage total war against Tipu Sultan.

### 5. British Response
- **Coalition Warfare**: Consistently appeased and allied with Marathas and Nizam to isolate Mysore from Indian regional support.
- **Territorial Disruption**: Ceded northern Mysore territories to Nizam and Marathas while British seized strategic Kanara, Coimbatore, and Wayanad.
- **Restoration of Puppet Wodeyars**: Placed 5-year-old Krishnaraja III on Mysore throne under a suffocating **Subsidiary Alliance (1799)**.

### 6. Consequences
- **Mysore Partitioned**: Core territories became landlocked princely state controlled by British Resident; rest annexed directly by Madras Presidency.
- **French Eradication**: Decisively severed French diplomatic channel to India, removing Napoleon's prospective eastern invasion route.
- **Unchallenged Southern Dominion**: British secured uncontested territorial mastery across entire South Indian peninsula.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Modernized Resistance**: Tipu pioneered state-directed industrialism, military rocket artillery (**Mysorean rockets**), and independent naval docks.
- **Turning Point**: Elimination of Mysore removed the only southern Indian state capable of defeating European regular armies in open battle.

# 4. Anglo-Maratha Wars & Fall of the Maratha Confederacy (1775–1818)

```text
ANGLO-MARATHA WARS (1775–1818)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Third Battle of Panipat (1761) checked northward Maratha expansion
│   ├── Decentralized Maratha Confederacy: Peshwa, Scindia, Holkar, Gaekwad, Bhonsle
│   └── Succession crises in Poona court following Peshwa Madhavrao's death (1772)
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Political: British ambition to control Bombay hinterland and install puppet Peshwas
│   ├── Internal Feuds: Raghunath Rao (Raghoba) sought British military aid
│   └── Wellesley's Aggressive Imperialism: Enforcement of Subsidiary Alliance
│
├── MAIN EVENTS
│   ├── First War (1775–1782): Battle of Wadgaon; Treaty of Salbai (1782)
│   ├── Second War (1803–1805): Treaty of Bassein; Battles of Assaye and Laswari
│   └── Third War (1817–1818): Battles of Khadki, Koregaon; Pindari operations
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Nana Phadnavis ("Maratha Machiavelli"), Mahadji Scindia, Yashwantrao Holkar
│   ├── Peshwa Baji Rao II, Raghunath Rao
│   └── Warren Hastings, Lord Wellesley, Lord Hastings (Moira), Arthur Wellesley
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Signed conflicting treaties from Bombay and Calcutta Councils
│   ├── Imposed Subsidiary Alliance on Peshwa through Treaty of Bassein (1802)
│   └── Abolished the office of Peshwa; annexed core Maratha territories
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Dismantling of the Maratha Confederacy
│   ├── Peshwa Baji Rao II exiled to Bithur on annual pension
│   └── British East India Company became undisputed paramount power of India
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Ended the only Pan-Indian indigenous successor state to the Mughal Empire
    └── Established complete Pax Britannica across Central India, Deccan, and Gujarat
```

### 1. Background
- **Post-Panipat Rebound**: Mahadji Scindia and Peshwa Madhavrao quickly recovered Maratha power, restoring Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II to Delhi (1771).
- **Structure of Confederacy**: Autonomous regional warlords operated under nominal leadership of the Pune-based Peshwa:
  - **Peshwa** at Pune; **Scindia** at Gwalior; **Holkar** at Indore; **Bhonsle** at Nagpur; **Gaekwad** at Baroda.
- **Poona Court Factionalism**: Assassination of young Peshwa Narayanrao (1773) triggered bitter civil war between Raghunath Rao and Nana Phadnavis.
- **Bombay Presidency's Ambition**: British Bombay sought commercial revenue parity with wealthy Bengal through territorial conquests in Salsette and Bassein.

### 2. Causes
- **Political Causes**:
  - Raghunath Rao fled to Bombay British, signing treacherous **Treaty of Surat (1775)** to gain Peshwaship.
  - Nana Phadnavis rejected Treaty of Surat, backing infant posthumous Peshwa Madhavrao Narayan (Twelve Bhai Council).
- **Imperialistic Causes**:
  - Lord Wellesley's aggressive forward policy to eliminate Maratha neutrality and force them into Subsidiary Alliances.
- **Immediate Causes**:
  - First War: Bombay government dispatched military expedition to impose Raghunath Rao on Poona (1775).
  - Second War: Yashwantrao Holkar routed combined forces of Peshwa and Scindia at Hadapsar (1802); cowardly Baji Rao II signed **Treaty of Bassein**.
  - Third War: Lord Hastings encircled Maratha territories under pretext of suppressing roving **Pindari** freebooters (1817).

### 3. Main Events
- **First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782)**:
  - Marathas lured British army into Western Ghats passes; forced surrender at **Battle of Wadgaon (1779)**.
  - Warren Hastings dispatched Colonel Goddard (captured Ahmedabad) and Captain Popham (captured Gwalior Fort).
  - Mahadji Scindia negotiated **Treaty of Salbai (May 1782)**: Guaranteed 20 years of Anglo-Maratha peace; British retained Salsette.
- **Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–1805)**:
  - **Treaty of Bassein (1802)**: Peshwa Baji Rao II sold Maratha sovereignty for British protection.
  - Scindia and Bhonsle rejected treaty; launched war against British.
  - Major-General Arthur Wellesley crushed Scindia-Bhonsle forces at **Battle of Assaye (1803)**.
  - Lord Lake captured Delhi and Agra, winning **Battle of Laswari (1803)**.
  - Forced treaties: **Deogaon** (with Bhonsle) and **Surji-Anjangaon** (with Scindia).
- **Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1818)**:
  - Peshwa Baji Rao II made desperate bid for freedom; burned British Residency at Pune.
  - British defeated Peshwa at **Battle of Khadki** and **Battle of Koregaon (1818)**.
  - Scindia compelled to remain neutral by **Treaty of Gwalior (1817)**.
  - Holkar defeated at **Battle of Mahidpur (1817)**; signed **Treaty of Mandasor (1818)**.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Nana Phadnavis**: Chief Minister at Pune; held Maratha Confederacy together through astute diplomatic equilibrium until death (1800).
- **Mahadji Scindia**: Ruler of Gwalior; raised modernized infantry trained by French general Benoit de Boigne; controlled Delhi Emperor.
- **Peshwa Baji Rao II**: Incompetent and treacherous ruler whose egoistic infighting destroyed Maratha unity and invited British subjugation.
- **Arthur Wellesley (Duke of Wellington)**: Commanded southern army; perfected combined arms tactics at Battle of Assaye.
- **Lord Hastings (Marquess of Hastings)**: Governor-General who planned comprehensive encirclement and final liquidation of Maratha power.

### 5. British Response
- **Imposition of Subsidiary Alliances**: Disarmed each Maratha chief separately, stationing permanent British subsidiary forces at state capitals.
- **Abolition of the Peshwaship (1818)**: Liquidated hereditary office of Peshwa; exiled Baji Rao II to Bithur near Kanpur.
- **Creation of Satara State**: Created small princely state of Satara under Pratap Singh, a direct descendant of Chhatrapati Shivaji, to mollify sentiment.

### 6. Consequences
- **Annexation of Maharashtra**: Peshwa's dominions annexed directly into the expanding Bombay Presidency under Mountstuart Elphinstone.
- **Subjugation of Rajputana**: Rajput states liberated from Maratha chauth demands were immediately absorbed into British Subsidiary Alliances.
- **End of Maratha Hegemony**: Dissolved all interstate diplomacy among Indian rulers; British became undisputed sovereign overlord of India.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Loss of Last Indigenous Imperial Alternative**: Marathas were the only pan-Indian power capable of succeeding the Mughals; their defeat sealed British supremacy.
- **Institutional Failure**: Exposed fatal weaknesses of internal feudal rivalries, lack of unified command, and reliance on unassimilated mercenary forces.

# 5. Anglo-Sikh Wars & The Annexation of Punjab (1845–1849)

```text
ANGLO-SIKH WARS (1845–1849)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Unification of 12 Sikh Misls under Maharaja Ranjit Singh (1799–1839)
│   ├── Creation of Fauj-i-Khas (second-largest modern European-trained standing army in Asia)
│   └── Anarchy and rapid succession assassinations following Ranjit Singh's death (1839)
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── British expansionist appetite post First Anglo-Afghan War debacle
│   ├── Growing indiscipline and political dominance of the Sikh Khalsa Army (Panchayats)
│   └── Treachery of Court leaders (Lal Singh, Tej Singh, Gulab Singh) against Khalsa
│
├── MAIN EVENTS
│   ├── First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–1846): Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal, and Sobraon
│   ├── Treaty of Lahore & Treaty of Bhairowal (1846): British Council of Regency
│   ├── Multan Uprising by Governor Mulraj (1848)
│   └── Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848–1849): Chillianwala and Battle of Gujarat
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Maharaja Ranjit Singh, Maharani Jindan, Duleep Singh
│   ├── Diwan Mulraj, Chattar Singh & Sher Singh Attariwalla
│   └── Lord Hardinge, Lord Dalhousie, Sir Hugh Gough, Henry Lawrence
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── British forces crossed Sutlej; exploited internal treachery of Sikh commanders
│   ├── Stationed British Resident at Lahore with absolute governing authority
│   └── Lord Dalhousie declared total war and formal annexation of Punjab (1849)
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Complete annexation of Punjab; young Duleep Singh pensioned off to England
│   ├── Sale of Kashmir to Raja Gulab Singh of Jammu for Rs. 75 lakhs (Treaty of Amritsar)
│   └── Koh-i-Noor diamond surrendered to British Crown
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Eliminated the last major independent, sovereign sovereign state on the Indian subcontinent
    └── Advanced British Indian imperial frontiers directly to the Khyber Pass and Afghanistan
```

### 1. Background
- **Empire of Ranjit Singh (1799–1839)**: Captured Lahore (1799) and Amritsar (1802); united trans-Sutlej Punjab into a powerful sovereign empire.
- **Military Modernization**: Built **Fauj-i-Khas** and modern cannon foundries supervised by European mercenaries (Allard, Ventura, Court, Avitabile).
- **Treaty of Amritsar (1809)**: Accepted Sutlej River as perpetual eastern boundary with the British East India Company.
- **Post-Ranjit Chaos (1839–1845)**: Succession turbulence; Kharak Singh, Nau Nihal Singh, and Sher Singh assassinated; Khalsa army panchayats seized political power.

### 2. Causes
- **Political Causes**:
  - British sought to restore military prestige after disastrous annihilation in the First Anglo-Afghan War (1839–1842).
  - Fear of uncontrolled, radicalized Khalsa Army assembling large forces along the sensitive Sutlej frontier.
- **Internal Court Intrigue**:
  - Regent Maharani Jindan and Prime Minister Lal Singh feared the Khalsa Army; intentionally incited them against British to destroy their power.
- **Immediate Causes**:
  - First War: Sikh army crossed Sutlej River on 11 December 1845; British Governor-General Hardinge declared war.
  - Second War: Uprising of Diwan Mulraj at Multan (April 1848) and revolt of Chattar Singh and Sher Singh Attariwalla.

### 3. Main Events
- **First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–1846)**:
  - Pitched bloody battles fought at **Mudki**, **Ferozeshah**, **Aliwal**, and **Sobraon**.
  - Sikh soldiers fought with ferocious bravery but were systematically betrayed by commanders Lal Singh and Tej Singh.
  - **Battle of Sobraon (February 1846)**: Decisive slaughter; British crossed Sutlej and occupied Lahore.
- **Treaties of Lahore & Bhairowal (1846)**:
  - Sikh kingdom forced to cede Jalandhar Doab and pay Rs. 1.5 crore war indemnity.
  - **Treaty of Amritsar (1846)**: British sold Kashmir to Dogra chief Raja Gulab Singh for Rs. 75 lakhs.
  - **Treaty of Bhairowal (1846)**: British Resident Sir Henry Lawrence made virtual dictator of Punjab under Council of Regency.
- **Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848–1849)**:
  - Sparked by rebellion of Mulraj (Governor of Multan) who killed two British officers (Vans Agnew and Anderson).
  - General revolt spread across Punjab led by Sher Singh Attariwalla.
  - **Battle of Chillianwala (January 1849)**: Disastrous British setback; Sir Hugh Gough suffered catastrophic casualties.
  - **Battle of Gujarat (February 1849)**: "Battle of the Guns"; British heavy artillery routed the Sikh forces.
  - Sikh army surrendered at Rawalpindi on 14 March 1849.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Maharaja Ranjit Singh**: "Sher-e-Punjab"; master statesman who maintained complete communal harmony and built formidable secular empire.
- **Maharani Jindan**: Mother of infant Duleep Singh; courageous regent imprisoned and exiled by British to Benares.
- **Diwan Mulraj**: Patriotic Governor of Multan whose defiance triggered the Second Anglo-Sikh War.
- **Lord Dalhousie**: Governor-General; aggressive annexationist who annexed Punjab on 29 March 1849 without consulting London.
- **Sir Henry Lawrence & John Lawrence**: Governed Punjab through Administrative Board; established efficient colonial rule and pacification.

### 5. British Response
- **Annexation Proclamation**: Dalhousie annexed Punjab directly into British India (March 1849); dissolved the Lahore Durbar.
- **Board of Administration**: Established three-member governing board (Henry Lawrence, John Lawrence, Charles Mansel) to administer Punjab.
- **Total Disarmament**: Confiscated over 100,000 weapons from Sikh populace; disbanded regular Khalsa regiments.

### 6. Consequences
- **Imperial Frontier Realignment**: British borders extended directly to the Indus River and Afghan tribal zones.
- **Recruitment of Martial Races**: Disbanded Sikh warriors recruited into British Indian Army, playing decisive role in suppressing 1857 Revolt.
- **Exile of Duleep Singh**: Infant Maharaja stripped of titles, converted to Christianity, and exiled to Britain on meager pension.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **End of Independent Subcontinental States**: Marked final chapter of European military conquest of sovereign indigenous Indian states.
- **Creation of Strategic Buffer**: Punjab was transformed into the primary military rampart protecting the British Empire against Russian expansion (Great Game).

# 6. Early Civil, Peasant & Tribal Rebellions (1757–1856)

```text
EARLY CIVIL, PEASANT & TRIBAL REBELLIONS (1757–1856)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Destabilization of agrarian structures by colonial revenue demands
│   ├── Infiltration of moneylenders, traders, and British courts into tribal tracts
│   └── Dispossession of traditional zamindars, polygars, and religious mendicants
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Economic: Exorbitant land revenue, eviction, rack-renting, and usurious debt
│   ├── Administrative: Destruction of customary forest rights and police extortion
│   └── Socio-Cultural: Alien intrusion by outsiders (Dikus) and Christian missionary activity
│
├── MAIN EVENTS
│   ├── Sanyasi & Fakir Rebellion (1763–1800) in Bengal
│   ├── Paika Rebellion (1817) in Odisha under Bakshi Jagabandhu
│   ├── Kol Uprising (1831–1832) in Chhotanagpur
│   └── Santhal Hool (1855–1856) under Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu (Santhal leaders)
│   ├── Bakshi Jagabandhu (Paika commander)
│   ├── Buddho Bhagat (Kol leader)
│   └── Majnu Shah, Bhavani Pathak, Devi Chaudhurani (Sanyasi rebellion)
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Ruthless military suppression, martial law, and mass executions
│   ├── Creation of non-regulation tracts (Santhal Parganas Regulation 1855)
│   └── Passing of defensive tenancy and forest regulations to contain unrest
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Exposed inherent violence and illegitimacy of early colonial rule
│   ├── Segregation of tribal territories under special bureaucratic administration
│   └── Set tradition of militant subaltern resistance inspiring future movements
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Proved resistance to British rule was immediate, widespread, and sustained
    └── Highlighted the organic connection between peasant ruin and anti-colonial struggle
```

### 1. Background
- **Agrarian Rupture**: Introduction of colonial revenue auctions alienated hereditary zamindars, village headmen, and forest-dwelling communities.
- **Tribal Isolation Shattered**: British commercialized forest lands, bringing revenue contractors, timber merchants, and moneylenders (**Dikus**) into pristine tribal belts.
- **Demilitarization of Society**: Disbanding of feudal retainers, polygars, and royal militias created armed, unemployed populations ready for rebellion.

### 2. Causes
- **Economic Causes**:
  - Exorbitant land revenue assessments leading to widespread peasant evictions and land sales.
  - Usurious moneylending resulting in lifelong debt slavery (**Kamioti / Harvahi** systems).
- **Administrative Causes**:
  - British forest laws restricted customary rights to graze cattle, collect firewood, and practice shifting cultivation (**Jhum**).
  - Corrupt police, revenue peons, and complex British courts operated entirely in favor of wealthy exploiters.
- **Socio-Religious Causes**:
  - Resentment against European Christian missionaries interfering with indigenous animist and tribal cultural customs.

### 3. Main Events
- **Sanyasi and Fakir Rebellion (1763–1800, Bengal)**:
  - Famine of 1770 and British restrictions on holy pilgrimage sites provoked Hindu Sanyasis and Muslim Fakirs.
  - Leaders Majnu Shah, Chirag Ali, and Bhavani Pathak conducted guerrilla warfare against Company factories.
  - Immortalized in Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's famous patriotic novel **Anandamath** (source of *Vande Mataram*).
- **Paika Rebellion (1817, Odisha)**:
  - Paikas were the traditional hereditary landed militia of Gajapati rulers of Khurda.
  - Rebellion led by **Bakshi Jagabandhu Bidyadhar**; captured Puri, burning British police stations and courts.
- **Kol Uprising (1831–1832, Chhotanagpur)**:
  - Kol tribesmen (Ho, Munda, Oraon) revolted against transfer of ancestral lands to outsider Sikh and Muslim farmers.
  - Led by **Buddho Bhagat**; killed or expelled hundreds of outsiders before massive military suppression.
- **Santhal Hool / Rebellion (1855–1856, Rajmahal Hills)**:
  - Santhals settled in **Damin-i-Koh** revolted against rapacious extortion by Mahajans and corrupt railway contractors.
  - Brothers **Sidhu, Kanhu, Chand, and Bhairav Murmu** mobilized over 60,000 Santhals declaring sovereign independence.
  - Armed with bows and arrows, they besieged British stations; crushed only after brutal martial law and slaughter.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu**: Charismatic Santhal leaders who claimed divine revelation to end British and moneylender exploitation.
- **Bakshi Jagabandhu**: Former military commander of Khurda whose ancestral estate was fraudulently confiscated by Bengali revenue officials.
- **Buddho Bhagat**: Legendary Kol chieftain who died fighting British troops in battle alongside his family.
- **Devi Chaudhurani & Bhavani Pathak**: Legendary leaders of the North Bengal peasant and sanyasi uprisings against company extortion.

### 5. British Response
- **Savage Military Operations**: Deployed regular infantry and cavalry; torched hundreds of rebel villages and executed rebel ringleaders without trial.
- **Creation of Santhal Parganas (1855)**:
  - Carved out 5,500 square miles as non-regulation **Santhal Pargana district**; prohibited non-tribals from transferring tribal lands.
- **Administrative Concessions**: Pacified Paikas by reducing land revenue assessments and restoring confiscated Paika jagirs.

### 6. Consequences
- **Legal Protection of Tribal Lands**: Forced colonial state to enact special tenancy acts recognizing customary tribal land tenures.
- **Bureaucratic Segregation**: Enacted the **Scheduled Districts Act (1874)**, administering tribal areas through paternalistic executive officers rather than ordinary courts.
- **Infiltration Continued**: Despite legal bans, usurious credit networks gradually returned, laying roots for subsequent 20th-century tribal struggles.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Myth of Voluntary Submission Shattered**: Proved that colonial rule was established and maintained through systematic armed coercion and military terror.
- **Precursor to 1857**: Demonstrated grass-roots subaltern anger across central and eastern India, providing direct fuel for the 1857 conflagration.

# 7. The Great Revolt of 1857 (Sepoy Mutiny & First War of Independence)

```text
THE GREAT REVOLT OF 1857
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Century of colonial exploitation since Plassey (1757)
│   ├── Dalhousie's aggressive Doctrine of Lapse and annexation of Awadh (1856)
│   └── Growing racial arrogance and socio-religious anxiety across North India
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Political: Annexation of Awadh, abolition of royal titles, Nana Saheb's pension refused
│   ├── Economic: Ruin of artisans, destruction of peasantry, confiscation of Inam lands
│   ├── Social/Religious: General Service Enlistment Act, Lex Loci Act, fear of conversion
│   └── Immediate: Introduction of the greased Enfield rifle cartridge (tallow of cow/pig)
│
├── MAIN EVENTS
│   ├── Mangal Pandey's defiance at Barrackpore (29 March 1857)
│   ├── Mutiny at Meerut (10 May 1857) and capture of Delhi (11 May)
│   ├── Proclamation of Bahadur Shah Zafar as Shahenshah-e-Hindustan
│   └── Fierce sieges and fighting at Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi, Gwalior, and Arrah
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Bahadur Shah Zafar, General Bakht Khan (Delhi)
│   ├── Nana Saheb, Tatya Tope, Azimullah Khan (Kanpur)
│   ├── Begum Hazrat Mahal, Birjis Qadr (Lucknow)
│   ├── Rani Lakshmibai (Jhansi), Kunwar Singh (Arrah/Bihar)
│   └── British Generals: Colin Campbell, Hugh Rose, John Nicholson, James Outram
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Ruthless scorched-earth terror, mass hangings, and bombardment of cities
│   ├── Delhi recaptured (September 1857); royal princes murdered by William Hodson
│   └── Abolition of Company rule via Queen Victoria's Proclamation (November 1858)
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Government of India Act 1858: Direct governance transferred to British Crown
│   ├── Complete military reorganization under Peel Commission (Martial vs. Non-Martial races)
│   ├── Policy of Divide and Rule institutionalized; abandonment of social reforms
│   └── Princely states guaranteed territorial integrity as "breakwaters against storm"
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Marked the formal end of Mughal imperial sovereignty and Company rule
    └── Created a glorious national myth of united Hindu-Muslim resistance against imperialism
```

### 1. Background
- **Simmering Discontent**: A century of continuous economic extraction, rural displacement, and cultural alienation since Battle of Plassey (1757).
- **Dalhousie's Aggressive Annexations**:
  - Imposed **Doctrine of Lapse** on Satara (1848), Sambalpur (1849), Jhansi (1853), and Nagpur (1854).
  - Annexed **Awadh (1856)** on dubious pretext of "misgovernance," throwing thousands of court retainers and soldiers into unemployment.
- **The Bengal Army Composition**: Majority of sepoys recruited from high-caste Awadh Brahmins and Rajputs; Awadh was termed the "nursery of the Bengal Army."

### 2. Causes
- **Political Causes**:
  - Dalhousie announced succession to Bahadur Shah Zafar would be stripped of Red Fort residence and imperial title.
  - Discontinuation of annual pension to Peshwa Baji Rao II's adopted son, Nana Saheb.
- **Economic Causes**:
  - Permanent, Ryotwari, and Mahalwari settlements expropriated peasants; Inam Commission confiscated over 20,000 rent-free jagirs.
  - Destruction of traditional handicrafts left millions of handloom weavers destitute.
- **Social & Religious Causes**:
  - **Religious Disabilities Act (Lex Loci Act 1850)** allowed Hindu converts to Christianity to inherit ancestral property.
  - Abolition of Sati (1829) and Widow Remarriage Act (1856) viewed by orthodox masses as direct Christian assaults on faith.
- **Military Causes**:
  - **General Service Enlistment Act (1856)** mandated overseas service, violating high-caste Hindu taboos (*Kala Pani*).
  - Discrimination in promotions, poor allowances (*Batta*), and racial insults by British officers.
- **Immediate Cause**:
  - Introduction of the **Enfield P-53 rifle**: Required biting off the greased cartridge paper containing tallow from beef (sacred to Hindus) and pork (forbidden to Muslims).

### 3. Main Events
- **Spark at Barrackpore (29 March 1857)**:
  - Sepoy **Mangal Pandey** of 34th Bengal Native Infantry attacked British adjutant; executed on 8 April.
- **Outbreak at Meerut (10 May 1857)**:
  - Sepoys of 3rd Native Cavalry mutinied, killed European officers, released imprisoned comrades, and marched overnight to Delhi.
- **Seizure of Delhi (11–12 May 1857)**:
  - Captured Red Fort; proclaimed 82-year-old **Bahadur Shah Zafar** as *Shahenshah-e-Hindustan*.
  - Administrative council led by artillery subedar **General Bakht Khan**.
- **Major Centers of Uprising**:
  - **Kanpur**: Led by **Nana Saheb** assisted by brilliant general **Tatya Tope** and political advisor **Azimullah Khan**.
  - **Lucknow**: Led by **Begum Hazrat Mahal** and young son Birjis Qadr; besieged the British Residency for months.
  - **Jhansi & Gwalior**: **Rani Lakshmibai** led fierce resistance; allied with Tatya Tope, captured Gwalior Fort; died fighting in battle (June 1858).
  - **Bihar (Arrah/Jagdishpur)**: 80-year-old zamindar **Kunwar Singh** routed British forces across Bhojpur and Rohtas.
  - **Faizabad**: **Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah** defeated British forces under Henry Lawrence at Battle of Chinhat.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi**: Epitome of national valor; Sir Hugh Rose praised her as "the bravest and best military leader of the rebels."
- **Begum Hazrat Mahal**: Refused to surrender Awadh's sovereignty; led Lucknow struggle and eventually sought political refuge in Nepal.
- **Kunwar Singh**: Master guerrilla general of Bihar who defeated multiple British regiments despite losing his arm in battle.
- **General Colin Campbell**: British Commander-in-Chief who recaptured Kanpur, Lucknow, and pacified Rohilkhand.
- **Sir Hugh Rose**: British commander who suppressed Central Indian rebellion, capturing Jhansi, Kalpi, and Gwalior.

### 5. British Response
- **Reign of Terror**: General James Neill and Major Renaud enacted wholesale massacres, burning villages along the Grand Trunk Road.
- **Recapture of Delhi (September 1857)**:
  - British under John Nicholson breached Kashmiri Gate; Hodson shot Zafar's sons at point-blank range (*Khooni Darwaza*).
  - Bahadur Shah Zafar tried and exiled to Rangoon (Burma), where he died in 1862.
- **Reconquest of Strongholds**: Campbell captured Kanpur (December 1857) and Lucknow (March 1858); Tatya Tope captured and hanged (1859).

### 6. Consequences
- **Government of India Act 1858**:
  - Abolished East India Company; direct sovereignty vested in the British Crown.
  - Abolished Court of Directors and Board of Control; created office of **Secretary of State for India** assisted by 15-member Council.
  - Governor-General redesignated as **Viceroy** (Lord Canning became first Viceroy).
- **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 November 1858)**:
  - Read at Allahabad; promised religious non-interference, equal protection of law, and pardon to non-violent rebels.
  - Abandoned Doctrine of Lapse; confirmed princely rulers' right of adoption.
- **Military Reorganization (Peel Commission)**:
  - Decreased proportion of Indian troops; British-to-Indian ratio fixed at 1:2 in Bengal and 1:3 in Madras and Bombay.
  - Artillery, arsenals, and communications kept exclusively under European control.
  - Enforced policy of **Martial vs. Non-Martial races** (recruiting Sikhs, Gurkhas, Pathans; excluding Awadh/Bihar Brahmins).

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Watershed in Modern Indian History**: Marked irrevocable end of indigenous dynastic imperial claims and began the modern phase of Indian political evolution.
- **Birth of Communal Polarization Policy**: British consciously institutionalized "Divide and Rule," driving wedges between Hindus and Muslims to prevent united uprisings.

# 8. Socio-Religious Reform Movements (19th & Early 20th Century)

```text
SOCIO-RELIGIOUS REFORMS
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Colonial ideological critique accusing Indian society of stagnation and barbarism
│   ├── Spread of modern Western education, rationalism, and humanitarian ideas
│   └── Inward introspection by Indian intelligentsia against obscurantist social evils
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Inhumane practices: Sati, female infanticide, child marriage, untouchability
│   ├── Rigidity of caste system preventing national integration and social mobility
│   └── Aggressive proselytizing campaigns by Christian missionaries
│
├── MAIN MOVEMENTS & PROGRAMMES
│   ├── Reformist: Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh Movement
│   ├── Revivalist: Arya Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, Deoband Movement
│   └── Anti-Caste: Satyashodhak Samaj, Self-Respect Movement, SNDP Yogam
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, Swami Dayananda Saraswati
│   ├── Swami Vivekananda, Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, Jyotirao Phule, Sri Narayana Guru
│   └── Savitribai Phule, Pandita Ramabai, Kandukuri Veeresalingam, E.V.R. Periyar
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Early legislative reform: Abolition of Sati (1829), Widow Remarriage Act (1856)
│   ├── Age of Consent Act 1891, Child Marriage Restraint Act 1929 (Sarda Act)
│   └── Post-1857 withdrawal: Complete reluctance to touch orthodox social practices
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Significant legal and educational advancement for women and depressed classes
│   ├── Emergence of a modernized, self-confident national intelligentsia
│   └── Unintended revivalist communal friction between Hindu and Muslim identities
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Provided the essential intellectual and moral bedrock for Indian nationalism
    └── Transformed a fragmented, caste-ridden society into an aspiring democratic nation
```

### 1. Background
- **Colonial Intellectual Hegemony**: British rulers and missionaries depicted Indian society as degenerate, irrational, and incapable of self-rule.
- **Rise of Modern Intelligentsia**: Contact with Western enlightenment, rationalism, and liberalism prompted educated Indians to re-examine indigenous traditions.
- **Dual Character**:
  - **Reformist Movements**: Responded to modern Western challenges (Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh Movement).
  - **Revivalist Movements**: Sought inspiration from pristine ancient scriptures (Arya Samaj, Deoband Movement).

### 2. Causes
- **Social Evils**: Rampant subjugation of women through Sati, enforced widowhood, female infanticide, polygamy, and child marriage.
- **Caste Oppression**: Rigid varna hierarchy, untouchability, and denial of education and temple entry to lower castes.
- **Missionary Challenge**: Christian missionaries actively converted depressed classes by highlighting upper-caste tyranny and offering basic schooling.

### 3. Main Events & Movements
- **Brahmo Samaj (Founded 1828 by Raja Ram Mohan Roy)**:
  - Monotheistic reform; denounced polytheism, idol worship, priestcraft, and caste discrimination.
  - Succeeded by **Debendranath Tagore** (Tattvabodhini Sabha) and **Keshab Chandra Sen** (Brahmo Samaj of India).
- **Arya Samaj (Founded 1875 by Swami Dayananda Saraswati)**:
  - Slogan: **"Go Back to the Vedas"**; rejected Puranas, idol worship, child marriage, and hereditary caste.
  - Advocated caste based on merit (*Guna, Karma, Swabhava*); initiated **Shuddhi Movement** for reconversions.
  - Established extensive network of **Dayanand Anglo-Vedic (DAV)** schools and Gurukuls (Kangri).
- **Ramakrishna Mission (Founded 1897 by Swami Vivekananda)**:
  - Preached practical Vedanta, synthesis of spiritualism and modern science, and social service through education and famine relief.
  - Vivekananda electrified **World Parliament of Religions (Chicago, 1893)**, restoring pride in Indian civilization.
- **Aligarh Movement (Sir Syed Ahmad Khan)**:
  - Founded **Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College (1875)** at Aligarh; advocated modern scientific education for Indian Muslims.
  - Opposed blind religious adherence (*Taqlid*); interpreted Quran with rationalist principles.
- **Anti-Caste & Subaltern Reform Movements**:
  - **Satyashodhak Samaj (1873, Maharashtra)**: Founded by **Jyotirao Phule**; fought Brahminical hegemony and promoted education of lower castes and women.
  - **SNDP Yogam (1903, Kerala)**: Founded by **Sri Narayana Guru**; coined immortal slogan *"One Caste, One Religion, One God for All"*.
  - **Self-Respect Movement (1925, Tamil Nadu)**: Founded by **E.V. Ramasamy Naicker (Periyar)**; promoted rationalist, anti-Brahmin equality and non-priestly weddings.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Raja Ram Mohan Roy**: "Father of Modern Indian Renaissance"; ceaseless crusade led to **Abolition of Sati Act (1829)**.
- **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**: Reformer and Sanskrit scholar whose relentless campaigns secured **Hindu Widow Remarriage Act (1856)**.
- **Swami Dayananda Saraswati**: Author of *Satyarth Prakash*; founded Arya Samaj; first to proclaim *"India for Indians"*.
- **Jyotirao & Savitribai Phule**: Pioneered India's first school for girls and untouchables at Bhidewada (Pune, 1848).
- **Pandita Ramabai**: Founded **Sharada Sadan (1889)** in Bombay to educate and shelter young high-caste Hindu widows.

### 5. British Response
- **Pre-1857 Progressive Enactments**:
  - **Bengal Sati Regulation (1829)** under Lord William Bentinck.
  - **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (1856)** under Lord Dalhousie.
- **Post-1857 Conservative Retreat**:
  - Proclamation of 1858 pledged strict non-interference in Indian social and religious customs.
  - British resisted further reforms unless forced by sustained nationalist agitation (e.g., **Age of Consent Act 1891**, **Sarda Act 1929**).

### 6. Consequences
- **Female Emancipation**: Rapid increase in female literacy, legal ban on child marriages, and slow opening of professions to women.
- **Anti-Untouchability Consciousness**: Paved ideological way for Mahatma Gandhi's Harijan campaign and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's Dalit emancipatory movement.
- **Communal Fallout**: Revivalist movements (Shuddhi, Sangathan, Tabligh, Tanzim) widened theological divides, fostering future communal competition.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Genesis of National Consciousness**: By asserting human dignity, social equality, and critical questioning, reformers created the modern, secular Indian mind.
- **Integration of Social & Political Struggles**: Established foundational principle that political freedom without comprehensive social emancipation was meaningless.

# 9. Colonial Economic Policies, Land Revenue Systems & Drain of Wealth

```text
COLONIAL ECONOMIC POLICIES
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Transformation of Britain into first industrial factory of the world
│   ├── Subordination of Indian economy to British imperial industrial requirements
│   └── Systematic transition from mercantile extraction to industrial exploitation
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Need for raw materials (cotton, jute, indigo, tea) to feed British mills
│   ├── Need for captive colonial consumer market for British manufactured exports
│   └── Need for safe overseas investment opportunities for British financial capital
│
├── MAIN COMPONENTS
│   ├── Land Revenue: Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari System, Mahalwari System
│   ├── Commercialization of Agriculture: Forced cultivation of non-food cash crops
│   ├── De-industrialization: Ruin of traditional urban and rural textile handicrafts
│   └── Drain of Wealth: Unilateral transfer of Indian surpluses to Great Britain
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Lord Cornwallis (Permanent Settlement 1793), Thomas Munro (Ryotwari 1820)
│   ├── Holt Mackenzie (Mahalwari 1822), Mountstuart Elphinstone
│   └── Economic Critics: Dadabhai Naoroji, Romesh Chunder Dutt, M.G. Ranade
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Imposition of one-way free trade (heavy duties on Indian imports in Britain; zero tariffs on British goods in India)
│   ├── Complete denial of economic drain; defended home charges as payment for good governance
│   └── Creation of Famine Commissions (Strachey, Lyall, MacDonnell) offering cosmetic relief
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Pauperization of peasantry, rural indebtedness, and rise of absentee landlordism
│   ├── Frequent catastrophic famines killing over 30 million people in 19th century
│   └── Stagnation of Indian industrial development and complete economic backwardness
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Economic critique destroyed ideological claim of British "civilizing mission"
    └── United diverse Indian classes against common colonial economic subjugator
```

### 1. Background
- **Stages of British Colonialism (R.P. Dutt's Model)**:
  - **Stage 1: Mercantile Capitalism (1757–1813)**: Direct plunder, monopolistic trade, extraction of revenue to buy Indian goods.
  - **Stage 2: Industrial Capitalism (1813–1858)**: India converted into raw material exporter and captive consumer market for Manchester textiles.
  - **Stage 3: Financial Capitalism (Post-1860s)**: British finance capital invested in guaranteed Indian railways, irrigation, and plantations.

### 2. Causes
- **Industrial Revolution in Britain**: Mechanized mills in Lancashire required guaranteed cheap raw cotton and vast markets for mass-produced yarn.
- **Colonial Self-Funding**: Imperial conquest and civil administration were funded entirely through extortionate land revenue extracted from Indian peasantry.

### 3. Main Components & Policies
- **Permanent Settlement (1793, Bengal, Bihar, Orissa)**:
  - Introduced by **Lord Cornwallis** on advice of Sir John Shore.
  - Zamindars recognized as permanent owners of land; state demand fixed permanently at **10/11ths** for Company, 1/11th for Zamindar.
  - Rigid **Sunset Law**: If revenue was not deposited by sunset of fixed date, zamindari was auctioned off.
- **Ryotwari System (1820, Madras, Bombay, Assam)**:
  - Devised by **Thomas Munro** and **Captain Alexander Read**.
  - Direct settlement between colonial state and peasant cultivator (**Ryot**); revenue reassessed periodically (every 20–30 years).
  - Revenue demands set ruinously high (often 50% to 60% of gross produce), leading to moneylender subjugation.
- **Mahalwari System (1822, Ganga Valley, NWFP, Central India, Punjab)**:
  - Devised by **Holt Mackenzie**; modified by Robert Merttins Bird (1833).
  - Revenue settlement made with village community (**Mahal**) through village headman (**Lambardar**).
- **De-industrialization & Ruin of Handicrafts**:
  - British abolished protective tariffs in India while slapping heavy duties (up to 80%) on Indian handloom textiles in Britain.
  - Ruined legendary weaving towns (Dhaka, Murshidabad, Surat); artisan millions thrown back onto overburdened agriculture.
- **Commercialization of Agriculture**:
  - Peasants forced into cultivating cash crops (**Indigo, Opium, Jute, Cotton, Tea**) to pay land revenue in cash.
  - Reduced area under staple food grains, setting stage for recurrent catastrophic famines.
- **The Drain of Wealth**:
  - Systematic transfer of Indian financial surplus to Britain without any equivalent economic or material return.
  - Comprised **Home Charges** (pensions, salaries of British civil/military officers in London, guaranteed 5% railway interest, India Office expenses).

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Dadabhai Naoroji**: "Grand Old Man of India"; authored *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901)*; mathematically proved the **Drain Theory**.
- **Romesh Chunder Dutt (R.C. Dutt)**: Authored *The Economic History of India*; exposed how land settlements systematically caused rural famines.
- **Justice Mahadev Govind Ranade**: Argued for state-supported industrialization and protectionist trade tariffs to build domestic Indian industries.
- **Lord Cornwallis**: Instituted Permanent Settlement, creating a loyal, conservative class of landed zamindars tied to colonial survival.
- **Sir Thomas Munro**: Championed Ryotwari system, believing it preserved ancient Indian landowning peasant traditions.

### 5. British Response
- **One-Way Free Trade**: Denied tariff protection to fledgling Indian industries while flooding Indian markets with British imports.
- **Ideological Defense**: Colonial apologists (Strachey, Curzon) claimed "Home Charges" were reasonable payments for "peace, order, and good governance."
- **Famine Commissions**:
  - **Strachey Commission (1880)**: Framed the **Famine Code**; recommended creation of Famine Insurance Fund.
  - **MacDonnell Commission (1901)**: Appointed by Curzon; recommended moral strategy of non-official assistance and agricultural banks.

### 6. Consequences
- **Peasant Indebtedness & Rural Proletarianization**:
  - Millions of peasants lost land titles to moneylenders, becoming bonded agricultural day-laborers.
- **Famines of Unprecedented Scale**:
  - Terrible famines: 1866 (Orissa), 1876–1878 (Great Deccan Famine), 1896–1897, 1899–1900; killed tens of millions due to bureaucratic callousness.
- **Stunted Industrial Growth**:
  - Modern Indian-owned industries (cotton in Bombay/Ahmedabad, steel at Jamshedpur) developed only in spite of colonial obstruction.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Bedrock of Anti-Colonial Nationalism**: The economic critique conclusively proved that India's poverty was not natural, but manufactured by British colonial exploitation.
- **Unified National Grievance**: United peasants, artisans, and educated urban classes under one shared goal of achieving economic and political sovereignty.

# 10. Early Nationalist Phase & The Moderate Congress (1885–1905)

```text
EARLY NATIONALIST PHASE & MODERATES (1885–1905)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Pre-Congress political associations (Indian Association, Poona Sarvajanik Sabha)
│   ├── Repressive policies of Lord Lytton (Vernacular Press Act, Arms Act 1878)
│   ├── Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883) exposing deep British white racial chauvinism
│   └── Formation of Indian National Congress (December 1885, Bombay)
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Growth of modern all-India national consciousness through press and railways
│   ├── Discontent among educated Indian youth denied higher administrative appointments
│   └── Need for an all-India political platform to mobilize public opinion
│
├── MAIN PROGRAMMES & METHODS
│   ├── The 3 P's Method: Prayers, Petitions, and Protests
│   ├── Legislative Demands: Expansion of legislative councils, control over budget
│   ├── Administrative Demands: Simultaneous ICS exams in India, separation of judiciary
│   └── Economic Demands: Reduction of military expenditure, abolition of salt tax
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── W.C. Bonnerjee (first INC President), A.O. Hume (Founder/General Secretary)
│   ├── Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta
│   └── Surendranath Banerjea, Dinshaw Wacha, Badruddin Tyabji, Anandamohan Bose
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Initial benevolent neutrality under Lord Dufferin
│   ├── Rapid hostility: Dufferin ridiculed Congress as a "microscopic minority" (1887)
│   ├── Official ban on government servants attending Congress sessions
│   └── Indian Councils Act of 1892 granting minimal, non-official representation
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Political education of Indian masses and inculcation of common national identity
│   ├── Systematic demolition of the moral and economic legitimacy of colonial rule
│   └── Disillusionment of younger generation leading to rise of Extremist nationalism
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Laid the indispensable institutional, ideological, and constitutional foundations of INC
    └── Successfully transformed isolated regional grievances into an organized all-India struggle
```

### 1. Background
- **Pre-Congress Political Bodies**:
  - **Bangabhasha Prakasika Sabha (1836)**: Earliest organized political association.
  - **British Indian Association (1851)**: Zamindari interests; petitioned British Parliament.
  - **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870)**: Led by M.G. Ranade and G.V. Joshi; mediated between government and ryots.
  - **Indian Association of Calcutta (1876)**: Founded by **Surendranath Banerjea** and **Anandamohan Bose**; mobilized all-India campaigns.
- **Catalysts for National Organization**:
  - **Lord Lytton's Reactionary Regime (1876–1880)**: Vernacular Press Act (1878), Arms Act (1878), Imperial Durbar during terrible famine.
  - **Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883)**: European community violently opposed bill allowing Indian judges to try European offenders; exposed deep colonial racism.
- **Foundation of Indian National Congress (1885)**:
  - Founded at **Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay** (28–31 December 1885).
  - Initiated by retired British ICS officer **Allan Octavian Hume**; presided over by **W.C. Bonnerjee**; attended by 72 delegates.

### 2. Causes
- **Rise of All-India National Consciousness**: Western education, modern newspapers, postal networks, and railways fostered interconnected national political sentiment.
- **Exclusion from Administration**: Frustration of educated Indian youth barred from higher bureaucratic posts through age-limit reductions in Civil Services.
- **Need for Democratic Platform**: Need to weld disparate ethnic, linguistic, and religious groups into a unified national political movement.

### 3. Main Programmes & Methods
- **Methodology of Agitation**:
  - Total faith in British sense of justice, fair play, and constitutional legality.
  - Relied strictly on constitutional agitation: **Prayers, Petitions, Memorials, and Protests** (**3 P's**).
  - Dispatched deputations of Indian leaders to Great Britain to educate British public opinion.
- **Core Nationalist Demands**:
  - **Constitutional**: Expansion of Imperial and Provincial Legislative Councils; genuine Indian representation; control over imperial budget.
  - **Administrative**: Holding simultaneous **Indian Civil Service (ICS)** examinations in India and England; raising maximum age limit.
  - **Judicial & Civil Rights**: Complete separation of judiciary from executive; repeal of Arms Act; freedom of speech and press.
  - **Economic**: Substantial reduction in land revenue; abolition of salt tax; reduction of bloated military expenditures; protective industrial tariffs.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Dadabhai Naoroji**: Elected INC President three times (1886, 1893, 1906); established British Committee of the Congress in London (1889).
- **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**: Political guru of Mahatma Gandhi; founded **Servants of India Society (1905)**; master parliamentarian in Imperial Council.
- **Surendranath Banerjea**: "Indian Burke"; convened Indian National Conference (1883); author of *A Nation in Making*.
- **Pherozeshah Mehta**: "Lion of Bombay"; defended civic autonomy and guided Congress constitution with uncompromising Moderate discipline.
- **Badruddin Tyabji**: First Muslim President of the Indian National Congress (Madras Session, 1887); championed secular national unity.

### 5. British Response
- **Initial Neutrality to Hostility**:
  - Lord Dufferin initially hosted tea party for delegates (1886); soon dismissed INC as a **"microscopic minority" (1887)**.
  - Lord Curzon declared (1900): *"Congress is tottering to its fall, and my ambition is to assist it to a peaceful demise."*
- **Legislative Concession (Indian Councils Act 1892)**:
  - Marginally increased non-official members in councils; allowed discussion on budget but denied right to vote or ask supplementary questions.
- **Encouragement of Counter-Forces**:
  - British officials actively encouraged Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and Raja Shiva Prasad of Benares to establish the **United Indian Patriotic Association (1888)** to oppose Congress.

### 6. Consequences
- **Politicization of Middle Class**: Succeeded in awakening political awareness among urban educated professionals across all Indian provinces.
- **Exposed Colonial Exploitation**: The economic drain theory created an indestructible critique of British rule that discredited British paternalism forever.
- **Limitations & Disillusionment**:
  - Failed to mobilize rural peasantry, industrial workers, or women; movement remained an elite, urban, bourgeois phenomenon.
  - Failure of "political mendicancy" to yield tangible concessions triggered the rise of Militant / Extremist Nationalism (Lal-Bal-Pal).

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Foundational Crucible of Modern India**: Created the ideological vocabulary, democratic ethos, and organizational framework of the Indian National Congress.
- **Seeds of Swaraj**: Though Moderates demanded colonial self-government within the British Empire, their groundwork made subsequent radical mass movements possible.

# 11. Swadeshi & Boycott Movement (1903–1908) & The Surat Split (1907)

```text
SWADESHI & BOYCOTT MOVEMENT (1903–1908)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Lord Curzon's imperialist policies (Calcutta Corporation Act, Indian Universities Act)
│   ├── Official scheme to partition Bengal into two administrative provinces
│   └── Formal announcement of Partition on 19 July 1905; implemented 16 October 1905
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Political: Divide Bengali linguistic and political unity; create Muslim-majority province
│   ├── Ideological: Challenge to British prestige and rising militant nationalist sentiment
│   └── Economic: Impoverishment of domestic industries under unrestricted British imports
│
├── MAIN PROGRAMMES & EVENTS
│   ├── Boycott of foreign Manchester cloth, Liverpool salt, and British goods
│   ├── Promotion of Swadeshi industries, indigenous education (National Council of Education)
│   ├── Mass mourning on 16 October 1905 (Raksha Bandhan, Arandhan, bathing in Ganga)
│   ├── Social Participation: Students, women, urban middle class, Samitis (Swadesh Bandhab)
│   └── Surat Split (1907): Ideological rupture between Moderates and Extremists
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Lal-Bal-Pal: Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal
│   ├── Sri Aurobindo Ghosh, Rabindranath Tagore, Ashwini Kumar Dutt
│   └── Moderates: Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Surendranath Banerjea, Rashbehari Ghosh
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Carlyle Circular banning student political participation; police lathi charges
│   ├── Seditious Meetings Act (1907), Criminal Law Amendment Act (1908)
│   ├── Deportation of Lala Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh; Tilak sentenced to 6 years in Mandalay
│   └── Annulment of Partition (1911) and shifting of imperial capital to Delhi
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Paralysis of the Indian National Congress following the 1907 Surat Split
│   ├── Stimulated domestic indigenous industries (Bengal Chemicals, Tata Steel)
│   └── Rise of underground revolutionary terrorism among frustrated youth
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── First genuine mass movement of modern India transcending constitutional mendicancy
    └── Introduced modern political techniques: mass boycotts, passive resistance, national schools
```

### 1. Background
- **Lord Curzon's Reactionary Measures**:
  - **Calcutta Corporation Act (1899)**: Reduced elected Indian members, making it European-dominated.
  - **Indian Universities Act (1904)**: Placed universities under tight government official control.
- **Scheme to Partition Bengal**:
  - Official pretext: Bengal (population 78 million) was too unwieldy for a single Lieutenant-Governor to administer.
  - Real imperial design: Bifurcate politically vocal Bengali intelligentsia and create a Muslim-majority Eastern Bengal province.
- **Announcement**: Partition formally declared on **19 July 1905**; took effect on **16 October 1905**.

### 2. Causes
- **Political Causes**:
  - Conscious British tactic to curb Bengal's emerging nationalist nerve center and divide Bengalis by religion and language.
  - Creation of communal cleavage: Assured Muslims that East Bengal would revive historic privileges of Muslim rule.
- **Ideological Causes**:
  - Failure of Moderate petitioning tactics; growing popularity of Extremist leaders advocating self-reliance (**Atmashakti**).
- **Immediate Causes**:
  - Disregard of thousands of protest petitions, press pamphlets (*Sanjivani*, *Hitavadi*), and mammoth public meetings against partition.

### 3. Main Programmes & Events
- **Proclamation of Swadeshi Movement (7 August 1905)**:
  - Passed historic Boycott Resolution at Calcutta Town Hall; movement spread across India.
- **Day of Partition (16 October 1905)**:
  - Observed as day of national mourning; fasting (*Arandhan*) and bare-foot processions singing *Vande Mataram*.
  - **Rabindranath Tagore** suggested **Raksha Bandhan** as a symbol of unbreakable brotherhood between East and West Bengal.
- **Programme of Boycott & Swadeshi**:
  - Boycott of Manchester textiles, foreign sugar, cigarettes, and Liverpool salt; public bonfires of foreign cloth.
  - Social boycott of consumers, shopkeepers, and washermen using or dealing in British goods.
  - Promotion of indigenous enterprises: **Bengal Chemical Swadeshi Stores** (P.C. Ray), swadeshi banks, and textile mills.
- **National Education**:
  - Established **National Council of Education (August 1906)** and Bengal National College with **Aurobindo Ghosh** as principal.
- **Social Participation**:
  - **Students**: Forefront of picketing and distribution of pamphlets; defied government warnings.
  - **Women**: Stepped out of domestic seclusion for the first time in large numbers to picket foreign shops.
  - **Corps of Volunteers (Samitis)**: **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti** of Ashwini Kumar Dutt mobilized thousands of rural villagers.
  - **Limitations**: Muslim peasantry largely stayed away, alienated by Hindu revivalist idioms (Ganga bath, Kali worship).
- **The Surat Split (December 1907)**:
  - Extremists (Tilak) demanded extending Swadeshi and Boycott to all India and expanding it to full passive resistance.
  - Moderates (Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta) insisted on restricting movement to Bengal and foreign goods boycott only.
  - At Surat session (presided by Rashbehari Ghosh), violent pandemonium broke out; Congress split irrevocably.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**: "Lokmanya"; popularized Shivaji and Ganapati festivals; declared: *"Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it."*
- **Bipin Chandra Pal & Lala Lajpat Rai**: Extended Swadeshi campaign into Punjab and nationalized radical political rhetoric.
- **Sri Aurobindo Ghosh**: Propounded comprehensive doctrine of **Passive Resistance** through editorial columns of *Bande Mataram*.
- **Rabindranath Tagore**: Composed immortal patriotic anthem *Amar Sonar Bangla* (later national anthem of Bangladesh).
- **Ashwini Kumar Dutt**: Master organizer of Barisal whose Swadesh Bandhab Samiti had 159 rural branches.

### 5. British Response
- **Ruthless Repression**:
  - **Carlyle Circular (October 1905)**: Threatened to withdraw grants and affiliations from educational institutions whose students participated.
  - Police lathi charges on peaceful Barisal Conference delegates (1906); public shouting of *Vande Mataram* banned.
- **Repressive Enactments**:
  - Seditious Meetings Act (1907), Explosive Substances Act (1908), Indian Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act (1908).
- **Arrests & Deportations**:
  - Tilak tried for sedition and imprisoned in **Mandalay (Burma)** for 6 years (1908–1914).
  - Aurobindo Ghosh arrested in Alipore Conspiracy Case, subsequently retired to Pondicherry for spiritual pursuits.
- **Annulment of Partition (1911)**:
  - Lord Hardinge annulled Partition of Bengal at Delhi Durbar (1911); reunited Bengal but separated Bihar and Orissa.
  - Shifted imperial capital of British India from **Calcutta to Delhi (1911)**.

### 6. Consequences
- **Collapse of Movement by 1908**: Severe repression, arrest of top leaders, internal split at Surat, and lack of institutional structure.
- **Congress Ineffective**: Moderate-dominated rump Congress lost all mass support and vitality until reunion in 1916.
- **Direct Impetus to Revolutionary Terrorism**: Frustrated youth took to pistols and bombs to avenge brutal police crackdowns.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Leap into Modern Mass Politics**: First movement where women and students participated actively on an unprecedented public scale.
- **Cultural Renaissance**: Flourishing of vernacular literature, nationalistic painting (Abanindranath Tagore's *Bharat Mata*), and scientific research (J.C. Bose).

# 12. Revolutionary Nationalism: Phase I (1907–1917)

```text
REVOLUTIONARY NATIONALISM: PHASE I (1907–1917)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Brutal suppression and fizzling out of the open Swadeshi Movement
│   ├── Failure of Moderate constitutionalism and inaction after Surat Split (1907)
│   └── Inspiration from Irish Fenians, Russian Nihilists, and Italian Carbonari
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Burning desire to avenge colonial state violence and police brutality
│   ├── Disillusionment with elderly Congress leadership's compromises
│   └── Objective of striking terror into British officials to paralyze administration
│
├── MAIN ORGANISATIONS & ACTIONS
│   ├── Bengal: Anushilan Samiti, Yugantar (Alipore Bomb Case, Barrah Dacoity)
│   ├── Maharashtra: Abhinav Bharat (V.D. Savarkar), assassination of Curzon Wyllie
│   ├── Punjab: Anjuman-i-Mohisban-i-Watan (Ajit Singh), Delhi Conspiracy Case 1912
│   ├── Abroad: India House (London), Ghadar Party (USA/Canada), Berlin Committee
│   └── Komagata Maru Incident (1914) and planned 1915 Armed Ghadar Mutiny
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Barindra Ghosh, Bhupendranath Dutta, Khudiram Bose, Prafulla Chaki
│   ├── V.D. Savarkar, Madan Lal Dhingra, Rash Behari Bose, Sachin Sanyal
│   └── Ghadar Leaders: Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, Kartar Singh Sarabha
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Defence of India Act 1915 (summary trials by special tribunals)
│   ├── Mass hanging of Ghadarites (Lahore Conspiracy Trials)
│   └── Crushing of Bagha Jatin at Battle of Balasore (1915)
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Severe depletion of revolutionary cadres due to executions and internments
│   ├── Kept national spirit alive during political lull of World War I
│   └── Forced British into contemplating constitutional concessions (Montagu Declaration 1917)
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Inculcated supreme self-sacrifice, heroism, and intense patriotism among Indian youth
    └── Demonstrated that British rule could be directly assaulted with armed resistance
```

### 1. Background
- **Post-Swadeshi Void**: Brutal police crackdowns and the Surat Split left no legal, constitutional outlet for militant nationalist youth.
- **Philosophical Underpinnings**:
  - Believed individual heroic assassinations of oppressive British officials would awaken the dormant masses and shake colonial morale.
  - Influenced by the Bhagavad Gita's call for righteous duty, Bankim's *Anandamath*, and revolutionary texts like *Bhavani Mandir*.

### 2. Causes
- **Immediate State Violence**: Lathi charges on student picketers, flogging of patriots, and muzzling of the national vernacular press.
- **Racial Injustice**: Blatant bias in colonial courts, where European murderers of Indians consistently received token fines or acquittals.
- **International Spark**: Defeat of Tsarist Russia by Asian Japan (1905) proved European military might was not invincible.

### 3. Main Organisations & Actions
- **Bengal Hub**:
  - **Anushilan Samiti (1902)**: Founded by Pramatha Nath Mitra, Jatindranath Banerjee, and Barindra Kumar Ghosh.
  - **Yugantar Newspaper (1906)**: Founded by Barindra Ghosh and Bhupendranath Dutta; preached open armed rebellion.
  - **Muzaffarpur Bomb Outrage (1908)**: **Khudiram Bose** and **Prafulla Chaki** threw bomb at carriage of tyrannical judge Kingsford (killed two British women).
  - Prafulla Chaki shot himself; 18-year-old Khudiram Bose was hanged (became immortal folk martyr).
  - **Alipore Conspiracy Case (1908)**: Police raided Manicktala bomb factory; Aurobindo Ghosh brilliantly defended and acquitted by **C.R. Das**.
  - **Howrah & Barrah Gang Cases**: Led by legendary revolutionary **Bagha Jatin (Jatindranath Mukherjee)**.
- **Maharashtra Hub**:
  - **Mitra Mela / Abhinav Bharat Society (1904)**: Founded by **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar** and his brother Ganesh Savarkar.
  - Anant Kanhere shot dead A.M.T. Jackson, Collector of Nasik (1909), in retaliation for Babarao Savarkar's deportation.
- **Delhi Conspiracy Case (1912)**:
  - **Rash Behari Bose** and **Sachin Sanyal** threw a bomb at Viceroy Lord Hardinge during state imperial procession in Chandni Chowk (Delhi).
  - Hardinge was injured; Rash Behari Bose evaded capture and subsequently escaped to Japan.
- **Revolutionary Centers Abroad**:
  - **India House (London)**: Established by **Shyamji Krishna Varma** (1905); brought out *The Indian Sociologist*.
  - **Madan Lal Dhingra** assassinated Sir William Curzon Wyllie in London (1909); faced the gallows with fearless serenity.
  - **Paris & Geneva Centers**: Led by **Madam Bhikaji Cama**; unfurled first Indian National Flag at Stuttgart Socialist Congress (Germany, 1907).
- **The Ghadar Movement (1913–1915, North America)**:
  - Founded in San Francisco by **Lala Har Dayal**, **Sohan Singh Bhakna**, and **Taraknath Das**; published weekly journal *The Ghadar*.
  - Objective: Wage an armed mutiny across Indian garrisons during Britain's preoccupation with World War I.
  - **Komagata Maru Incident (1914)**: Japanese ship carrying 376 prospective Indian immigrants under **Gurdit Singh** turned away by Canada; fired upon at Budge Budge (Calcutta).
  - Planned pan-Indian army mutiny for **21 February 1915**; betrayed by British spy Kirpal Singh; hundreds executed in **Lahore Conspiracy Trials**.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Rash Behari Bose**: Mastermind of Delhi bomb plot (1912) and Ghadar uprisings; later founded Indian Independence League in Tokyo.
- **Bagha Jatin**: Hero of Jugantar; fought pitched battle against armed British police with pistols at **Chashakhand (Balasore, 1915)**.
- **Lala Har Dayal**: Brilliant intellectual who galvanized Punjabi immigrants on Pacific Coast into a fearless revolutionary force.
- **Kartar Singh Sarabha**: 19-year-old heroic Ghadar organizer hanged in Lahore; lifelong idol and inspiration of Bhagat Singh.
- **V.D. Savarkar**: Penned *The Indian War of Independence of 1857*; orchestrated supply of Browning automatic pistols to Indian revolutionaries.

### 5. British Response
- **Draconian Legislation**:
  - Enacted **Defence of India Act (1915)**; created non-appealable summary special military tribunals.
  - Incarcerated over 7,000 revolutionaries and sympathizers without formal trial in Punjab and Bengal.
- **Brutal Executions**:
  - Executed over 40 Ghadar leaders and awarded life sentences in Cellular Jail (Andamans) to hundreds of young patriots.

### 6. Consequences
- **Decapitation of Revolutionary Cadres**: The movement collapsed under ruthless police espionage, arms shortages, and lack of mass participation.
- **Exposed Secret Spy Networks**: British CID thoroughly penetrated underground groups through paid informants and intercepted couriers.
- **Paved Way for Gandhi**: Failure of individual violence convinced Indian youth of the necessity of organized mass mobilization.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Unflinching Spirit of Martyrdom**: Raised patriotism to spiritual heights; proved Indians were ready to face the gallows for national freedom.
- **Secular & Internationalist Character**: The Ghadar movement united Sikh, Hindu, and Muslim immigrants on an uncompromisingly secular socialist platform.

# 13. Home Rule League Movement & The Lucknow Pact (1916)

```text
HOME RULE LEAGUE & LUCKNOW PACT (1916)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Political vacuum in India during World War I
│   ├── Release of Bal Gangadhar Tilak from Mandalay Prison (1914)
│   ├── Arrival of Irish Theosophist Annie Besant into Indian national politics
│   └── Re-entry of Extremists into the Indian National Congress
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── War-induced miseries: Skyrocketing prices, war loans, and heavy taxes
│   ├── Global resonance of Irish Home Rule agitation against British rule
│   └── Need for continuous year-round political campaigning instead of 3-day sessions
│
├── MAIN PROGRAMMES & EVENTS
│   ├── Tilak's League (April 1916): Covered Maharashtra (excl. Bombay), Karnataka, CP, Berar
│   ├── Besant's League (September 1916): Covered rest of India including Bombay city
│   ├── Propaganda via press: Kesari, Mahratta, New India, Commonweal
│   └── Lucknow Session (1916): Historic reunion of Moderates-Extremists & Lucknow Pact
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Annie Besant, Mohammad Ali Jinnah
│   ├── Motilal Nehru, Jawaharlal Nehru, Subrahmanya Aiyer, Shankarlal Banker
│   └── Ambica Charan Mazumdar (INC President at Lucknow, 1916)
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Banned student participation; interned Annie Besant, George Arundale, B.P. Wadia (1917)
│   ├── Sir S. Subramanya Aiyer renounced knighthood in protest
│   └── Montagu Declaration (August 1917): Promised gradual development of self-governing institutions
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Massive surge in grassroots political awakening across towns and countryside
│   ├── Besant released and elected first woman President of INC (Calcutta, 1917)
│   └── Lucknow Pact's acceptance of separate electorates institutionalized communalism
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Shifted Indian nationalist demand definitively towards self-government ("Swaraj")
    └── Built the organizational network that Mahatma Gandhi mobilized during Non-Cooperation
```

### 1. Background
- **Dormant Political Landscape**: Moderate Congress was moribund; revolutionaries were suppressed; World War I was raging in Europe.
- **Tilak's Return**: Released in June 1914; declared loyalty to British Crown while advocating legitimate constitutional self-government (**Home Rule**).
- **Annie Besant's Vision**: Inspired by Irish Home Rule movement; campaigned for Indian self-government within the British Empire.

### 2. Causes
- **Wartime Economic Distress**: Rampant inflation, scarcity of basic goods, forced wartime grain requisitions, and recruitment of rural youth.
- **Allied Hypocrisy**: Britain fought claiming to defend "democracy and self-determination," while denying elementary self-governance to India.
- **Need for Organizational Dynamism**: Congress operated only as an annual three-day debating society without standing grassroots district networks.

### 3. Main Programmes & Events
- **Two Separate Home Rule Leagues**:
  - **Tilak's League (April 1916, Belgaum)**: Restricted to Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces, and Berar (6 branches).
  - **Annie Besant's League (September 1916, Madras)**: Covered the rest of India including Bombay city (over 200 branches).
  - Maintained distinct operational jurisdictions to avoid personality friction while coordinating national strategies.
- **Agitation Methods**:
  - Organised political libraries, reading rooms, student study circles, public lectures, and distribution of vernacular pamphlets.
  - Tilak propagated demand through newspapers *Kesari* and *Mahratta*; Besant mobilized public through *New India* and *Commonweal*.
- **The Lucknow Congress Session (1916)**:
  - Presided over by Moderate veteran **Ambica Charan Mazumdar**.
  - **Reunion of Congress**: Tilak and Extremists formally readmitted into Congress after 9-year rift since Surat Split.
- **The Lucknow Pact (December 1916)**:
  - Historic joint agreement signed between **Indian National Congress** and the **All India Muslim League**.
  - Engineered primarily by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** and **Mohammad Ali Jinnah** (hailed as "Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity").
  - Congress conceded **Separate Electorates** for Muslims and fixed weighted representation in imperial and provincial legislative councils.
  - Joint demands: Immediate self-government; elected majorities in legislative councils; fiscal independence.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**: Architect of the grassroots agitation; demanded Swaraj and reorganization of Indian states on linguistic lines.
- **Annie Besant**: Irish orator and organizer; first woman Congress President (1917); galvanized Madras presidency.
- **Mohammad Ali Jinnah**: Crucial bridge between Congress and League; championed constitutional nationalism and joint Hindu-Muslim action.
- **Sir S. Subramanya Aiyer**: Former Madras High Court Judge; renounced imperial knighthood in protest against Besant's arrest.

### 5. British Response
- **Executive Repression**:
  - Barred students from attending political meetings; prohibited Tilak from entering Punjab and Delhi.
  - Interned **Annie Besant, George Arundale, and B.P. Wadia** in Ootacamund (June 1917).
- **The Montagu Declaration (20 August 1917)**:
  - Secretary of State Edwin Montagu announced British policy: *"Increasing association of Indians in every branch of administration and gradual development of self-governing institutions."*
  - Pacified Moderates and took wind out of the Home Rule movement's sails.

### 6. Consequences
- **Besant's Release & Triumphant Leadership**: Released in September 1917; presided over historic Calcutta Congress session (1917).
- **Decline of the Movement (1918–1919)**:
  - Besant vacillated over Montagu-Chelmsford reforms; Tilak departed for London to pursue libel lawsuit against Valentine Chirol.
- **Institutionalization of Communal Representation**: The Lucknow Pact legitimized religion-based electorates, providing official Congress sanction to communal politics.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Mass Base Expanded**: Pulled hundreds of towns, smaller mofussil centers, and thousands of new cadre into nationalist politics.
- **Prepared Ground for Gandhian Era**: Created the active, functioning organizational machinery that Gandhi mobilized during the Non-Cooperation Movement.

# 14. Rowlatt Satyagraha & Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (1919)

```text
ROWLATT SATYAGRAHA & JALLIANWALA BAGH (1919)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Expiry of wartime Defence of India Regulations (1915)
│   ├── Seditious activities inquiry under Justice Sidney Rowlatt (1918)
│   └── Passing of the Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act (March 1919)
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Universal Indian outrage against imprisonment without trial ("No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal")
│   ├── Post-war economic hardships: Severe inflation, influenza epidemic, crop failures
│   └── Betrayal of wartime promises of constitutional self-government
│
├── MAIN EVENTS
│   ├── Gandhi organized Satyagraha Sabha; called for All-India Hartal on 6 April 1919
│   ├── Widespread strikes, demonstrations, and Hindu-Muslim unity (Dr. Satyapal & Saifuddin Kitchlew)
│   ├── Arrest of local leaders in Amritsar and outbreak of anti-British mob violence
│   ├── General Dyer imposed de facto martial law; banned public assemblies
│   └── Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919): Over 1,000 peaceful citizens gunned down
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Mahatma Gandhi, Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew, Dr. Satyapal
│   ├── General Reginald Dyer (The Butcher of Amritsar), Michael O'Dwyer (Lt. Governor of Punjab)
│   └── Rabindranath Tagore, Udham Singh
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Brutal martial law in Punjab: Public floggings, crawling orders, stripping of purdah
│   ├── Appointment of Hunter Committee (Disorders Inquiry Committee) to whitewash atrocities
│   └── British House of Lords praised Dyer, presenting him with a jeweled sword and cash fund
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Total destruction of Indian faith in British sense of justice and fair play
│   ├── Rabindranath Tagore renounced British Knighthood; Gandhi surrendered Kaiser-i-Hind medal
│   └── Directly paved way for the nationwide Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Irrevocable moral turning point: Transformed Gandhi from imperial loyalist to uncompromising rebel
    └── Exposed the naked, unmasked militaristic foundation of the British Raj
```

### 1. Background
- **The Rowlatt Committee (1918)**: Appointed under Justice Sidney Rowlatt to investigate alleged revolutionary conspiracies and recommend permanent emergency legislation.
- **The Black Act (March 1919)**:
  - Passed **Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act (1919)** despite unanimous opposition from all Indian non-official members in Imperial Council.
  - Allowed arrest and indefinite detention of any suspected political activist for up to two years without warrant or trial.
  - Deprived Indians of fundamental legal protections: Popularly denounced as **"No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal"**.

### 2. Causes
- **Betrayal of Wartime Sacrifice**: Over 1.3 million Indian soldiers fought in World War I expecting self-rule; received draconian repression instead.
- **Economic Misery**: Post-war commodity price hikes, acute food shortages, wartime heavy taxation, and catastrophic 1918 influenza epidemic killing millions.
- **Punjab Discontent**: Forced wartime military recruitments and heavy-handed grain requisitions by autocratic Lieutenant-Governor Sir **Michael O'Dwyer**.

### 3. Main Events
- **Call for All-India Satyagraha**:
  - Mahatma Gandhi formed **Satyagraha Sabha** (February 1919); called for a nationwide **Hartal on 6 April 1919**.
  - First truly pan-Indian political strike; closed shops, suspended business, and mobilized millions in peaceful prayer and fasting.
- **Unprecedented Hindu-Muslim Unity**:
  - Swami Shraddhanand addressed mammoth congregation at Jama Masjid (Delhi).
  - In Amritsar, **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr. Satyapal** led united, peaceful civic demonstrations.
- **Crisis in Amritsar (9–11 April 1919)**:
  - Kitchlew and Satyapal arrested and deported without trial on 10 April.
  - Angry crowds gathered at railway station; police opened fire, killing several; mob burned banks and attacked Europeans.
  - Brigadier-General **Reginald Dyer** arrived with troops; took control of city administration.
- **The Massacre at Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919, Baisakhi Day)**:
  - Over 15,000 unarmed men, women, and children gathered at Jallianwala Bagh to celebrate Baisakhi and protest leaders' arrests.
  - Ground was enclosed by high brick walls with only one narrow exit.
  - Dyer blocked exit with troops and armored cars; without issuing any dispersal warning, ordered soldiers to shoot into densest crowds.
  - Fired 1,650 rounds of ammunition until bullets ran out; over 1,000 slaughtered and more than 1,500 grievously wounded.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Mahatma Gandhi**: Led Rowlatt Satyagraha; suspended agitation on 18 April after sporadic mob violence, calling it a **"Himalayan Blunder"**.
- **Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer**: Perpetrator of massacre; unrepentantly claimed he fired to create a "moral and widespread terror effect."
- **Sir Michael O'Dwyer**: Lieutenant-Governor of Punjab who approved Dyer's massacre; assassinated in London in 1940 by **Udham Singh**.
- **Rabindranath Tagore**: Renounced British **Knighthood (Sir)**, writing to Viceroy: *"The time has come when badges of honour make our shame glaring."*

### 5. British Response
- **Barbaric Martial Law Regime**:
  - Inflicted the infamous **Crawling Order** (Indians forced to crawl on bellies through street where a British woman was assaulted).
  - Public floggings, open-air wire cages, and aerial bombings of peaceful villages in Gujranwala.
- **Hunter Committee Whitewash (October 1919)**:
  - Appointed Disorders Inquiry Committee (Lord Hunter); Indian members (Chimanlal Setalvad, Sultan Ahmad, Jagat Narayan) dissented sharply.
  - Censured Dyer merely for an "error of judgment"; he was relieved of command without any criminal prosecution.
- **British Public Acclaim for Dyer**:
  - Morning Post raised £26,000 for Dyer; British House of Lords passed resolution approving his actions.

### 6. Consequences
- **Total Loss of Faith in British Justice**: Demolished the last lingering illusions among Indian nationalists regarding British liberal democracy.
- **Surrender of Titles**: Gandhi surrendered his **Kaiser-i-Hind** gold medal awarded for South African humanitarian work.
- **Catalyst for Non-Cooperation**: Transformed mass discontent into the unstoppable tide of the Non-Cooperation Movement of 1920.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Psychological Turning Point**: Severed moral bond between rulers and ruled; converted millions of loyalists into passionate freedom fighters.
- **Vow of Independence**: Punjab atrocities became the burning national rallying cry that propelled the complete struggle for Purna Swaraj.

# 15. Non-Cooperation Movement & The Khilafat Agitation (1920–1922)

```text
NON-COOPERATION & KHILAFAT (1920–1922)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Rowlatt Act
│   ├── Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
│   ├── Khilafat Issue (Dismemberment of Ottoman Caliphate by Treaty of Sevres)
│   └── Economic conditions (Post-war inflation, high prices, poverty)
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Political: Humiliation of Punjab atrocities and denial of Swaraj
│   ├── Economic: Ruin of artisans, war-time taxation, and peasant exploitation
│   ├── Social: Surge in national self-respect and desire for mass action
│   └── Religious: Threat to Muslim Caliphate uniting Muslims with Congress
│
├── PROGRAMME
│   ├── Boycott schools and colleges
│   ├── Boycott British law courts and settle disputes via Panchayats
│   ├── Boycott foreign cloth and goods
│   ├── Resignation from government posts and nominated seats
│   └── Constructive: Promotion of Khadi, Charkha, National Schools, Untouchability removal
│
├── PARTICIPATION
│   ├── Students: Left government institutions in thousands for National Schools
│   ├── Lawyers: Boycotted British courts (C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, Rajendra Prasad)
│   ├── Peasants: Kisan Sabhas, Awadh peasant uprising, Eka Movement
│   ├── Women: Came out of purdah to picket liquor and foreign cloth shops
│   └── Traders: Boycotted British merchandise and financed Tilak Swaraj Fund
│
├── WITHDRAWAL
│   └── Chauri Chaura Incident (4 February 1922, Gorakhpur)
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Mass mobilisation on an unprecedented national scale
│   ├── New political consciousness and fearlessness among common masses
│   ├── Congress transformed from elite body to a revolutionary mass organization
│   └── Limitations: Communal riots post-suspension (Moplah Rebellion)
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── First genuine pan-Indian mass movement under Mahatma Gandhi's leadership
    └── Proved the formidable political power of non-violent non-cooperation
```

### 1. Background
- **Post-War National Agony**: Simmering rage over the Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh massacre, and Hunter Committee whitewash.
- **The Khilafat Question**:
  - British dismembered the Ottoman Empire after World War I under the **Treaty of Sevres (1920)**.
  - Indian Muslims saw the stripping of Ottoman Sultan's powers as a direct attack on the supreme Caliph (**Khalifa**) of Islam.
  - **Ali Brothers** (Shaukat Ali and Mohammad Ali) formed the **All-India Khilafat Committee (1919)**.
- **Gandhi's Insight**: Recognized Khilafat as an unprecedented opportunity to forge unbreakable Hindu-Muslim unity against the British Raj.

### 2. Causes
- **Political Causes**:
  - Punjab Wrongs: Failure of the British to punish General Dyer and Michael O'Dwyer.
  - Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919) failed to satisfy Indian aspirations for real self-government.
- **Economic Causes**:
  - Post-war economic crisis, skyrocketing prices of cloth and food grains, and oppressive taxes on artisans.
- **Social & Religious Causes**:
  - Universal demand of Indian Muslims to defend the temporal authority of the Caliphate and holy sites of Islam.

### 3. Programme & Main Events
- **Formal Launch (1 August 1920)**:
  - Launched on the day **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** passed away; nation observed nationwide hartal and mourning.
  - **Special Calcutta Session (September 1920)**: Gandhi's Non-Cooperation resolution approved despite initial hesitation from C.R. Das.
  - **Nagpur Congress Session (December 1920)**:
    - Historic endorsement under C.R. Das; goal shifted to achieving **"Swaraj by peaceful and legitimate means"**.
    - Reorganized Congress structure: 15-member Working Committee (CWC) and Provincial Congress Committees on linguistic basis.
- **Boycott Programme**:
  - Surrender of government titles and honorary offices (Gandhi returned Kaiser-i-Hind).
  - Boycott of government-aided schools, colleges, and university examinations.
  - Boycott of British law courts by lawyers and litigants; settlement of disputes via people's Panchayats.
  - Boycott of legislative council elections under the Government of India Act 1919.
  - Total boycott of foreign cloth and merchandise; burning of foreign fabrics in giant public bonfires.
  - Picketing of government-licensed liquor and toddy shops.
- **Constructive Programme**:
  - Mobilization of **Tilak Swaraj Fund**: Overshot target by collecting over Rs. 1 crore within six months.
  - Universal adoption of hand-spun **Khadi** and the **Charkha** as symbols of economic self-reliance.
  - Founding of National Universities: **Jamia Millia Islamia, Kashi Vidyapith, Gujarat Vidyapith, Bihar Vidyapith**.
  - Eradication of untouchability and promotion of national Hindu-Muslim communal brotherhood.
- **Social Participation**:
  - **Students**: Over 90,000 students abandoned colonial colleges; enrolled in national institutions or became volunteers.
  - **Lawyers**: Top legal luminaries gave up lucrative practices: **C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad**.
  - **Women**: Thousands discarded purdah; led picketing of liquor shops and foreign cloth marts; donated gold jewelry to Swaraj Fund.
  - **Peasants**: Turned movement into agrarian revolt against landlords: **Baba Ramchandra** (Awadh), **Madari Pasi** (Eka Movement).
  - **Working Class**: Massive railway and dock strikes organized under nascent trade unions.
  - **Tribals**: **Alluri Sitarama Raju** launched militant Rampa Rebellion (1922) in Andhra using guerrilla tactics.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Mahatma Gandhi**: Supreme undisputed commander who guided the moral ethos, strategy, and discipline of the mass campaign.
- **Ali Brothers (Mohammad Ali & Shaukat Ali)**: Mobilized millions of Muslims across India to unite with Congress on the Khilafat platform.
- **Chittaranjan Das (C.R. Das)**: "Deshbandhu"; sacrificed princely legal practice in Calcutta; led Bengal Non-Cooperation movement.
- **Motilal Nehru & Jawaharlal Nehru**: Transformed their aristocratic lifestyle into simple Khadi-wearing national crusaders.
- **Subhas Chandra Bose**: Resigned from prestigious Indian Civil Service (ICS) in 1921 to become Principal of Bengal National College.

### 5. British Response & Withdrawal
- **Repression Unleashed**:
  - Declared Congress Volunteer Corps illegal; banned public meetings under Section 144.
  - Arrested over 30,000 national volunteers within months, including C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, and the Ali Brothers.
  - Boycott of **Prince of Wales's Visit (November 1921)** met with police lathi charges and mass detentions.
- **The Chauri Chaura Incident (4 February 1922)**:
  - At Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP), police fired upon a peaceful peasant procession.
  - Infuriated crowd retaliated by locking 22 policemen inside the police station and setting it on fire; all died.
- **Withdrawal of Movement (12 February 1922)**:
  - Gandhi was horrified by the intrusion of violence into a movement based on pure Satyagraha.
  - Convened Congress Working Committee at **Bardoli**; unilaterally passed resolution suspending the entire Non-Cooperation Movement.
  - Gandhi arrested on 10 March 1922; Judge C.N. Broomfield sentenced him to 6 years imprisonment for sedition.

### 6. Consequences
- **Massive Surge in Popular Fearlessness**: The spell of British invincibility was permanently broken; common folk shed their fear of colonial prisons.
- **Congress Structural Metamorphosis**: Congress ceased being an elite bourgeois debating society; became a genuinely pan-Indian mass revolutionary platform.
- **Limitations & Fractures**:
  - Sudden withdrawal caused deep disillusionment among young leaders (Jawaharlal, Subhas Bose, Bhagat Singh).
  - Abolition of the Caliphate in Turkey by **Mustafa Kemal Ataturk (1924)** rendered Khilafat issue obsolete.
  - Rise of communal riots across North India and tragic **Moplah Rebellion (1921)** in Malabar.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Paradigm Shift in Freedom Struggle**: Proved that non-violent mass defiance could bring the mighty British colonial machinery to a virtual standstill.
- **Birth of Nationalist Hegemony**: Nationalists established moral and political counter-hegemony over the minds of the vast Indian peasantry.

# 16. Swarajists vs. No-Changers & Revolutionary Nationalism: Phase II (1922–1934)

```text
SWARAJISTS & REVOLUTIONARIES PHASE II (1922–1934)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Abrupt withdrawal of Non-Cooperation Movement post Chauri Chaura (1922)
│   ├── Imprisonment of Mahatma Gandhi and widespread political demoralization
│   └── Emergence of two tactical camps: Pro-Changers (Swarajists) vs. No-Changers
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Pro-Changers: Wreck colonial councils from within to end boycott paralysis
│   ├── No-Changers: Focus on Gandhian constructive work in rural villages
│   └── Revolutionaries: Complete disillusionment with non-violence leading to socialist armed struggle

│   ├── Congress-Khilafat Swarajya Party founded (January 1923, Gaya Session)

│   ├── Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Sukhdev, Rajguru, Jatin Das

│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Mass arrests of revolutionaries under Bengal Criminal Law Amendment
│   ├── Kakori hangings: Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqullah Khan, Roshan Singh, Rajendra Lahiri
│   ├── Lahore Conspiracy Case: Hanging of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru (23 March 1931)
│   └── Brutal martial law in Chittagong; Surya Sen captured and tortured to death (1934)
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Exposed unrepresentative and impotent nature of British-created councils
│   ├── Infused revolutionary movement with secular, Marxist, and working-class ideology
│   └── Surging youth radicalism forced Congress to adopt complete independence (Purna Swaraj)
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Prevented national apathy by keeping anti-imperialist political resistance alive
    └── Elevated supreme revolutionary martyrdom into an eternal patriotic inspiration
```

### 1. Background
- **Post-Chauri Chaura Void**: Sudden withdrawal of Non-Cooperation Movement (1922) created immense disillusionment and tactical division within the Congress.
- **The Council Entry Debate**:
  - **Pro-Changers (Swarajists)**: Led by C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru; advocated entering legislative councils to "end or mend" them.
  - **No-Changers**: Led by C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, and Rajendra Prasad; advocated sticking strictly to Gandhian constructive rural work.
- **Formation of Swaraj Party**: Gaya Congress (1922) rejected council entry; Das and Motilal resigned, forming the **Congress-Khilafat Swarajya Party (1923)**.

### 2. Causes
- **Swarajist Motive**: Wanted to expose the mockery of dyarchy from inside the councils and prevent loyalists from claiming to speak for India.
- **No-Changers' Motive**: Feared that council entry would lead to corrupting political careerism and distract from mass rural mobilization.
- **Revolutionary Resurgence**: Youth who left schools for Gandhi's promise of "Swaraj in one year" felt betrayed by the sudden withdrawal; turned to armed socialism.

### 3. Main Programmes & Events
- **Swarajist Council Triumphs (1923–1926)**:
  - Won 42 out of 101 elected seats in Central Legislative Assembly in the 1923 elections.
  - Forged coalitions; repeatedly defeated government on budgets and constitutional demands.
  - Elected **Vithalbhai Patel** as the first Indian **President (Speaker) of the Central Legislative Assembly (1925)**.
  - Successfully threw out the repressive **Public Safety Bill (1928)**.
- **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA, 1924)**:
  - Founded at Kanpur by **Sachin Sanyal, Ram Prasad Bismil, and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee**.
  - Objective: Establish a Federal Republic of the United States of India through armed revolution.
  - Published revolutionary manifesto **The Revolutionary (1925)**.
- **The Kakori Train Action (9 August 1925)**:
  - HRA revolutionaries halted 8-Down train at Kakori near Lucknow; looted government railway cash to purchase arms.
  - Bismil, Ashfaqullah Khan, Roshan Singh, and Rajendra Lahiri were arrested and hanged (December 1927).
- **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA, 1928)**:
  - Reorganized at **Ferozeshah Kotla (Delhi)** under the leadership of **Chandrashekhar Azad** and **Bhagat Singh**.
  - Formally adopted **Socialism** as the ultimate goal; shifted ideology from individual terror to organized mass revolution.
- **Assassination of Saunders (December 1928)**:
  - Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Azad shot dead British ASP **John Saunders** in Lahore to avenge the death of **Lala Lajpat Rai**.
- **Central Assembly Bombing (8 April 1929)**:
  - **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** threw harmless smoke bombs into the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi.
  - Protested passing of the Public Safety Bill and Trade Disputes Bill; threw leaflets stating: *"To make the deaf hear"*.
  - Offered themselves for arrest to use the British court as a national revolutionary propaganda platform.
- **Historic Hunger Strike & Jatin Das (1929)**:
  - Political prisoners in Lahore Jail launched fast demanding status of political prisoners rather than common criminals.
  - **Jatin Das** embraced heroic martyrdom on the **63rd day of his hunger strike (13 September 1929)**.
- **Chittagong Armoury Raid (18 April 1930)**:
  - Led by **Masterda Surya Sen** and Indian Republican Army (Chittagong Branch).
  - Captured two government armouries, cut telegraph links, declared Provisional Revolutionary Government, and hoisted national flag.
  - Fought heroic pitched battle on **Jalalabad Hill (22 April 1930)** against heavy British military forces.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Chittaranjan Das & Motilal Nehru**: Master tacticians who used legislative chambers to wreck British colonial executive autocracy from within.
- **Bhagat Singh**: Intellectual colossus; authored *Why I am an Atheist*; brilliantly integrated Marxism, rationalism, and anti-imperialist freedom struggle.
- **Chandrashekhar Azad**: Commander-in-Chief of HSRA; preserved secret revolutionary networks; shot himself with his last bullet at Alfred Park (Allahabad, 1931).
- **Surya Sen ("Masterda")**: Bengali schoolteacher who organized the Chittagong raid; captured, brutally tortured, and hanged in 1934.
- **Women Revolutionaries**: **Pritilata Waddedar** (attacked Pahartali European Club; died of cyanide), **Kalpana Datta**, and **Bina Das** (fired at Governor Stanley Jackson).

### 5. British Response
- **Savage Legal Repression**:
  - Enacted **Bengal Criminal Law Amendment Act** providing detention without trial and special tribunal death sentences.
- **Executions & Martyrs**:
  - Hanged Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Sukhdev at Lahore Central Jail on **23 March 1931** (Shaheed Diwas).
  - Sentenced Batukeshwar Dutt to life imprisonment in the Cellular Jail.
- **Cracking Down on Swarajists**:
  - Used viceregal certifications to bypass legislative assembly vetoes, gradually exhausting Swarajist obstruction tactics.

### 6. Consequences
- **Exposed Façade of Dyarchy**: Proved that legislative assemblies were powerless decorative talking shops without genuine executive authority.
- **Ideological Transformation of Nationalism**: Injected socialist ideals into Indian politics; inspired youth wings led by Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Bose.
- **Paved Way for Civil Disobedience**: Demoralization was swept away; Congress rejoined active mass confrontation in 1930.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Bridged the Agitational Void**: Filled the crucial 1922–1928 vacuum, preventing the national freedom movement from collapsing into apathy.
- **Secularization of Revolutionary Thought**: Emphasized that true independence meant freedom from both foreign imperialism and indigenous capitalist-landlord exploitation.

# 17. Simon Commission, The Nehru Report & Lahore Session (1927–1929)

```text
SIMON COMMISSION TO LAHORE SESSION (1927–1929)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Statutory requirement to review Government of India Act 1919 after 10 years
│   ├── Conservative British government feared imminent loss in British elections
│   └── Appointed all-white Indian Statutory Commission (November 1927) under Sir John Simon
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Insult to Indian self-respect by excluding any Indian member from the commission
│   ├── Birkenhead's arrogant challenge that Indians could not draft an agreed constitution
│   └── Unanimous national demand for immediate Dominion Status and responsible government
│
├── MAIN PROGRAMMES & EVENTS
│   ├── Universal boycott: "Simon Go Back" black flag demonstrations across India
│   ├── Madras Congress Session (1927) authorized total boycott of Simon Commission
│   ├── Police lathi charge at Lahore; fatal head injuries to Lala Lajpat Rai (1928)
│   ├── All-Parties Conference drafts the historic Nehru Report (August 1928)
│   ├── Jinnah rejects Nehru Report; issues Jinnah's Fourteen Points (March 1929)
│   └── Lahore Congress Session (December 1929): Historic Purna Swaraj Declaration
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Sir John Simon, Lord Birkenhead (Secretary of State), Lord Irwin (Viceroy)

│   └── Jawaharlal Nehru, Subhas Chandra Bose, Mohammad Ali Jinnah
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Brutal police violence on peaceful demonstrators (Lahore, Lucknow, Patna)
│   ├── Lord Irwin's vague "Deepavali Declaration" (October 1929) promising Dominion Status
│   └── Simon Commission Report (1930) recommended abolition of Dyarchy and separate electorates
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Complete collapse of constitutional consensus between Congress and Muslim League
│   ├── Ascendance of youthful socialist leadership (Jawaharlal Nehru & Subhas Bose) in Congress
│   └── Adoption of complete independence and authorization of Civil Disobedience
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Definitively transformed the freedom struggle's goal from Dominion Status to absolute Purna Swaraj
    └── Set 26 January 1930 as the first Independence Day of India (birth of Republic Day)
```

### 1. Background
- **The Simon Commission (1927)**:
  - Appointed two years ahead of schedule by the British Conservative government under Prime Minister Stanley Baldwin.
  - Seven-member **Indian Statutory Commission** chaired by **Sir John Simon**.
  - Contained **zero Indian members**; all seven members were white British parliamentarians.
- **Lord Birkenhead's Arrogant Challenge**:
  - Conservative Secretary of State Lord Birkenhead challenged Indians to draft a workable constitution acceptable to all communities.
- **National Outrage**: Unprecedented wave of anger united Congress, Hindu Mahasabha, and Jinnah's wing of the Muslim League in a complete boycott.

### 2. Causes
- **Racial Exclusion**: Deliberate denial of India's right to participate in framing its own political destiny.
- **Fear of Tory Maneuver**: British Conservatives sought to entrench communal divisions before losing power to the British Labour Party.
- **Surging Mass Restlessness**: Rise of youth leagues, peasant unrest, and trade union strikes demanding radical political confrontation.

### 3. Main Programmes & Events
- **"Simon Go Back" Agitation (1928)**:
  - Landed in Bombay on **3 February 1928**; greeted by nationwide general strike, black flags, and deafening cries of **"Simon Go Back"**.
  - Mass demonstrations organized in every city visited: Calcutta, Madras, Delhi, Patna, Lucknow, Lahore.
  - In Lucknow, **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Govind Ballabh Pant** were severely battered by mounted police lathis.
  - In Lahore (30 October 1928), **Lala Lajpat Rai** was mercilessly struck on the chest by police superintendent James Scott.
  - Lala Lajpat Rai declared: *"Every blow aimed at me is a nail in the coffin of British imperialism."* Died on 17 November 1928.
- **The Nehru Report (August 1928)**:
  - Drafted by committee chaired by **Motilal Nehru** with young **Jawaharlal Nehru** as secretary.
  - First major attempt by Indians to draft a complete, modern constitutional framework for their country.
  - **Key Recommendations**:
    - **Dominion Status** on lines of Canada and Australia within British Commonwealth.
    - Rejection of **Separate Electorates**; proposed **Joint Electorates** with reserved seats for religious minorities.
    - Nineteen **Fundamental Rights** including universal adult suffrage and equal rights for women.
    - Federal structure with **residual powers vested in the Centre**.
    - Dissolution of religious privileges; complete separation of state from religion.
- **Communal Rift & Jinnah's Fourteen Points (March 1929)**:
  - Muslim League under Mohammad Ali Jinnah rejected the Nehru Report's abolition of separate electorates.
  - Jinnah proposed three amendments; when rejected, formulated his famous **Fourteen Points** demanding 1/3 central Muslim quota and residual provincial powers.
- **The Historic Lahore Congress Session (December 1929)**:
  - Presided over by 40-year-old **Jawaharlal Nehru** on the banks of the River Ravi.
  - Passed the historic **Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) Resolution**.
  - Declared that Dominion Status was no longer acceptable; complete severance of British ties was the only goal.
  - Decided to boycott future Round Table Conferences and authorized CWC to launch **Civil Disobedience**.
  - Tricolor flag of Indian independence hoisted at midnight on **31 December 1929**.
  - Fixed **26 January 1930** to be celebrated nationwide as the **First Independence Day** (Purna Swaraj Day).

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Lala Lajpat Rai**: Lion of Punjab; sacrificed his life leading the Simon Commission boycott demonstration in Lahore.
- **Motilal Nehru**: Masterminded the pioneering constitutional blueprint of the Nehru Report.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru**: Symbol of radical youth; unfurled the flag of complete independence at Lahore.
- **Subhas Chandra Bose**: Allied with young Nehru; demanded complete independence over Dominion status at Calcutta (1928).
- **Sir John Simon**: Headed the Statutory Commission whose reactionary report was discarded by all Indian parties.

### 5. British Response
- **Police Brutality**: Deployed mounted police, tear gas, and brutal baton charges on peaceful nationalist crowds across all major cities.
- **Lord Irwin's Declaration (31 October 1929)**:
  - "Deepavali Declaration": Vaguely announced that the natural constitutional objective of India was **Dominion Status**.
  - Promised to convene a **Round Table Conference** in London to consult all parties.
- **The Simon Commission Report (May 1930)**:
  - Recommended abolition of Dyarchy and setting up of responsible government in provinces.
  - Rejected responsible government at the Centre and recommended retaining separate communal electorates.

### 6. Consequences
- **Generational Shift in Leadership**: Generational torch handed over from veterans (Motilal, Malaviya) to dynamic radicals (Jawaharlal, Subhas).
- **Permanent Hindu-Muslim Split**: Jinnah proclaimed the "parting of ways"; constitutional cooperation between Congress and League collapsed forever.
- **Launch of Salt Satyagraha**: Lahore resolution gave Gandhi the supreme mandate to launch the momentous Civil Disobedience Movement.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Definitive Sovereign Pledge**: Purna Swaraj pledge established that India's struggle was not for colonial dominion status, but for total sovereignty.
- **Sanctity of 26 January**: The historic memory of 26 January 1930 was later preserved by promulgating the **Constitution of India on 26 January 1950**.

# 18. Civil Disobedience Movement & Salt Satyagraha (1930–1934)

```text
CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE MOVEMENT (1930–1934)
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Lahore Congress (1929) mandate of Purna Swaraj
│   ├── Gandhi's Eleven Demands presented to Viceroy Lord Irwin (January 1930)
│   └── Irwin's silence and rejection triggering the Salt Satyagraha
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Political: Rejection of Indian demands for complete self-rule
│   ├── Economic: Devastating impact of the Great Depression (1929) on peasants
│   └── Salt Monopoly: Regressive British salt tax crushing the poorest of the poor
│
├── MAIN PROGRAMMES & PHASES
│   ├── Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930): 240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi
│   ├── Defiance of Salt Laws across coastal India (Tamil Nadu, Malabar, Orissa, Bengal)
│   ├── Dharasana Salt Raid under Sarojini Naidu, Imam Saheb, and Manilal Gandhi
│   ├── Regional Variants: Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (NWFP), Rani Gaidinliu (Manipur)
│   ├── First Phase (1930–1931), Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 1931), Karachi Session (1931)
│   └── Second Phase (1932–1934): Willingdon's ruthless suppression post-Second RTC
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Mahatma Gandhi, Sarojini Naidu, C. Rajagopalachari, K. Kelappan
│   ├── Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan ("Frontier Gandhi"), Rani Gaidinliu
│   └── Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Poona Pact 1932), Lord Irwin, Lord Willingdon
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Over 90,000 satyagrahis jailed; Congress declared an unlawful organization
│   ├── Brutal lathi charges at Dharasana; Garhwali soldiers court-martialled for refusing fire
│   ├── Ramsay MacDonald's Communal Award (August 1932) granting separate electorates to Dalits
│   └── Poona Pact (September 1932): Gandhi's fast unto death resulting in reserved seats
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── British forced to negotiate with Gandhi on terms of sovereign equality (Irwin Pact)
│   ├── Substantial participation of women, transforming the gender landscape of Indian politics
│   └── Direct catalyst leading to the Government of India Act 1935
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── The most disciplined, widespread, and socio-politically profound mass movement in Indian history
    └── Exposed the moral bankruptcy and administrative helplessness of the British Empire
```

### 1. Background
- **Mandate of Lahore**: Authorized Gandhi to start Civil Disobedience at the time and place of his choosing.
- **Gandhi's Eleven Demands (January 1930)**:
  - Formulated practical composite demands to unite all classes:
    - 50% reduction in land revenue and military spending.
    - Abolition of salt tax and government salt monopoly.
    - Protective tariff on foreign cloth and coastal reservation for Indian shipping.
    - Release of all political prisoners and right to carry firearms for self-defense.
- **Irwin's Dismissal**: Lord Irwin completely ignored Gandhi's letter; Gandhi declared: *"On bended knees I asked for bread and received a stone instead."*

### 2. Causes
- **The Salt Tax Injustice**:
  - Salt was an indispensable biological necessity for humans and cattle.
  - Colonial monopoly taxed salt manufacture, affecting the poorest rural peasants disproportionately.
- **The Great Economic Depression (1929)**:
  - Agricultural commodity prices crashed by over 50%; peasants could not pay fixed colonial cash revenues or debt interest.
- **Aspiration for Complete Sovereignty**: Mass hunger to give practical realization to the newly declared pledge of Purna Swaraj.

### 3. Main Programmes & Events
- **The Historic Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930)**:
  - Gandhi set out from **Sabarmati Ashram** with 78 chosen satyagrahis on a 240-mile march to Dandi on Gujarat coast.
  - Reached Dandi on 5 April; on morning of **6 April 1930**, picked up a lump of natural salt, formally breaking the British salt law.
- **Pan-Indian Proliferation of Salt Satyagraha**:
  - **Tamil Nadu**: **C. Rajagopalachari** led march from Tiruchirappalli to Vedaranyam.
  - **Kerala**: **K. Kelappan** (Kerala Veda) marched from Calicut to Payyanur.
  - **Andhra**: Sibirams (camps) organized in coastal districts to manufacture illicit salt.
  - **Bengal & Orissa**: Mass violation of salt laws at Contai and Balasore.
- **The Epic Raid at Dharasana (May 1930)**:
  - Following Gandhi's arrest, **Sarojini Naidu**, **Imam Saheb**, and Gandhi's son **Manilal** led 2,500 volunteers to raid Dharasana Salt Works.
  - American journalist **Webb Miller** reported: Unarmed marchers walked forward in waves, beaten down by steel-shod lathis without raising a hand to defend themselves.
- **Regional Variations of Agitation**:
  - **North-West Frontier Province (NWFP)**: **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (Badshah Khan)** organized the non-violent **Khudai Khidmatgars (Red Shirts)**.
  - In Peshawar, Hindu soldiers of the **Royal Garhwal Rifles** under Chandra Singh Garhwali refused orders to fire on unarmed Muslim Khudai Khidmatgars.
  - **North-East (Nagaland/Manipur)**: 13-year-old **Rani Gaidinliu** raised tribal rebellion; captured and imprisoned for life (honored as "Rani" by Nehru).
  - **Central Provinces & Maharashtra**: Violation of repressive British forest laws.
  - **United Provinces (UP)**: Peasant **No-Revenue and No-Rent campaign** led by Jawaharlal Nehru.
- **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931)**:
  - Signed after months of negotiations; government placed Congress on footing of equal status.
  - **Pact Terms**: Government agreed to release non-violent political prisoners, return confiscated lands, permit peaceful picketing, and allow coastal residents to make salt for domestic consumption.
  - Congress agreed to suspend Civil Disobedience and participate in the **Second Round Table Conference**.
- **Karachi Congress Session (March 1931)**:
  - Presided over by **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**.
  - Endorsed the Gandhi-Irwin Pact despite mass grief over execution of Bhagat Singh (23 March).
  - Adopted two historic resolutions: **Fundamental Rights** and the **National Economic Programme** (drafted by Jawaharlal Nehru).
- **The Second Round Table Conference (London, 1931)**:
  - Gandhi attended as the sole representative of the Indian National Congress.
  - Deadlocked on the communal question: British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald and communal delegates prioritized separate electorates over Indian freedom.
  - Gandhi returned empty-handed.
- **Second Phase of Civil Disobedience (1932–1934)**:
  - New Viceroy **Lord Willingdon** unleashed ruthless pre-emptive crackdown; arrested Gandhi and banned Congress within hours.
  - Movement gradually lost momentum and was formally withdrawn in **April 1934**.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Mahatma Gandhi**: Generalissimo of the movement; weaponized everyday salt into a global symbol of moral defiance against imperialism.
- **Sarojini Naidu**: "Nightingale of India"; first Indian woman INC President (1925); led the legendary non-violent assault on Dharasana.
- **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan**: Proved that Pathan warriors could practice supreme, disciplined non-violence under the Khudai Khidmatgar banner.
- **C. Rajagopalachari**: Brilliant intellectual who organized the Vedaranyam march and mobilized the Tamil populace.
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**: Championed rights of the Depressed Classes; negotiated the **Poona Pact (1932)** with fasting Gandhi, securing 148 reserved seats.

### 5. British Response
- **Savage Legal & Police Terror**:
  - Promulgated draconian emergency ordinances; confiscated property, livestock, and lands of satyagrahis.
  - Jailed over 90,000 citizens in the first phase and over 120,000 in the second phase.
- **The Communal Award (August 1932)**:
  - Ramsay MacDonald announced **Separate Electorates for Depressed Classes (Dalits)**, treating them as a separate minority community from Hindus.
- **Gandhi's Epic Fast & The Poona Pact (September 1932)**:
  - Imprisoned in Yerwada Jail (Pune), Gandhi began a **fast unto death** against bifurcating Hindu society.
  - **Poona Pact (24 September 1932)** signed between Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Madan Mohan Malaviya:
    - Abandoned separate electorates for Depressed Classes.
    - Increased reserved legislative seats for Depressed Classes from 71 to **148** in provincial councils.

### 6. Consequences
- **Irrevocable Loss of British Legitimacy**: The moral brutality witnessed by foreign correspondents destroyed Britain's global claim to civilized governance.
- **Unprecedented Awakening of Women**: Women stepped onto the streets in tens of thousands, picketing shops, courting arrest, and transforming gender roles.
- **Direct Impetus for GOI Act 1935**: British recognized that governing India without some constitutional devolution of power was no longer viable.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Apex of Mass Satyagraha**: Demonstrated the highest degree of organizational discipline and non-violent perseverance in world revolutionary history.
- **Inclusion of Marginalized Communities**: Brought peasants, students, women, merchants, and lower castes into a united national front against imperial domination.

# 19. Government of India Act 1935 & 1937 Provincial Congress Ministries

```text
GOI ACT 1935 & 1937 MINISTRIES
│
├── BACKGROUND
│   ├── Deliberations of the Three Round Table Conferences (1930–1932)
│   ├── British White Paper (1933) and Joint Select Committee Report (Lord Linlithgow)
│   └── Enactment of the longest act passed by British Parliament (August 1935)
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Need to appease Indian constitutional moderates and divide nationalist forces
│   ├── Objective of using conservative Princely States to counter democratic Congress
│   └── British intention to retain ultimate financial, military, and foreign policy control
│
├── MAIN PROVISIONS & EVENTS
│   ├── Proposed All-India Federation uniting British Provinces and Princely States
│   ├── Abolition of Dyarchy in provinces; introduction of Provincial Autonomy
│   ├── Introduction of Dyarchy at the Centre (Reserved vs. Transferred subjects)
│   ├── Establishment of Federal Court, Reserve Bank of India, and Federal PSC
│   ├── 1937 Provincial Elections: Congress sweeps 7 out of 11 provinces
│   └── 28 Months of Congress Rule: Agrarian reforms, civil liberties restored
│
├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES
│   ├── Lord Linlithgow (Viceroy), Lord Zetland (Secretary of State)
│   ├── Congress Premiers: C. Rajagopalachari (Madras), Govind Ballabh Pant (UP)
│   ├── B.G. Kher (Bombay), Sri Krishna Sinha (Bihar), Gopinath Bordoloi (Assam)
│   └── Mohammad Ali Jinnah, A.K. Fazlul Huq, Sir Sikandar Hayat Khan
│
├── BRITISH RESPONSE
│   ├── Governors retained draconian discretionary veto powers and Ordinance rules
│   ├── Lord Linlithgow declared India at war with Germany (1939) without consulting ministries
│   └── Encouraged Muslim League's Day of Deliverance following Congress resignations
│
├── CONSEQUENCES
│   ├── Resignation of all Congress Ministries in October–November 1939
│   ├── Muslim League celebrated "Day of Deliverance" (22 December 1939)
│   └── Passed Lahore Resolution (March 1940) formally demanding independent Pakistan
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Provided the structural, administrative, and constitutional skeleton for the 1950 Indian Constitution
    └── Proved the exceptional administrative competence and governance capabilities of Congress leaders
```

### 1. Background
- **The Constitutional Journey**: Outcome of Simon Commission Report (1930), Nehru Report, Round Table Conferences, and White Paper of 1933.
- **Enactment of the Act**: Passed by British Parliament in **August 1935**; longest piece of legislation enacted until that date (321 sections, 10 schedules).
- **Double Structure**:
  - Provided for an **All-India Federation** at the Centre.
  - Granted **Provincial Autonomy** to 11 British Indian provinces.

### 2. Causes
- **Imperial Strategy**: Devised by British conservatives to derail the mass national movement by co-opting nationalists into provincial administration.
- **Countering Congress at the Centre**: The proposed All-India Federation required princely states to join, ensuring conservative princes counter-balanced democratic Congress forces.
- **Retaining British Real Power**: Essential imperial subjects (**Defence, External Affairs, Finance**) remained under exclusive discretionary control of the Viceroy.

### 3. Main Provisions & Events
- **Key Features of GOI Act 1935**:
  - **Proposed All-India Federation**: To comprise 11 British Indian provinces, 6 Chief Commissioner's provinces, and princely states willing to accede. (Never materialized as princes refused).
  - **Abolition of Dyarchy in Provinces**: Provincial executive transferred to ministers responsible to elected provincial legislatures (**Provincial Autonomy**).
  - **Dyarchy at the Centre**: Federal subjects divided into **Reserved** (administered by Viceroy: Defence, Foreign Affairs, Ecclesiastical) and **Transferred** (administered by Council of Ministers).
  - **Bicameral Legislatures**: Introduced in 6 out of 11 provinces (Bengal, Bombay, Madras, UP, Bihar, Assam).
  - **Three Legislative Lists**: Federal List (59 items), Provincial List (54 items), and Concurrent List (36 items); **Residuary Powers vested in the Viceroy**.
  - **New Institutions Established**: **Federal Court (1937)** at Delhi, **Reserve Bank of India (1935)**, and Federal Public Service Commission.
  - **Electorate Expanded**: Extended franchise to about 14% of population; expanded separate communal electorates to women and labor.
  - **Separation of Burma & Aden**: Burma separated from India; Orissa and Sindh created as separate new provinces.
- **The 1937 Provincial Elections**:
  - Congress contested elections to "combact the Act from within"; swept polls convincingly.
  - Secured clear absolute majorities in 5 provinces (Madras, UP, Bihar, CP, Orissa); emerged as largest party in Bombay, Assam, NWFP.
  - Formed ministries in **8 out of 11 provinces**; Muslim League failed to secure a majority in any Muslim-majority province.
- **28 Months of Congress Rule (1937–1939)**:
  - **Civil Liberties Restored**: Repealed emergency laws; lifted ban on political organizations; returned confiscated press deposits.
  - **Agrarian Relief**: Passed Tenancy Acts in UP, Bihar, and Bombay, protecting tenant farmers from arbitrary rent hikes and evictions.
  - **Labor & Social Welfare**: Passed Bombay Industrial Disputes Act; instituted primary education schemes (**Wardha Scheme**); encouraged Khadi.
  - **National Planning Committee (1938)**: Congress President Subhas Chandra Bose set up committee under **Jawaharlal Nehru** to chart future planned economic development.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Lord Linlithgow**: Longest-serving Viceroy (1936–1943); imperialist bureaucrat who ruthlessly preserved British imperial prerogatives.
- **C. Rajagopalachari**: Premier of Madras; introduced sales tax to fund prohibition and enacted radical Temple Entry legislation.
- **Govind Ballabh Pant & Rafi Ahmed Kidwai**: Led UP Ministry; pioneered comprehensive agrarian debt and tenancy legislation.
- **Mohammad Ali Jinnah**: Reorganized Muslim League after 1937 electoral humiliation; adopted aggressive communal rhetoric targeting Congress ministries.

### 5. British Response
- **Viceroy's Discretionary Vetoes**: Governors retained special powers to overrule ministers, dismiss cabinets, and promulgate ordinances (**Section 93**).
- **Outbreak of World War II (September 1939)**:
  - Lord Linlithgow declared India a belligerent at war with Nazi Germany without consulting Indian provincial ministries or leaders.
  - Congress protested: Demanded immediate declaration of Britain's war aims and establishment of a genuinely responsible national government at the Centre.
- **Resignation of Congress Ministries (October–November 1939)**:
  - British refused to give any satisfactory pledge; all eight Congress provincial ministries resigned in protest.
  - Jinnah and Muslim League celebrated **"Day of Deliverance" (22 December 1939)** with British tacit approval.

### 6. Consequences
- **Political Deadlock**: Resignation of ministries removed Congress from provincial power, handing strategic leverage back to the colonial bureaucracy and Muslim League.
- **The Lahore Resolution (March 1940)**:
  - Muslim League met at Lahore; passed historic resolution demanding independent sovereign states in Muslim-majority northwestern and eastern zones (**The Pakistan Resolution**).
- **End of Constitutional Moderation**: Demonstrated that partial constitutionalism could not satisfy India's demand for complete sovereign freedom.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **Blueprint for Indian Constitution**: The GOI Act 1935 contributed nearly 75% of the administrative, structural, and federal provisions of the 1950 Constitution of India.
- **Proved Indian Administrative Capability**: Dispelled colonial slander that Indians were incompetent to govern; Congress ministers proved superior to British bureaucrats.

```text
│
├── CAUSES
│   ├── Political: British refusal to state war aims or grant immediate national government
│   ├── Strategic: Collapse of France (May 1940) forcing British to seek Indian war support
│   └── Moral: Need to assert free speech against forced war participation without violence
│
├── MAIN PROGRAMMES & EVENTS
│   ├── Viceroy Linlithgow announces the August Offer (8 August 1940)
│   ├── Rejection of August Offer by both Congress and Muslim League
│   ├── Gandhi launches Individual Satyagraha (October 1940)

├── IMPORTANT PERSONALITIES

│   └── Lord Linlithgow (Viceroy), Winston Churchill (British Prime Minister)
│

│   ├── Arrest of Vinoba Bhave, Nehru, and over 25,000 individual satyagrahis

│

│   ├── Demonstrated Indian moral opposition to fascism and imperialism simultaneously
│   ├── Preserved nationalist political momentum without hindering British war effort
│   └── Forced Churchill's war cabinet to dispatch the Cripps Mission (1942)
│
└── HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
    ├── Asserted the fundamental democratic right to freedom of speech against colonial coercion
    └── Re-established Mahatma Gandhi's supreme moral leadership over the freedom movement
```

### 1. Background
- **Outbreak of War**: Britain declared war on Germany on **3 September 1939**; Viceroy Linlithgow dragged India in without consulting Indian leaders.
- **Congress Resignation**: Congress offered conditional support if Britain declared war aims and formed an interim national government; British refused, prompting ministerial resignations.
- **Battle of Britain & Fall of France (1940)**: Nazi blitzkrieg captured France and besieged Britain; British desperate for Indian men and industrial war resources.

### 2. Causes
- **Political Deadlock**: British government under imperialist Winston Churchill refused to contemplate any meaningful wartime transfer of sovereign power.
- **Need for Moral Protest**: Gandhi insisted on asserting the democratic right to free speech against war without launching a disruptive mass agitation.

### 3. Main Programmes & Events
- **The August Offer (8 August 1940)**:
  - Announced by Viceroy Lord Linlithgow to appease Indian opinion during the Battle of Britain.
  - **Key Terms**:
    - **Dominion Status** as the ultimate objective for India at an unspecified future date.
    - Expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council to include more Indians.

- **Rejection of the August Offer**:
  - Congress rejected it; Nehru declared: *"Dominion status concept is dead as a door-nail."*
  - Muslim League rejected it because it gave no clear guarantee for the creation of Pakistan.
- **Individual Civil Disobedience / Satyagraha (October 1940 – December 1941)**:

  - Selected satyagrahis stepped forward publicly, made anti-war speeches, and courted arrest.
  - **Satyagrahi Sequence**:
    - **First Satyagrahi**: **Acharya Vinoba Bhave** (arrested after making anti-war speech).
    - **Second Satyagrahi**: **Jawaharlal Nehru** (arrested in Gorakhpur).
    - **Third Satyagrahi**: **Brahma Datt** of Gandhi's ashram.
  - Over **25,000 satyagrahis** courted arrest across India; released in December 1941 as Japanese threat loomed.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Mahatma Gandhi**: Devised Individual Satyagraha to register moral protest while giving Britain no excuse for mass military crackdowns.
- **Acharya Vinoba Bhave**: Chosen first satyagrahi due to his impeccable spiritual dedication to non-violence and constructive village work.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru**: Second satyagrahi; relentlessly exposed British imperial hypocrisy on world anti-fascist platforms.

- **Cosmetic Executive Expansion (July 1941)**:
  - Expanded Viceroy's Executive Council from 7 to 12 members (8 Indians), but retained Defence, Home, and Finance with Europeans.

### 6. Consequences

### 1. Background
- **The Japanese Threat (1942)**: Japan swept through Southeast Asia, capturing Singapore (February 1942) and Rangoon (March 1942); bombs fell on Vizag and Calcutta.
- **The Cripps Mission (March–April 1942)**:
  - British War Cabinet dispatched left-wing Labour leader **Sir Stafford Cripps** to negotiate with Indian leaders.
  - **Cripps Proposals**:
    - Creation of an Indian Union with **Dominion Status** after the war.
    - Constitution-making body elected by provincial assemblies and nominated by princes.
    - **Secession Clause**: Any province or princely state not willing to accept new constitution could secede and form separate union.
    - British control of Indian Defence during wartime.
  - **Rejection**:
    - Gandhi famously termed it a **"Post-dated cheque on a crashing bank"**.
    - Rejected by Congress (opposed right of provinces to secede) and Muslim League (did not grant Pakistan explicitly).

### 2. Causes
- **Threat of Japanese Aggression**: Gandhi argued that Britain's presence in India was an open invitation to Japanese invasion; only a free India could defend herself.
- **Wartime Economic Devastation**: Skyrocketing prices, severe shortages of rice and kerosene, and brutal British boat-confiscation policies in coastal Bengal.
- **Disillusionment with Negotiations**: Convinced Indian leaders that Britain would never voluntarily grant self-rule without an ultimate mass struggle.

### 3. Main Programmes & Events
- **Historic Bombay Resolution (8 August 1942)**:
  - All-India Congress Committee met at **Gowalia Tank Maidan (August Kranti Maidan)**, Bombay.
  - Passed the **Quit India Resolution** demanding immediate end of British rule.
  - Gandhi gave the immortal national battle-cry: **"Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro)**: *"We shall either free India or die in the attempt."*
- **Operation Zero Hour (9 August 1942)**:
  - In pre-dawn swoops, British police arrested all top Congress leaders (Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad, Rajendra Prasad).
  - Congress was declared an unlawful association; its funds seized and offices padlocked.
- **Course of the Rebellion (Three Dynamic Phases)**:
  - **Phase 1: Urban Upsurge (August 1942)**: Spontaneous strikes, student demonstrations, clashing with police, boycotts in Bombay, Ahmedabad, Delhi, Kanpur.
  - **Phase 2: Rural Guerrilla Revolt (1942)**: Peasants torched police stations, dismantled railway tracks, and severed telegraph wires across Eastern UP and Bihar.
  - **Phase 3: Underground Networks & Parallel Governments (Late 1942–1944)**:
    - **Congress Radio**: Secret underground broadcasting operated by 22-year-old **Usha Mehta** and Babubhai Prasad.
    - **Azad Dasta**: Underground guerrilla fighting force organized in Nepal jungles by **Jayaprakash Narayan** after escaping Hazaribagh Central Jail.
- **Parallel Governments (Prati Sarkars)**:
  - **Balia (UP, August 1942)**: **Chittu Pandey** established short-lived popular government, releasing all Congress prisoners.
  - **Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal, Dec 1942–Sep 1944)**: **Tamluk Jatiya Sarkar** formed; organized *Bidyut Vahinis* and famine relief; 73-year-old **Matangini Hazra** was shot dead holding the tricolor.
  - **Satara (Maharashtra, 1943–1946)**: **Prati Sarkar** organized by **Nana Patil** and **Y.B. Chavan**; operated people's courts (*Nyayadan Mandals*) and rural administration.

### 4. Important Personalities
- **Mahatma Gandhi**: Imprisoned at Aga Khan Palace (Pune); undertook a heroic 21-day fast in 1943; his wife Kasturba and secretary Mahadev Desai died in prison.
- **Aruna Asaf Ali**: "Heroine of 1942"; defied police to hoist the National Flag at Gowalia Tank on 9 August; evaded arrest throughout the war.
- **Jayaprakash Narayan (JP)**: Escaped from Hazaribagh Jail; mobilized youth and socialist cadres for armed sabotage.
- **Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia**: Broadcast fearless patriotic speeches over the underground Congress Radio.
- **Sucheta Kripalani**: Coordinated non-violent relief, womens' underground courier networks, and legal defense of captured satyagrahis.

### 5. British Response
- **Savage State Terrorism**:
  - Deployed 57 British army battalions; authorized machine-gunning of crowds from military airplanes in Bihar and Bengal.
  - Burned hundreds of villages; flogged suspected rebels; imposed over Rs. 90 lakhs in collective village fines.
  - Over **10,000 citizens killed** in police and military firings; more than 100,000 imprisoned without trial.
- **Churchill's Ruthlessness**:
  - British War Cabinet refused to release Gandhi even when doctors feared he was dying during his 1943 fast.

### 6. Consequences
- **Temporary Administrative Suppression**: Open mass movement suppressed by end of 1943 through overwhelming military violence.
- **The Great Bengal Famine of 1943**:
  - Man-made catastrophe caused by wartime grain diversions, scorched-earth boat seizures, and Churchill's refusal to divert grain ships; killed over **3 million people**.
- **Permanent Paralysis of Colonial Will**: British administrators realized that maintaining the Raj by military force was economically and politically unsustainable.

### 7. Historical Significance
- **The Point of No Return**: Quit India demonstrated that the Indian people's desire for immediate, unconditional independence was irreversible.
- **Spontaneous Mass Leadership**: When all senior leaders were jailed, ordinary students, peasants, and women took up the struggle as their own self-directed revolution.

  - Added fourth Law Member to Council (**Lord Macaulay**); codified Indian laws.
- **Charter Act (1853)**:
  - Separated legislative and executive functions of Governor-General's Council.
  - Introduced open competitive examination for the **Indian Civil Service (ICS)**.
- **Government of India Act (1858)**:
  - Transferred sovereignty from Company to British Crown; abolished Board of Control and Court of Directors.
  - Created **Secretary of State for India** (assisted by 15-member India Council); Viceroy became Crown representative.
- **Indian Councils Act (1861)**:
  - Initiated legislative decentralization; restored legislative powers to Madras and Bombay Presidencies.
  - Introduced **Portfolio System** under Lord Canning; empowered Viceroy to issue **Ordinances** valid for 6 months.
- **Indian Councils Act (1892)**:
  - Increased non-official members; allowed discussion of annual budget and asking questions on public interest.
- **Morley-Minto Reforms / Indian Councils Act (1909)**:
  - Introduced **Separate Electorates for Muslims**, legalizing communalism (Lord Minto: "Father of Communal Electorate").
  - First Indian appointed to Viceroy's Executive Council: **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** (Law Member).
- **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms / GOI Act (1919)**:
  - Introduced **Dyarchy in Provinces**: Transferred subjects (Indian ministers) and Reserved subjects (Governor's executive council).
  - Introduced **Bicameral Legislature** at the Centre (Council of State and Legislative Assembly).
  - Extended separate communal electorates to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, and Europeans.
- **Government of India Act (1935)**:
  - Proposed **All-India Federation**; abolished provincial dyarchy and introduced **Provincial Autonomy**.
  - Introduced Dyarchy at the Centre; created **Federal Court** (1937) and **Reserve Bank of India** (1935).
- **Indian Independence Act (1947)**:
  - Ended British rule on 15 August 1947; partitioned British India into two independent dominions (**India and Pakistan**).

### 2. Major Viceroys & Significant Milestones
- **Lord Canning (1856–1862)**: 1857 Revolt; Queen's Proclamation 1858; Indian Penal Code (1860); Police Act (1861).
- **Lord Lytton (1876–1880)**: Reactionary rule; Vernacular Press Act 1878; Arms Act 1878; Delhi Durbar 1877 during famine.
- **Lord Ripon (1880–1884)**: "Father of Local Self-Government"; repealed Vernacular Press Act; First Factory Act 1881; Ilbert Bill 1883.
- **Lord Curzon (1899–1905)**: Partition of Bengal (1905); Indian Universities Act 1904; Ancient Monuments Preservation Act 1904.
- **Lord Hardinge II (1910–1916)**: Annulment of Bengal Partition (1911); transfer of capital from Calcutta to Delhi (1911).
- **Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921)**: Home Rule Leagues; Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh (1919); Non-Cooperation Movement (1920).
- **Lord Irwin (1926–1931)**: Simon Commission (1928); Nehru Report; Dandi March and Salt Satyagraha (1930); Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931).
- **Lord Linlithgow (1936–1943)**: Longest tenure; 1937 elections; August Offer 1940; Cripps Mission and Quit India Movement 1942.
- **Lord Wavell (1943–1947)**: Wavell Plan & Simla Conference (1945); INA Trials; Cabinet Mission (1946); Direct Action Day.
- **Lord Mountbatten (March–August 1947)**: June 3rd Plan; Radcliffe Boundary Commission; Indian Independence Act 1947.

### 3. Key Peasant, Tribal & Working-Class Movements
- **Indigo Revolt (1859–1860, Bengal)**: Led by Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Biswas; dramatized in Dinabandhu Mitra's *Neel Darpan*.
- **Pabna Agrarian Leagues (1873, Bengal)**: Legal resistance by ryots against zamindari rack-renting; led to Bengal Tenancy Act 1885.
- **Deccan Riots (1875, Maharashtra)**: Social boycott of Gujarati and Marwari moneylenders; led to Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act 1879.
- **Munda Ulgulan (1899–1900, Chhotanagpur)**: Led by **Birsa Munda** against Dikus and *Bet-begari*; led to Chhotanagpur Tenancy Act 1908.
- **Bardoli Satyagraha (1928, Gujarat)**: Peasant protest against 22% land revenue hike; led by **Vallabhbhai Patel** (awarded title "Sardar").
- **Tebhaga Movement (1946–1947, Bengal)**: Sharecroppers (*Bargadars*) demanded two-thirds share of produce under Kisan Sabha.
- **Telangana Movement (1946–1951)**: Armed peasant uprising against feudal Nizam, Deshmukhs, and Razakars.

- **Tribal & Peasant Leaders**: Regions, leaders, and triggers of Santhal, Munda, Rampa, and Kol uprisings.
