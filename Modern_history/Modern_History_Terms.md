### 1. Colonial Expansion, Treaties & Diplomatic Doctrines

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Farman** | Imperial royal decree issued by the Mughal Emperor granting trade privileges (e.g., Farrukhsiyar's 1717 Farman). | British Expansion |
| **Dastak** | Duty-free trade pass issued to British East India Company exempting company cargo from internal transit tolls. | Colonial Trade |
| **Gumashta** | Commercial Indian agent employed by the Company to force weavers to sign monopolistic supply contracts. | Colonial Exploitation |
| **Diwani** | Sovereign right to collect civil land revenue and administer civil justice (granted to Company in 1765). | Company Rule |
| **Nizamat** | Sovereign executive functions: Police, criminal justice, and military defense left nominally with the Nawab. | Company Rule |
| **Dual Government** | System in Bengal (1765–1772) devised by Clive: Company exercised power without responsibility; Nawab had responsibility without power. | Robert Clive's Rule |
| **Policy of Ring Fence**| Strategy of Warren Hastings defending frontiers of neighboring buffer states (like Awadh) to protect Bengal. | British Expansion |
| **Subsidiary Alliance** | Lord Wellesley's system forcing Indian states to surrender foreign affairs, host British troops, and pay subsidies. | Wellesley's Expansion |
| **Doctrine of Lapse** | Lord Dalhousie's policy annexing princely states whose rulers died without a natural biological male heir. | Dalhousie's Expansion |
| **Policy of Subordinate Isolation**| British policy (1813–1857) reducing Indian princely rulers to isolated subordinate allies through unilateral treaties. | Princely States Policy |
| **Policy of Subordinate Union**| Post-1858 policy treating princely rulers as integral feudatories of the British Crown ('breakwaters in the storm'). | Princely States Policy |

### 2. Colonial Land Settlements & Agrarian Terminology

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Permanent Settlement** | Cornwallis's 1793 settlement in Bengal recognizing Zamindars as permanent owners with permanently fixed state demand. | Land Revenue System |
| **Sunset Law** | Legal clause under Permanent Settlement: If revenue was not deposited by sunset of the specified date, estate was auctioned. | Land Revenue System |
| **Ryotwari System** | Settlement introduced by Thomas Munro (1820) establishing direct land contracts with individual peasant cultivators (*Ryots*). | Land Revenue System |
| **Mahalwari System** | Revenue settlement devised by Holt Mackenzie (1822) made collectively with village bodies (*Mahals*) through headmen. | Land Revenue System |
| **Lambardar / Village Headman**| Hereditary village headman responsible for collecting and remitting land revenue under the Mahalwari system. | Rural Administration |
| **Jotedar** | Wealthy de facto rural landowners in Bengal who leased out lands to sharecroppers and dominated village credit. | Agrarian Hierarchy |
| **Bargadar / Adhiar** | Landless sharecropper cultivating lands owned by Jotedars or Zamindars in exchange for half the produce share. | Agrarian Hierarchy |
| **Tinkathia System** | Exploitative system in Champaran (Bihar) forcing peasants to cultivate European indigo on 3/20th of their holdings. | Indigo Cultivation |
| **Kamia / Kamioti** | Hereditary debt-bondage system in Bihar where agricultural laborers remained bonded for life against meager credit. | Bonded Labor |
| **Hali / Harvahi** | System of hereditary bonded agricultural labor prevalent in Gujarat and western India. | Bonded Labor |
| **Famine Code** | Official administrative guidelines formulated after 1880 (Strachey Commission) to organize famine relief works. | Colonial Administration |

### 3. Colonial Economy, Exploitation & The Drain Theory

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **De-industrialization** | Systematic destruction of India's traditional urban handloom and handicraft industries under British machine imports. | Colonial Economic Impact |
| **One-Way Free Trade** | British policy removing import duties on British manufactured goods entering India while slapping tariffs on Indian goods in Britain. | Colonial Trade Policy |
| **Commercialization of Agriculture**| Forced shift from subsistence food crops to market cash crops (opium, indigo, jute, cotton) to feed British industry. | Colonial Agriculture |
| **Drain of Wealth** | Unilateral transfer of Indian financial surplus and resources to Britain without any equivalent material return. | Nationalist Economics |
| **Home Charges** | Annual payments remitted from Indian revenues to London (pensions, India Office expenses, railway guaranteed 5% interest). | Nationalist Economics |
| **Council Bills** | British financial mechanism used by the Secretary of State to siphon Indian export earnings directly to London banks. | Colonial Finance |
| **Guarantee System** | British state scheme guaranteeing private British railway investors a risk-free 5% return on capital from Indian taxes. | Infrastructure Policy |

### 4. Nationalist Methods, Ideology & Gandhian Concepts

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Moderates (Early INC)** | Early nationalist leaders (1885–1905) relying on constitutional agitation, rule of law, and British liberal justice. | Early Nationalism |
| **The 3 P's** | Moderate political method of constitutional agitation: **Prayers, Petitions, and Protests**. | Moderate Strategy |
| **Extremists (Militant Nationalists)**| Radical faction (Lal-Bal-Pal, Aurobindo) demanding Swaraj as a natural right through mass direct action. | Radical Nationalism |
| **Atmashakti** | Ideology of self-reliance, national self-respect, and indigenous enterprise popularized during the Swadeshi Movement. | Swadeshi Movement |
| **Passive Resistance** | Doctrine formulated by Sri Aurobindo Ghosh advocating non-violent non-cooperation and boycott to paralyze government. | Nationalist Strategy |
| **Swaraj** | Self-rule; originally interpreted as colonial self-government, later redefined at Lahore (1929) as complete independence. | Nationalist Goals |
| **Purna Swaraj** | Complete sovereign independence from the British Empire; adopted as the sole objective at the 1929 Lahore Congress. | Nationalist Goals |
| **Satyagraha** | 'Holding onto Truth'; Gandhi's supreme method of non-violent soul-force and active defiance of unjust laws. | Gandhian Philosophy |
| **Ahimsa** | Non-violence in thought, word, and deed; foundational moral cornerstone of Gandhian political struggle. | Gandhian Philosophy |
| **Hartal** | Nationwide cessation of business, commerce, and work as a collective peaceful protest against colonial injustice. | Mass Agitation |
| **Constructive Programme**| Gandhi's long-term village reconstruction work: Khadi spinning, removal of untouchability, adult literacy, sanitation. | Gandhian Strategy |
| **Trusteeship** | Gandhian socio-economic doctrine asserting that wealthy capitalists should hold wealth in trust for common welfare. | Gandhian Economics |
| **Sarvodaya** | 'Upliftment of all'; Gandhian philosophical vision of an egalitarian, non-exploitative, decentralized cooperative society. | Gandhian Philosophy |
| **Nai Talim (Wardha Scheme)**| Basic education scheme emphasizing learning through productive manual crafts, mother tongue, and civic self-reliance. | Gandhian Education |
| **Harijan** | 'Children of God'; affectionate term used by Mahatma Gandhi for the Depressed Classes / Untouchables. | Social Reform |
| **Pro-Changers (Swarajists)**| Congress leaders (C.R. Das, Motilal) who advocated entering councils to wreck dyarchy from within. | Swaraj Party |
| **No-Changers** | Orthodox Gandhian leaders (Patel, Rajagopalachari, Prasad) who rejected council entry to focus on constructive village work. | Congress Strategy |

### 5. Revolutionary Nationalist Organizations & Actions

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Anushilan Samiti** | Pioneering revolutionary secret society founded in Bengal (1902) dedicated to armed anti-colonial resistance. | Revolutionary Phase I |
| **Yugantar** | Revolutionary newspaper and faction of Anushilan Samiti advocating open armed rebellion and bomb manufacturing. | Revolutionary Phase I |
| **Abhinav Bharat** | Secret society founded in Maharashtra (1904) by Vinayak Damodar Savarkar, inspired by Mazzini's Young Italy. | Revolutionary Phase I |
| **Ghadar Party** | Revolutionary international organization founded in San Francisco (1913) by Lala Har Dayal to trigger armed Indian mutiny. | Revolutionary Phase I |
| **Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)**| Revolutionary body founded at Kanpur (1924) aiming to establish a Federal Republic of India via armed revolution. | Revolutionary Phase II |
| **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)**| Reorganized HRA at Ferozeshah Kotla (1928) under Bhagat Singh and Azad, officially adopting **Socialism** as goal. | Revolutionary Phase II |
| **Kakori Train Action** | Famous HRA operation (9 August 1925) halting 8-Down train at Kakori to seize colonial treasury cash for arms purchases. | Revolutionary Phase II |
| **Central Assembly Bombing**| Protest action (8 April 1929) by Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt throwing smoke bombs 'to make the deaf hear'. | Revolutionary Phase II |
| **Chittagong Armoury Raid**| Armed raid (18 April 1930) on British armouries led by Surya Sen; declared Provisional Revolutionary Government. | Revolutionary Phase II |
| **Azad Dasta** | Underground guerrilla resistance brigade organized in Nepal jungles by Jayaprakash Narayan during Quit India (1942). | Quit India Movement |
| **Congress Secret Radio**| Clandestine transmitter operated by Usha Mehta in Bombay broadcasting uncensored nationalist news during Quit India. | Quit India Movement |
| **Prati Sarkar** | Independent parallel revolutionary governments established in Balia (UP), Tamluk (Bengal), and Satara (Maharashtra). | Quit India Movement |
| **Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind**| Provisional Government of Free India proclaimed in Singapore (21 October 1943) by Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose. | INA Liberation |
| **Rani of Jhansi Regiment**| Historic all-female combat military unit of the Indian National Army commanded by Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan. | INA Liberation |

### 6. Constitutional Enactments, Reforms & Political Formulas

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Dyarchy** | Division of executive administration into **Reserved** (British) and **Transferred** (Indian Ministers) subjects (1919 Act). | Constitutional Reform |
| **Reserved Subjects** | Crucial portfolios (Police, Justice, Land Revenue, Finance) retained under the Governor and his Executive Council. | GOI Act 1919 |
| **Transferred Subjects**| Nation-building portfolios (Education, Health, Local Self-Govt, Agriculture) handed to Indian ministers responsible to council. | GOI Act 1919 |
| **Separate Electorates** | Electoral system where voters of a specific religion elect representatives reserved exclusively for their own community. | Communalism |
| **Joint Electorates** | Electoral system where all voters cast ballots together, with certain seats reserved for marginalized communities. | Democratic Electoral System |
| **Communal Award** | British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald's 1932 decision granting separate electorates to Depressed Classes (Dalits). | British Divide & Rule |
| **Poona Pact** | Agreement (September 1932) between Gandhi and Ambedkar abandoning separate electorates for Dalits in exchange for 148 reserved seats. | Caste & Politics |
| **Provincial Autonomy** | Abolition of dyarchy in provinces and grant of full ministerial governance under the Government of India Act 1935. | GOI Act 1935 |
| **All-India Federation** | Proposed federation in 1935 Act uniting British Indian provinces and acceding princely states (never materialized). | GOI Act 1935 |
| **Residuary Powers** | Legislative subjects not enumerated in Federal, Provincial, or Concurrent lists (vested in Viceroy under 1935 Act). | Constitutional Law |
| **August Offer (1940)** | Linlithgow's declaration offering post-war Dominion status and granting a virtual minority veto to the Muslim League. | Wartime Policy |
| **Individual Satyagraha**| Symbolic non-violent protest (1940) launched by Gandhi asserting freedom of speech against imperial war participation. | Gandhian Satyagraha |
| **Cripps Proposals** | British wartime offer (1942) promising Dominion status and provincial secession right; rejected as 'post-dated cheque'. | Wartime Mission |
| **Rajagopalachari (CR) Formula**| 1944 proposal by C. Rajagopalachari offering cooperation to the Muslim League in return for supporting independence. | Political Negotiation |
| **Desai-Liaquat Pact** | 1945 proposal for equal parity (20% each) between Congress and League in the interim central executive council. | Political Negotiation |
| **Wavell Plan & Simla Conference**| 1945 proposal to reconstruct Executive Council; collapsed due to Jinnah's demand for an exclusive Muslim nominee veto. | Political Deadlock |
| **Cabinet Mission Plan**| 1946 British mission proposing a Three-Tier Federal Union with Grouping (Sections A, B, C); rejected Pakistan. | Transfer of Power |
| **Direct Action Day** | 16 August 1946; League's call to force Pakistan triggering horrific communal riots (*The Great Calcutta Killings*). | Partition Tragedy |
| **Mountbatten Plan (June 3rd Plan)**| British plan accepting partition of India into two dominions (India and Pakistan) and partition of Punjab and Bengal. | Partition Settlement |
| **Radcliffe Line** | Boundary demarcation drawn by Sir Cyril Radcliffe partitioning Punjab and Bengal between India and Pakistan. | Boundary Commission |
| **Instrument of Accession**| Legal document signed by princely state rulers acceding to the Dominion of India on Defence, External Affairs, and Communications. | National Integration |
| **Standstill Agreement**| Agreement between Dominion of India and princely states maintaining administrative status quo pending accession. | National Integration |

### 7. Subaltern, Peasant & Tribal Movements Terminology

| Term | Historical Meaning & UPSC Context | Period / Domain |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Dikus** | Derogatory tribal term for exploitative non-tribal outsiders: British revenue peons, moneylenders, and traders. | Tribal Resistance |
| **Damin-i-Koh** | Designated forested hill-skirt tract in Rajmahal Hills where Santhals were settled before revolting in 1855. | Tribal Geography |
| **Ulgulan** | 'The Great Tumult'; millenarian armed religious-political uprising led by Birsa Munda (1899–1900) in Chhotanagpur. | Tribal Resistance |
| **Khuntkatti System** | Customary Munda tribal landholding system based on collective, joint clan ownership of cleared forest land. | Tribal Land Systems |
| **Bet-Begari** | Unpaid forced agricultural labor extorted by non-tribal landlords from indigenous tribal cultivators. | Feudal Exploitation |
| **Paikas** | Traditional hereditary landed military retainers of the Gajapati Kings of Khurda who revolted in 1817. | Civil Resistance |
| **Kisan Sabha** | Peasant unions organized across UP, Bihar, and Andhra in the 1920s and 1930s (Swami Sahjanand Saraswati). | Peasant Movement |
| **Tebhaga** | Sharecropper uprising in Bengal (1946) demanding two-thirds (2/3rd) share of crop harvest instead of half. | Peasant Movement |
| **Vetti / Vettichakiri**| Forced unpaid labor and illegal exactions imposed by feudal Deshmukhs on peasants in the Nizam's Hyderabad. | Telangana Movement |
