# Architecture of Humayun

> **Source**: [Architecture of Humayun](https://compass.rauias.com/art-culture/architecture-humayun/)

---

- First distinct example of Mughal architecture inspired by **Persian architecture**, is the tomb of Humayun, in Delhi, built by his widow, **Begha Begum**. This tomb has provided the prototype, followed by architects who designed the **Mausoleum of Jahangir at Shahdara, Lahore**, as well as the celebrated Taj Mahal, at Agra.

- The tomb itself is in the centre of a large garden, laid out in char bagh (four-fold) style, with pools joined by channels. The main entrance is on the south side, and there is another entrance on the west side.

![image 324](https://i0.wp.com/compass.rauias.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/image-324.png?resize=433%2C142&ssl=1)

- Red sandstone, the **double-storeyed structureof the mausoleum rises over a high square terrace, raised over a series of cells that are like a musical composition.

- The octagonal form of the central chamber containing the cenotaph is inspired by Syrian and earlier Islamic models. It is for the first time that **pink sandstoneand white are used with admirable effect.
